## SECTION 1: PRODUCT CONTEXT

**Product:** Rap Sheets — complaint cards formatted as office infraction notices. Valentine-style front (the infraction), complaint/rant on back (the sentence).
**Brand:** Witty Yeti (consumer-facing) / Elevate Co. (in-world brand)
**Founder character:** Brad Sterling
**Box format:** Tuck-end box, ~4" x 2.5" x 0.75", 6 printable surfaces + tuck flap
**Kit:** One item in the "Office Survival Kit," a gag gift briefcase sold on Amazon
**Tray:** Tray 2 (alongside Cell Talk, Sweet Nothings, and Confiscated Stapler recess)

**Physical contents:** 15 cards (per v01 — spec says 10-20)
**Design aesthetic:** Matte black, cream/gold text, red rubber stamps, institutional/legal — looks like a case file or evidence folder

**Card format (from sample):** Front = "OFFICE INFRACTION NOTICE" with specific violation and severity stamp. Back = "SENTENCE:" with an escalating rant about the violation, ending with "CASE CLOSED" stamp. This is a strong dual-sided concept.

**Recurring characters:**
- Kevin (4-6 refs across full kit — 0 on this box currently, but Kevin is the PERFECT character for this item — he's the reason these cards exist)
- Gary (QC stamps only, 2-3 refs — 1 on this box: "INSPECTED / QC / PASSED" stamp on front)
- Diane (HR, implied — possibly implied in "HR meeting" in the before/after column)

### FRONT

**[BRAND LINE]** ELEVATE CO. — Interpersonal Accountability Division

**[QC STAMP]** INSPECTED / QC / PASSED *(circular rubber stamp, tilted)*

**[PRODUCT NAME]** RAP SHEETS™

**[SUBTITLE]** Interpersonal Feedback Delivery System

**[DESCRIPTION]** Structured Accountability Cards for the Modern Workplace

**[TAGLINE]** Because "let it go" was not working.

**[SPEC LINE]** Contains 15 Documented Infractions • Unresolved

**[STAMP]** AUTHORIZED FOR DISCIPLINARY USE *(red rubber stamp, angled)*

**[FINE PRINT]** Contents have been tested on coworkers and They know what they did.

**Assessment:** Strong front, strong design. The matte black + cream/gold + red stamps visual identity is completely different from the other boxes, which is correct — this is the "official file" of the kit. It looks like a classified case folder.

"Interpersonal Accountability Division" is another great Brad institutional subdivision name.

"Because 'let it go' was not working" is a good tagline — it's warm, it talks to the buyer, it implies escalation. It has personality. The quotes around "let it go" are a nice touch — they make it sound like advice someone gave you that failed.

"Contains 15 Documented Infractions • Unresolved" is excellent. "Documented Infractions" instead of "cards" is perfect Brad Mode 1 institutional language. "Unresolved" at the end is doing heavy lifting — it implies these complaints are OPEN CASES, not resolved, and the buyer is about to reopen them.

"AUTHORIZED FOR DISCIPLINARY USE" stamp is a great visual element — it looks like a real authorization stamp on an official document.

"Contents have been tested on coworkers and They know what they did." — This is close to WY quality. "They know what they did" is a good landing. The capital "T" on "They" feels intentional — it gives it an ominous, almost biblical weight. The line talks TO the buyer and makes them complicit.

The QC stamp is a Gary appearance — counts toward the 2-3 Gary refs across the kit.

**One note:** "Structured Accountability Cards for the Modern Workplace" is the most generic/corporate line on the front. It reads more like Brad Mode 2 (sincere, corporate) than Mode 1 (institutional with dark content). It's functional — it tells you what the cards are — but it's the weakest line on this surface. Not a problem unless the boss flags it.

### BACK

**[HEADER]** INCIDENT SUMMARY REPORT
**[SUBHEADER]** Interpersonal Accountability Division · Case Review

**[TWO-COLUMN FORMAT]**

| BEFORE RAP SHEETS | AFTER RAP SHEETS |
|---|---|
| Said nothing | Selected card |
| Said something | Delivered card |
| Said too much | Returned to desk |
| Punched face | .... |
| HR meeting | Recipient said nothing |
| Lit a fire | Recipient doesn't sleep |
| Slashed tires | Recipient anger issues |
| Ate lunch supervised | Recipient bar fight |
| Live under bridge | Recipient issued new cell |

**[BRAD PHOTO + TITLE]**
Brad Sterling
Chief Accountability Officer
Interpersonal Accountability Division Elevate Co

**[BRAD QUOTE]** "Documentation isn't passive-aggressive. It's proactive-aggressive. The difference is a filing system."

**Assessment:** This is a COMPLEX back panel with a lot going on. Let me break it down:

**The before/after concept:** The "BEFORE RAP SHEETS" column shows an escalation of what happens when you DON'T have these cards: you say nothing, then something, then too much, then you punch someone, go to HR, set fires, slash tires, get supervised, end up homeless. The "AFTER RAP SHEETS" column shows what happens when you DO: select card, deliver card, go back to desk... but then the RECIPIENT spirals — doesn't sleep, develops anger issues, gets in bar fights, gets "issued new cell."

**The joke:** The product doesn't SOLVE the problem — it just MOVES it to the other person. You're fine. They're destroyed. This is a dark, clever structural joke. It's similar to the Shart Kit's energy — "we solved YOUR problem, we didn't say anything about anyone else's."

**Concerns with this surface:**
1. The "BEFORE" column escalates into actual violence and homelessness ("Punched face," "Lit a fire," "Slashed tires," "Live under bridge"). This is very dark, which the boss wants, but the escalation from "said nothing" to "live under bridge" in 9 steps might feel rushed and the tonal jumps are big.
2. The "AFTER" column's ellipsis ("....") is doing structural work — it implies time passing and consequences emerging — but it's a subtle formatting choice on a small surface.
3. "Recipient issued new cell" is doing prison-world double duty (new cubicle / new prison cell) — good.
4. "Ate lunch supervised" and "Live under bridge" are the most ambitious jumps and could be the funniest OR could feel disconnected from the office-prison world (living under a bridge isn't office or prison territory).

**Brad's quote:** "Documentation isn't passive-aggressive. It's proactive-aggressive. The difference is a filing system." This is a solid Brad quote. "Proactive-aggressive" is a made-up corporate buzzword that Brad would absolutely use with complete sincerity. "The difference is a filing system" grounds it in Brad's institutional worldview — he thinks the SYSTEM is what separates aggression from professionalism.

**Overall:** This is the most ambitious back panel across all item boxes. The before/after format is a bigger visual and comedic risk than the other boxes' back panels. When it lands, it could be the funniest. Some of the escalation steps might need tightening.

### LONG SIDE A

**[TEXT]** USE BEFORE COOLING OFF

**[STYLE]** Stencil text, red on black, military surplus aesthetic

**Assessment:** Excellent. Three words. Subverts the standard safety instruction "use after cooling off" / "allow to cool before handling." The implication: these cards should be deployed while you're still angry. Don't wait. Don't calm down. Strike while the rage is fresh. This is the brand being conspiratorial and slightly irresponsible — it's encouraging you to act on impulse, which is the opposite of what HR would say.

The stencil/military aesthetic matches the case-file visual identity of this box. No changes needed.

---

### LONG SIDE B (hazard symbols)

**[VISUALS]** Four GHS hazard diamond symbols: skull & crossbones, flame, exclamation mark, corrosive/environmental

**[TEXT]** CAUTION CONTENTS MAY ESCALATE

**Assessment:** Strong. The GHS hazard symbols are universally recognized chemical safety labels — putting them on a card deck box is a perfect format/content contrast (Brad Mode 1). The symbols imply this product is DANGEROUS — toxic, flammable, corrosive, hazardous.

"CAUTION CONTENTS MAY ESCALATE" is a great line. It reads like "CAUTION CONTENTS MAY SHIFT DURING TRANSPORT" (a real shipping label) but "ESCALATE" is the punchline. The cards themselves may make things worse. The product is warning you about itself. This is WY-quality — the product knows what it is and is cheerfully warning you.

No changes needed.

---

### SHORT SIDE A

**[VISUAL]** Gavel icon

**[TEXT]** NO APPEALS

**Assessment:** Perfect. Two words, one icon. Legal format (gavel = courtroom). "No appeals" means the sentence is final — there's no recourse. In the context of office infraction cards, it means: once you hand someone this card, it's done. No discussion. No counterargument. The verdict stands.

This works on the prison-world level (no appeals = your sentence is final) and the office level (no appeals = don't come to me to argue about it). No changes needed.

---

### SHORT SIDE B

**[VISUAL]** Anger meter / gauge — gradient from green (LOW) through yellow (MODERATE) to red (HOSTILE), with the needle pegged at HOSTILE

**Assessment:** Strong visual gag. The anger meter is a universally readable gauge format. The needle being maxed at HOSTILE implies you should only open this box when you're already at peak rage — which ties to "USE BEFORE COOLING OFF" on Long Side A.

"LOW — MODERATE — HOSTILE" is a good three-tier escalation. "HOSTILE" instead of "HIGH" or "EXTREME" is the right word choice — it's a workplace HR word (hostile work environment) being used as a rage meter reading. No changes needed.

### TUCK FLAP (visible when box is opened)

**[TEXT]** YOU KNOW WHO THIS IS FOR.

**Assessment:** Excellent. Six words. The best tuck flap for THIS specific product. When you open a box of complaint cards, the first thing you see is "You know who this is for." It's the brand being conspiratorial — it assumes you already have someone in mind. You didn't buy this randomly. You opened this box with PURPOSE. You have a TARGET.

This is warm, it talks TO the buyer, it makes them complicit, and it lands on "for" which creates the implication without naming anyone. It's the kind of line that makes someone laugh and then immediately think of a specific coworker.

Comparable to Cell Talk's "You opened this for a reason" but more pointed and more specific to the product's premise. No changes needed.

### SAMPLE CARD (FRONT)

**[FORMAT]** OFFICE INFRACTION NOTICE

**[VIOLATION]** Leaving 0:03 on the microwave instead of clearing it.

**[SEVERITY]** UNFORGIVABLE *(red stamp)*

**Assessment:** The card front format is excellent. "OFFICE INFRACTION NOTICE" looks like a real write-up form. The specific violation (leaving 0:03 on the microwave) is a PERFECT office grievance — it's universal, petty, specific, and instantly relatable. Everyone has seen those three seconds left on the microwave. Everyone has been annoyed by it. Nobody has ever formally addressed it — until now.

"SEVERITY: UNFORGIVABLE" is the escalation that makes it funny — a real infraction form would say "Minor" or "Moderate." Stamping a microwave timer violation as "UNFORGIVABLE" is the gap between format (official) and content (petty) that makes Brad Mode 1 work.

This card format is strong. If every card follows this template (specific relatable violation + absurd severity rating), the deck will work.

### SAMPLE CARD (BACK)

**[TEXT]**
SENTENCE:

"Three seconds. You couldn't commit to three more seconds. You walked away like a man who has never finished anything. I see you. I see all threee of your seconds."

CASE CLOSED *(red stamp)*

**Assessment:** The "SENTENCE" format is strong — it continues the legal/judicial framing from the front. The rant itself is good: it starts with the specific fact ("Three seconds"), escalates the accusation ("You couldn't commit to three more seconds"), makes a character judgment ("You walked away like a man who has never finished anything"), and lands on an absurdly intense closer ("I see you. I see all three of your seconds").

The energy is right — it reads like someone who has been holding this in for MONTHS and is finally letting it out through the official channel of a complaint card. The pettiness-to-intensity ratio is well calibrated.

**Typo:** "threee" should be "three" (extra 'e'). This needs to be fixed.

**"CASE CLOSED" stamp** is the right ending — it signals finality. Once this card is delivered, the matter is closed. No discussion.

## SECTION 4: OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**This is a strong box with the most distinctive visual identity in the kit.** The matte black + cream/gold + red stamps aesthetic looks like a classified case file, which perfectly matches the "documented infractions" premise. It looks nothing like the other item boxes, which is correct — each card deck has its own visual lane (Cell Talk = wellness, Rap Sheets = legal file, Sweet Nothings = TBD).

**Strengths:**
- The front is loaded with working copy — tagline, spec line, fine print, stamps all land
- "USE BEFORE COOLING OFF" is one of the best short lines in the kit
- "CAUTION CONTENTS MAY ESCALATE" is WY-quality
- The tuck flap ("YOU KNOW WHO THIS IS FOR") is perfectly targeted to the product
- The card format (Infraction Notice front / Sentence back) is the strongest card format concept — it has a clear structure, escalation, and visual stamps
- The GHS hazard symbols are a killer visual gag
- The anger meter is shareable
- "NO APPEALS" is a perfect two-word side panel
- The QC stamp counts as a Gary ref

**Potential concerns (minor):**
1. **"Structured Accountability Cards for the Modern Workplace"** is the weakest line on the front — it's generic corporate. Not bad, but not contributing comedy.
2. **The before/after back panel** is ambitious. "Ate lunch supervised" and "Live under bridge" in the BEFORE column jump outside the office-prison world slightly. "Punched face" and "Slashed tires" are very direct violence that might be more explicit than the rest of the kit's tone (which implies violence without depicting it). The AFTER column's "Recipient bar fight" and "Recipient issued new cell" are strong though.
3. **Typo on sample card back:** "threee" needs to be fixed to "three."

**Compared to WY reference products:**
- "Because 'let it go' was not working" has the same warm conspiratorial energy as Shart Kit's "Give the gift of preparedness"
- "CAUTION CONTENTS MAY ESCALATE" could sit on Child Chucker packaging
- The product warning about its own consequences ("Contents may escalate," anger meter at HOSTILE, "Use before cooling off") has the same self-aware-product energy as Dehydrated Water's "We didn't put any in"

**Recommendation:** Send to boss as-is with the typo fix. If the back panel's before/after escalation gets flagged, have tighter alternatives ready.

## SECTION 7: KEY LEARNINGS

1. **Rap Sheets has the most distinctive visual identity.** Matte black + cream/gold + red stamps looks like a classified case file. This makes it feel like a "premium" item in the kit — it has weight and seriousness that the bright yellow Turf Tags and soft green Cell Talk don't.

2. **The card format is the best-structured of the three decks.** OFFICE INFRACTION NOTICE (front) → SENTENCE (back) gives every card a clear narrative arc: crime → punishment. The stamps (UNFORGIVABLE, CASE CLOSED) add visual punctuation.

3. **"USE BEFORE COOLING OFF" is one of the best short lines in the kit.** It subverts safety instructions and gives the product its own personality — this is a product that wants to be used in anger.

4. **The before/after back panel is the biggest creative risk.** If it lands, it's the funniest back panel. If the escalation steps feel disconnected or too extreme, it could be the weakest. The concept is strong — the execution may need tightening in the most violent/extreme steps.

5. **This is the only item box with a Gary stamp on the front.** The QC stamp is well-placed and counts toward the 2-3 Gary refs across the kit.

6. **The sample card's microwave violation is a perfect choice.** Universally relatable, specifically petty, immediately funny. The card content session should generate 14 more infractions at this level of specificity.

7. **Fix the "threee" typo on the sample card back before anything else.**
