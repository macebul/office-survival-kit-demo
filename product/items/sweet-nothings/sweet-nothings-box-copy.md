# Sweet Nothings — Complete Box Copy

**Last Updated:** June 4, 2026
**Status:** Draft v1 — Full copy for all 6 surfaces + tuck flap
**Purpose:** Every text element on the Sweet Nothings item box, organized by surface.
**Voice:** Brad Sterling / Elevate Co. — Mode 1 (institutional, luxury stationery). Played 100% straight.
**Brand:** Elevate Co. — Ambient Relations Bureau
**Product:** Sweet Nothings™ — Ambient Communication Cards
**Division:** Ambient Relations Bureau
**SKU:** EC-ARB-001

> **The Rule:** The format is the straight man. The content is the comedian. This box looks like a legitimate luxury stationery product from an upscale card brand. It happens to contain weaponized insults. The more beautiful and refined the packaging, the funnier the content reveal becomes.

> **Naming Hierarchy (LOCKED):** "SWEET NOTHINGS™" dominant. "Ambient Communication Cards" subordinate. Matches Lifer Ink / Turf Tags pattern.

## SURFACE 1 — FRONT FACE (4.0" × 2.5")

*The face visible when the box sits in Tray 2. First thing the recipient reads. This surface establishes the elegant stationery world in one glance — luxury card product with ornamental flourishes and a wax seal. The prettiest object in the entire kit.*

---

### SN-1: Brand Header
**Treatment:** Small, spaced uppercase serif or elegant sans-serif at top center. Refined. Understated luxury.

**Placement:** Top center, above everything

> **ELEVATE CO.**
> Ambient Relations Bureau

**Design note:** "Ambient Relations Bureau" is Brad's diplomatic division. "Ambient" does heavy lifting — it means "relating to the surrounding area," which Brad interprets as "the general vibe of interpersonal dynamics." "Relations Bureau" sounds like a government agency responsible for diplomacy. Brad created a bureau for the act of passively insulting coworkers through beautifully designed cards. He considers this his most sophisticated product line.

---

### SN-2: Ornamental Frame & Flourishes
**Treatment:** Rose gold ornamental corner flourishes and thin decorative border framing the entire front face. Wedding invitation aesthetic — the kind of ornamental detail you'd see on formal correspondence, luxury stationery, or a ceremony program.

**Placement:** Full border treatment with corner ornaments, framing all other elements

**Design note:** The flourishes are doing structural work — they signal "expensive" and "formal" before the recipient reads a single word. The visual language of luxury stationery is the disguise. Every ornamental curl is another layer of plausible deniability.

---

### SN-3: Product Name (Hero Copy Element)
**Treatment:** Large, dominant, elegant serif script. Beautiful. The kind of typeface that makes you think this costs $28 at Anthropologie. This is the first text the recipient reads.

**Placement:** Centered, dominant position on the face — the largest text element

> **SWEET NOTHINGS™**

**Design note:** "Sweet Nothings" works on two levels: (1) the whispered affectionate phrases between lovers (which these decidedly are not), and (2) empty, meaningless words — which is exactly what the card fronts are (nice but hollow) before the backs reveal the real message. Brad chose the name because he thought it sounded elegant. He doesn't realize it literally means "empty words." He thinks it means "thoughtful gifts."

---

### SN-4: Corporate Rebrand Subtitle
**Treatment:** Smaller, lighter weight serif. Subordinate to "SWEET NOTHINGS™" but clearly present. This is Brad's formal product classification.

**Placement:** Directly below "SWEET NOTHINGS™", centered

> Ambient Communication Cards

**Design note:** "Ambient Communication" is Brad's euphemism for passive-aggressive messaging. "Ambient" implies it happens in the background, naturally, without anyone having to acknowledge it directly. "Communication" is generous — these cards communicate, but in the same way a Post-it on the microwave communicates. Brad named these like an art installation: "Ambient Communication, 2024. Mixed media. Cards on desk."

---

### SN-5: Tagline
**Treatment:** Italic serif, set apart by thin ornamental dividers above and below. Reads as a brand promise — elegant, sincere, hiding something.

**Placement:** Below subtitle, centered, with breathing room

> *Say what you mean. Beautifully.*

**Design note:** This tagline is doing the entire job of the box in five words. "Say what you mean" — direct communication. "Beautifully" — but make it elegant. The period after "Beautifully" is the tell — it lands with finality, like a door closing gently but firmly. The tagline promises beauty and honesty simultaneously, which is what these cards deliver. Just not in the way you expect.

---

### SN-6: Card Count & Classification
**Treatment:** Small, clean, factual. Elegant but informational.

**Placement:** Below tagline, centered

> Contains 15 Sentiments · Artisan Grade

**Design note:** "Sentiments" instead of "cards." "Artisan Grade" is Brad's quality designation — not "premium," not "luxury," but "artisan," which implies handcrafted care. Brad considers weaponized insults an artisan product. He's not wrong — the insults ARE crafted. The ™ is implied. Brad would trademark "Artisan Grade" but his legal advisor said he couldn't.

---

### SN-7: Wax Seal
**Treatment:** Circular wax seal in rose gold — the kind you'd see on formal correspondence, a certificate, or a luxury candle box. Ornamental, tactile, official.

**Placement:** Bottom center area, above fine print. Prominent — this is the box's signature comedy moment.

> HANDCRAFTED WITH
> PLAUSIBLE DENIABILITY™

**Design note:** The wax seal is the turn. Everything above it reads as genuinely elegant. The seal reads as elegant for exactly one second — "HANDCRAFTED WITH" is the setup (you expect "love" or "care") — then "PLAUSIBLE DENIABILITY" lands. It reframes everything on the box. These aren't affectionate cards. They're weapons with an alibi. The ™ is essential — Brad trademarked the phrase "Plausible Deniability." He thinks this is a brand asset.

---

### SN-8: Fine Print & SKU
**Treatment:** Very small text at the bottom of the surface. Real packaging fine print, but in elegant micro-serif.

**Placement:** Bottom edge, full width

> Sentiments expressed on the front of each card are legally considered the message. Anything on the back is decorative.
>
> EC-ARB-001 | Elevate Co. Ambient Relations Bureau

**Design note:** The disclaimer establishes that the FRONT of the card (the nice message) is the official communication. The BACK (the devastating insult) is "decorative." This is legal cover, Brad-style — he's preemptively argued that the insults don't count because they're on the back. It's plausible deniability encoded into the fine print. The SKU: EC = Elevate Co., ARB = Ambient Relations Bureau, 001 = first product.

## SURFACE 2 — BACK FACE (4.0" × 2.5")

*The "read more" surface. Visible when the recipient flips the box. This is where Brad appears as the diplomatic authority. The format is a satisfaction survey — the kind of feedback form a luxury brand includes to show they care about your experience. Pre-filled, of course.*

---

### SN-9: Report Header
**Treatment:** Elegant section header with thin ornamental divider. Formal correspondence energy. Rose gold accents on cream.

**Placement:** Top of surface, spanning width

> **SENTIMENT EFFICACY SURVEY**
> Ambient Relations Bureau · Quality Assurance

**Design note:** "Sentiment Efficacy Survey" is a customer satisfaction form, Brad-style. "Efficacy" implies these cards are meant to produce a measurable effect (they are). "Quality Assurance" implies an ongoing evaluation program (there isn't one). The entire back face is formatted as a pre-completed satisfaction survey — Brad filled it out himself and reported the results.

---

### SN-10: Survey Results Block
**Treatment:** A structured survey form — fields with questions and pre-filled answers, like a customer feedback card from a luxury hotel or fine dining experience. Elegant, clean, serif type.

**Placement:** Upper half of surface, below header

> **Cards Deployed:** 15
> **Delivery Method:** Left on desk (71%), Handed with eye contact (18%), Taped to monitor (11%)
> **Recipient Reactions:**
> &nbsp;&nbsp; Genuine smile ................. 100%
> &nbsp;&nbsp; Turned card over .............. 100%
> &nbsp;&nbsp; Second genuine smile ............ 0%
> **Overall Satisfaction:** Beautiful
> **Would Deploy Again:** Involuntarily

**Design note:** The survey tells the story of what happens when someone receives a Sweet Nothing. They smile at the front (100%). They turn it over (100%). They do NOT smile again (0%). "Overall Satisfaction: Beautiful" is not a rating — it's a word Brad chose because he thinks it's a valid survey response. "Would Deploy Again: Involuntarily" means the recipient didn't choose to use the cards — they were compelled. This mirrors Cell Talk's "Compliance Rate: Involuntary" — Brad's programs are never optional.

---

### SN-11: Brad Sterling — Diplomatic Authority
**Treatment:** Small circular photo of Brad (professional headshot, open-collar shirt, warm smile, slightly tilted head — "I care about your experience" energy), with his title and a quote. Framed as the bureau director.

**Placement:** Center-lower portion of the surface

> [Brad headshot — warm, refined, diplomatic variant]
>
> **Brad Sterling**
> Director of Ambient Relations
> Elevate Co.
>
> *"Honesty without elegance is just aggression. Elegance without honesty is just stationery. This is both. You're welcome."*

**Design note:** Brad's title changes per division. On Cell Talk he's "Chief Normative Optimization Officer" (zen). On Rap Sheets he's "Chief Accountability Officer" (enforcement). Here he's "Director of Ambient Relations" — a title that sounds like he runs a diplomatic corps. His quote is his most articulate moment in the kit: he draws a real distinction between honest-but-ugly and beautiful-but-empty, then claims his cards are the synthesis. He's right. He just doesn't realize the "honesty" on the backs is insults about shopping cart wheels. "You're welcome" is consistent with Cell Talk — Brad always thinks you should thank him.

---

### SN-12: Disclaimer
**Treatment:** Very small text at the bottom. Real legal disclaimer format, but in elegant serif.

**Placement:** Bottom edge of surface

> Survey administered by Elevate Co. to a focus group of one (1). Focus group was Brad. Brad rated every sentiment "Beautiful." Brad does not accept feedback on his feedback. For inquiries, write a letter. Letters will be read. Replies will be ambient.

**Design note:** The disclaimer reveals that Brad surveyed himself again (consistent pattern across all three card deck boxes). "Brad rated every sentiment 'Beautiful'" — he used his own satisfaction rating as the survey scale. "Letters will be read. Replies will be ambient." is the best line — it means Brad will respond in the same passive-aggressive spirit as the cards themselves. You'll get an answer. It just won't be direct.

## SURFACE 3 — TOP LONG EDGE (4.0" × 0.75")

*Narrow strip visible when box is sitting in the tray. One strong beat.*

---

### SN-13: Elegance Certification
**Treatment:** Centered text, elegant serif. Reads like a luxury brand tagline or a certification of quality. Rose gold or blush pink text on cream.

**Placement:** Centered on the narrow strip

> SINCERITY ON THE FRONT · HONESTY ON THE BACK

**Design note:** This is the card mechanic stated plainly but elegantly. "Sincerity on the front" (the nice message) and "Honesty on the back" (the insult). The implication is that sincerity and honesty are two different things — which they are on these cards. The front is sincere but dishonest. The back is honest but devastating. Brad thinks this is a profound observation about communication. It's actually just a usage instruction.

## SURFACE 4 — BOTTOM LONG EDGE (4.0" × 0.75")

*Narrow strip on the underside. Reward for the close reader.*

---

### SN-14: Ingredients Statement
**Treatment:** Small centered text, clean elegant serif. Reads like an ingredients list or contents declaration.

**Placement:** Centered on the narrow strip

> CONTENTS: 15 COMPLIMENTS (FRONT-FACING). 15 TRUTHS (REAR-FACING). 0 APOLOGIES.

**Design note:** Formatted like a nutritional contents label or ingredient list. The three items enumerate what's in the box with perfect accuracy: compliments (on the front), truths (on the back), and zero apologies. The parenthetical "(FRONT-FACING)" and "(REAR-FACING)" are directional labels — like you'd see on a shipping box. "0 APOLOGIES" is the kicker: this product ships without remorse. Brad considers this a feature, not a limitation.

## SURFACE 5 — SHORT SIDE A (2.5" × 0.75")

*One of the two narrow ends. Visible when box sits sideways in the tray.*

---

### SN-15: Workplace Honesty Statement
**Treatment:** Small text, elegant copy block format. Warm, considered, completely straight-faced.

**Placement:** Full width, centered vertically

> Every workplace deserves a little
> more honesty. These cards provide it.
> Gently.

**Design note:** This copy is partially visible in the hero image and it's the right tone. It reads as a genuine mission statement for a stationery brand — the kind of earnest copy you'd find on the side of a Papyrus box. "These cards provide it" — honesty, delivered. "Gently." — the standalone word after the period is the turn. It's the most understated warning on the box. The cards provide honesty, yes. But "gently" is doing a LOT of work. The insults are not gentle. Brad thinks they are because they're beautifully designed.

## SURFACE 6 — SHORT SIDE B (2.5" × 0.75")

*The other narrow end.*

---

### SN-16: Deployment Protocol Icon Strip
**Treatment:** 5 simple line-art icons in a horizontal row, each with a tiny label beneath. Same format as Cell Talk (WAKE > DRAW > READ > BREATHE > REPEAT) and Rap Sheets (IDENTIFY > DOCUMENT > PRESENT > EXIT > DENY). Elegant, ornamental line-art style in rose gold — same concept as other boxes but rendered to match the stationery aesthetic.

**Placement:** Full width, icons evenly spaced

> **DEPLOYMENT:**
>
> ✉ → 🪶 → 🎁 → 🚶 → 😇
> SELECT · INSCRIBE · PLACE · RETREAT · SMILE

**Design note:** Replace emoji with clean ornamental line-art icons in production: an envelope (select the right card), a quill/pen (optional — inscribe a personal note), a gift/hand placing card (deploy it on a desk or hand it over), a figure departing gracefully (retreat from the scene), a serene face (smile — maintain plausible deniability). The sequence teaches the recipient the deployment method: pick a card, place it, leave, and smile. "RETREAT" is the key word — it's more elegant than "EXIT" (Rap Sheets) but means the same thing: get out of there. "SMILE" is the plausible deniability step — the nice front IS the alibi.

## SURFACE 7 — TUCK FLAP (interior, visible when box is opened)

*The first text the recipient sees when they open the box. One line. The threshold moment.*

---

### SN-17: Opening Sentiment
**Treatment:** Elegant italic serif, centered, rose gold or blush pink text on cream. Beautiful. Intimate.

**Placement:** Centered on tuck flap interior

> *You deserve this.*

**Design note:** Three words. After an entire box of elegant language, satisfaction surveys, deployment protocols, and Brad's diplomatic philosophy, the tuck flap says: "You deserve this." It works on two levels: (1) a sincere, warm sentiment — you deserve something beautiful, and (2) a warning — you deserve what's coming. It mirrors the card mechanic perfectly: the FRONT reading is nice (you deserve these lovely cards), the BACK reading is ominous (you deserve to be compared to a shopping cart wheel). "You deserve this" is the most honest thing on the box, and it means completely different things depending on which side of the card you're looking at.
