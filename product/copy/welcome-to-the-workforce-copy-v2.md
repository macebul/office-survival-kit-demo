**STANDARD CREDENTIAL PHOTO PROTOCOL**
*As recommended by ELEVATE's Workplace Standards Division*

- [ ] Front-facing photograph — expression neutral but employable
- [ ] Side profile photograph — for interdepartmental recognition and cross-referencing
- [ ] Name and employee ID displayed on placard at chest height — for archival clarity
- [ ] Height markers visible in background — considered essential
- [ ] Personal belongings inventoried and stored — a presentation standard
- [ ] Biometric fingerprint scan — for "general purposes"

*Facilities that follow this protocol report a 97% credential satisfaction rate. Facilities that don't follow this protocol were not included in the study.*

### Callout Box — "BY THE NUMBERS"

### Collectors Feature Inset — "THE COLLECTORS"

What began as a break room curiosity is now one of corporate America's fastest-growing underground hobbies. Badge card collecting — the trading, grading, and cataloguing of current and former employee credentials — spans an estimated 340 facilities nationwide and shows no signs of slowing down.

Cards are graded on five criteria: Expression, Lighting, Posture, Background Uniformity, and "Energy." A perfect 5.0 has never been recorded. Most fall between 2.1 and 2.6.

Vintage badges command the highest value. Pre-merger credentials featuring discontinued logos are considered the hobby's holy grails. A 2003 badge recently changed hands after one collector offered his own credential in return. He told colleagues he needed the photo "for personal errands" — specifically, a stop at the bank, a visit to the jewelry store, and what he described as "an extended trip." He wasn't sure when he'd be back. Both parties described the terms as "fair."

"I have every badge from every person who's sat in my row since 2009," says one anonymous collector. "Some of them don't work here anymore. Some of them don't work anywhere anymore. I think about them every time I open the binder."

### Protocol Checklist — Two Options

**Concept 1: Carbon Copy Triplicate Form**
Government-style multi-part form. Three stacked paper layers (white, yellow, pink). Monospace type, hollow checkboxes, binder clip, "FOR OFFICE USE ONLY" section, form number, tear-here perforation line. Background generated as image, text placed as native InDesign type on top.

**Concept 3: Laminated Wall Poster**
OSHA/break room instructional poster. Clean, friendly infographic style with pastel cards, simple line-art icons, punch holes, lamination glare. Could be built entirely in InDesign or with generated background. Positive, helpful energy.
