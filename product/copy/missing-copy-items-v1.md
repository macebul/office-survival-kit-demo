### ON-MUG TEXT — Options for Mug Exterior

The mug exterior uses a booking-photo / mugshot aesthetic: height measurement lines running vertically, a horizontal frame line at chest level, and institutional text elements. The design should look like the front of a police booking photo — the kind of thing you hold up when they process you on your first day.

**Option A — Booking Number + Property Label:**

Front side:
> [Height measurement lines running vertically along left edge]
>
> **INMATE NO. 9-5**
>
> PROPERTY OF: [YOUR DEPT. HERE]
>
> PROCESSED: MONDAY
>
> STATUS: SERVING TIME

Back side:
> OFFICE OF INTAKE PROCESSING
> "DRINK RESPONSIBLY. SURVIVE ACCORDINGLY."

**Design note:** "INMATE NO. 9-5" is a callback to the evidence tag on the briefcase exterior (CASE NO.: 9-5). "9-5" works on both levels — it's a booking number and the hours of your sentence. "PROPERTY OF: [YOUR DEPT. HERE]" leaves a blank for the recipient to mentally fill in. "PROCESSED: MONDAY" implies everyone's intake happens on the worst day. The back side gives the issuing authority and a sign-off that reads like a prison mess hall poster.

---

**Option B — Full Booking Slate:**

Front side:
> [Height measurement lines]
>
> **OFFICE OF INTAKE PROCESSING**
>
> BOOKING NO. 08-00-AM
>
> NAME: ___________________
>
> DEPT: ___________________
>
> CHARGE: EMPLOYMENT
>
> PLEA: NO CONTEST

Back side:
> "YOU HAVE THE RIGHT TO REMAIN CAFFEINATED."

**Design note:** "BOOKING NO. 08-00-AM" is the time your sentence starts every day. The blank lines for NAME and DEPT make it personal — the recipient IS the inmate. "CHARGE: EMPLOYMENT" is the crime. "PLEA: NO CONTEST" implies they didn't fight it; they just showed up. The back-side quote is a Miranda rights riff that every coffee drinker will appreciate.

---

**Option C — Minimal / Stencil:**

Front side:
> [Height measurement lines]
>
> **MUG SHOT**
>
> NO. 9-5
>
> [Horizontal booking frame line]

Back side:
> EVIDENCE: DO NOT REMOVE FROM PREMISES
> ISSUED BY: ELEVATE CO.
> "THIS IS YOUR MUG NOW."

**Design note:** The most stripped-down option. "MUG SHOT" as the only hero text, with the number and frame doing all the visual work. The back side plays it as evidence — the mug is contraband, it's been logged, and "THIS IS YOUR MUG NOW" reads as both a statement of ownership and a threat. Minimal text, maximum design impact. The double meaning of "mug" does all the heavy lifting.

## 5. COMPARTMENT COVER COPY — Both Sides

**What it is:** Flat rigid paperboard piece (~0.0625" thick) sitting between Tray 2 and the hidden mug compartment. Both sides printable. The gate between acts two and three.

---

### TOP SIDE (visible when you lift Tray 2)

This is what the recipient sees after removing Tray 2 and the second document set. It should feel like a classified document cover — a restricted access barrier that makes you want to lift it. The format is institutional; the content builds anticipation.

> ---
>
> **RESTRICTED ACCESS**
>
> ---
>
> CLEARANCE LEVEL: BEYOND YOUR PAY GRADE
>
> AUTHORIZATION: PENDING (SEE BELOW)
>
> CONTENTS: [CLASSIFIED]
>
> ---
>
> The contents of this compartment have been withheld from the
> standard kit inventory for reasons that are none of your business.
>
> If you have reached this point, you have demonstrated sufficient
> commitment to the unboxing process. Your persistence has been noted.
>
> **TO PROCEED: LIFT THIS PANEL.**
>
> What you find beneath is yours to keep.
> What you do with it is between you and Monday.
>
> ---
>
> AUTHORIZED BY: B. Sterling, Chief Reveal Architect
> DOCUMENT ID: RC-001 | ELEVATE CO. | RESTRICTED ACCESS DIVISION
>
> ---

**Design note:** The format is a classified document cover sheet — the kind you'd see on a manila folder in a restricted filing cabinet. "CLEARANCE LEVEL: BEYOND YOUR PAY GRADE" is both an institutional classification and a workplace joke. "AUTHORIZATION: PENDING (SEE BELOW)" implies you need clearance, then tells you to look further, creating a read-down pull. The tone shifts from institutional restriction to direct address ("your persistence has been noted") to a command ("LIFT THIS PANEL"). The final line — "What you do with it is between you and Monday" — is warm under the institutional language. Brad's title as "Chief Reveal Architect" is consistent with the mug insert card. The document ID maintains bureaucratic detail.

---

### BOTTOM SIDE (visible after you flip the cover)

This is what the recipient sees the instant they lift the compartment cover and flip it over. Below them: the mug in shredded classified paper. In their hand: this message. The tone shifts from restriction to congratulation — you've made it through the system. This is the release.

> ---
>
> **FILE STATUS: COMPLETE**
>
> ---
>
> You opened the trays. You found the empty recess.
> You didn't stop. You kept going.
>
> That is not standard behavior.
> That is not what the intake form predicted.
>
> **RELEASE AUTHORIZED.**
>
> The item below has been held in secure storage
> since the beginning of your sentence.
> It was always here. Waiting for you to find it.
>
> Take it. You've done your time.
>
> ---
>
> PROCESSED BY: ELEVATE CO. | RESTRICTED ACCESS DIVISION
> STATUS: RELEASED TO CUSTODY OF RECIPIENT
> CASE CLOSED.
>
> ---

**Design note:** The bottom side is the emotional pivot. "You didn't stop. You kept going." rewards the recipient's curiosity directly. "That is not standard behavior" frames them as exceptional within the institutional system. "RELEASE AUTHORIZED" is the payoff to the top side's "RESTRICTED ACCESS" — the barrier has been lifted. "It was always here. Waiting for you to find it." is the warmest line in the entire kit, buried inside institutional formatting. "Take it. You've done your time." is the subheader from the Mug Shot item description, now delivered in context as a direct instruction. "CASE CLOSED" is the final stamp. The format never breaks — it's all institutional processing language — but the content underneath it is genuinely warm. This is the peak-end psychology moment: the last strong emotional beat before the mug itself.

## 6. CONFISCATED STAPLER — Complete Label Copy

**Prison name:** Confiscated Stapler
**Brad's rebrand:** The Minimalist Desk Sculpture™
**Real item:** Empty die-cut recess with chalk outline
**Zone:** Tray 2

The last recess in Tray 2 is empty. A white chalk outline of a stapler is printed at the bottom. The label sits adjacent to the recess, formatted as a confiscation notice / evidence tag.

---

> ---
>
> **CONFISCATION NOTICE**
>
> ---
>
> ITEM: Standard-Issue Desktop Stapler (Red)
> INCIDENT: #147
> DATE OF CONFISCATION: 10/14/2024
> LOCATION: Conference Room 3B
>
> REASON FOR REMOVAL:
> Reclassified as weaponized office equipment following
> the events of October 14th. Details remain under review.
> Gary has been asked not to discuss it.
>
> AUTHORIZING OFFICER: B. Sterling, CVO
> DISPOSITION: DO NOT REISSUE.
>
> ---

**Design note:** The format is a real confiscation/evidence notice. "Standard-Issue Desktop Stapler (Red)" carries the Office Space callback without naming it. The date and location are specific — this happened in Conference Room 3B, the same room from the exterior "Made In" label, giving the kit a shared geography. "The events of October 14th" is vague on purpose — nobody explains what happened, which is funnier than any specific incident could be. "Gary has been asked not to discuss it" is Gary's second appearance in the kit (first was the "Inspected By" sticker on the exterior), maintaining his role as the haunted witness who has seen things. "DO NOT REISSUE" closes it with finality. The label is 7 lines — dense enough to reward reading, short enough not to overshadow the visual gag of the empty chalk outline, which does the heavy lifting.

## 7. HIDDEN EASTER EGG COPY

Short copy for hidden discovery moments throughout the kit. These are found only by the curious — people who lift trays, flip things over, and look where they weren't expected to look. Zero additional COGS since these surfaces are already being printed.

---

### a. Tray 1 Underside (seen when you lift Tray 1 out)

This is the first hidden message in the kit. The recipient has just lifted the top tray to see Tray 2. If they're the type to flip it over and look at the bottom — they find this.

> You looked. Good instinct. Keep going.

**Design note:** Short, warm, rewarding. Confirms the behavior (looking where you weren't supposed to) and encourages more of it. "Keep going" is a gentle push toward the deeper layers. Small centered text, institutional serif. This should feel like finding a note someone left for you.

---

### b. Tray 2 Underside (seen when you lift Tray 2 out)

The second hidden message. The recipient has now lifted both trays and is about to discover the compartment cover. If they flip Tray 2 over, they find this — an escalation from Tray 1's message.

> You're still looking. We knew you would.
> The exit is not where they told you it was.

**Design note:** Escalation from the Tray 1 easter egg. "We knew you would" implies the recipient was expected — profiled, predicted, accounted for. "The exit is not where they told you it was" has double meaning: (1) keep looking, the kit isn't done, and (2) the way out of your corporate sentence isn't the obvious one. Two lines instead of one — the recipient has earned more text. Slightly more conspiratorial tone. Still warm.

---

### c. Tray 1 Interior Wall (visible text on the inside wall, between item recesses)

Printed on the interior wall of Tray 1, visible in the gaps between the die-cut recesses that hold the items. Discovered while removing items or after items have been taken out.

> INVENTORY CHECKED: ✓
> CONTRABAND STATUS: PENDING

**Design note:** Formatted like an internal logistics marking — the kind of stamp or label you'd find on the inside of a crate or storage container during processing. "CONTRABAND STATUS: PENDING" implies the kit's contents haven't been fully cleared yet. It's a small institutional detail that rewards the close reader without demanding attention. Two lines. Stencil font.

---

### d. Tray 2 Interior Wall (visible text on the inside wall of Tray 2)

Different message from Tray 1. Discovered the same way.

> INMATE MORALE: MONITORED
> DO NOT REMOVE TRAYS DURING INSPECTION

**Design note:** "INMATE MORALE: MONITORED" is funny because it implies someone is tracking how the items in this tray affect the recipient. "DO NOT REMOVE TRAYS DURING INSPECTION" is an instruction the recipient is already violating — they've removed the tray. The format is a facility management label. The humor is the institutional system addressing the recipient's behavior in real time. Two lines. Same stencil format as Tray 1 interior.

---

### e. Kit Base (the very bottom of the briefcase, visible when everything is removed)

This is the final hidden message in the entire kit. The recipient has removed both trays, the compartment cover, the mug, and all the shredded classified paper. They are looking at a completely empty briefcase. This is the end credits.

> ---
>
> **CASE NO. 9-5**
>
> ---
>
> This briefcase is empty now.
> That means you found everything.
>
> The pen. The ink. The tags.
> The cards you'll actually use.
> The stapler that isn't there.
> The mug that was.
>
> You went through every layer.
> You looked under things.
> You read the fine print.
>
> Not everyone does that.
>
> Go survive something.
>
> ---
>
> CASE CLOSED. FILE ARCHIVED.
> ELEVATE CO. | RESTRICTED ACCESS DIVISION
>
> ---

**Design note:** This is the warmest copy in the entire kit, and it earns that warmth by being the last thing found. The recipient has to empty the entire briefcase to see it — most people won't, which makes it a genuine reward for the committed. The list ("The pen. The ink. The tags.") recaps the entire kit in fragments, including "the stapler that isn't there" (acknowledging the absence as part of the experience) and "the mug that was" (the hidden compartment payoff). "Not everyone does that." is the line — it tells the recipient they're the kind of person who goes further than expected, and it means it. "Go survive something." is the closing statement: warm, direct, and applicable to both the office and everything else. "CASE CLOSED. FILE ARCHIVED." is the institutional stamp that brings the briefcase's narrative full circle — CASE NO. 9-5 from the evidence tag on the exterior lid is now closed. The format stays buttoned-up. The content, for the first and only time, drops the jokes and speaks directly to the person holding the empty case. That's the move.
