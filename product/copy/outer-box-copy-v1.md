# Outer Amazon Box — Complete Copy

**Last Updated:** June 5, 2026
**Status:** Draft v1.2 — Single-character system. Brad Sterling is sole creator and voice.
**Purpose:** Every text element on the outer Amazon box, organized by surface in design priority order.
**Voice:** 100% Brad Sterling. Earnest, polished, zero self-awareness. He is never in on the joke.
**Brand:** Elevate Co.
**Product:** Office Survival Kit — Professional Edition™
**URL:** bradsterling.com (real page — "LAUNCHING SOON" with headshot and newsletter signup)

> **The Comedy Rule:** Format stays buttoned-up. Content gets unleashed. The typography, layout, label designs, and corporate letterhead stay polished and straight-faced. The actual words inside those formats go for laughs on every line. The format credibility is what makes the content funny. Every surface earns its space by being funny.

> **Reference:** For physical specs, surface dimensions, and FBA compliance zones, see amazon-strategy-v1.md.

### OB-2: Motivational Quote (Hero Copy Element)
**Treatment:** Large elegant white serif typography dominating the right two-thirds of the surface. Oversized gold decorative quotation marks. The kind of inspirational typography you see painted on WeWork lobby walls or printed on corporate motivational posters in office hallways. This IS the front face — one man's words, presented with the gravity of a Steve Jobs keynote slide.

**Placement:** Right zone (~10.5" wide), centered vertically, spanning nearly full width with generous margins

> "I didn't create this product to change lives. But I'm not going to stand here and pretend it hasn't."

**Attribution (below quote, separated by thin gold horizontal rule):**

> — Brad Sterling, Chief Vision Officer & Founder, Elevate Co.

**Design note:** Brad chose this quote from a longer list. He thinks it's humble. He tested it with focus groups (his employees, who are not allowed to give negative feedback). The quote is technically a contradiction — he DID create the product to change lives — but Brad doesn't notice because he thinks it sounds profound. The gold decorative quotation marks and formal attribution give a sentence that makes no logical sense the weight of a founder's manifesto.

## SURFACE 3 — BACK FACE (15.5" × 6.5")

*The "read more" surface. Same narrow panoramic proportions as the front face. Visible in Slot 8 detail shots and video. This is where Brad drops testimonials, his bio, and the fine print. Higher density than the front — this surface rewards close reading.*

### OB-13: Testimonials
**Treatment:** 4 fabricated testimonials in the style of Amazon review pull-quotes or back-of-book blurbs. Each has a name, title, and a quote that reads as plausible for 3-5 seconds, then lands as absurd. Arranged horizontally across the surface with small decorative quotation marks.

**Placement:** Upper half of the surface, spanning the full width

**Testimonial 1:**
> "Changed the way I think about Mondays."
> — D. Martinez, Regional Accounts

**Testimonial 2:**
> "I gave this to my entire team. Productivity is down. Morale is... complicated. Would buy again."
> — J. Adebayo, Operations Lead

**Testimonial 3:**
> "My coworker won't stop reading the cards out loud. I have spoken to management. They are also reading the cards out loud."
> — S. Park, Senior Analyst

**Testimonial 4:**
> "I keep the briefcase on my desk. People ask what it is. I tell them it's classified. I have never felt more powerful."
> — R. Walsh, IT Support

**Design note:** Testimonial 1 is the anchor — it reads as completely real. Could be on any product. Testimonial 2 introduces the first crack ("morale is... complicated" and "would buy again" despite describing a problem). Testimonial 3 is the escalation — describes the product causing an office incident, but the reviewer isn't complaining, just reporting. Testimonial 4 is the payoff — the product has given someone a personality. Each testimonial should have a different visual treatment (different quotation mark styles, slightly different text sizes) to look like they were collected from different sources. Star ratings optional but effective.

### OB-14: Founder Bio
**Treatment:** A 3-4 sentence "About the Founder" blurb. Corporate boilerplate applied to someone who clearly doesn't deserve it. Small headshot with gold frame.

**Placement:** Center of the surface, below testimonials

> **ABOUT THE FOUNDER**
>
> When Elevate Co. founder Brad Sterling noticed that American office workers had begun describing their jobs using the same vocabulary as minimum-security inmates, he didn't see a cultural crisis. He saw a market. "These people needed workplace elevation solutions," he says. "What they lacked was someone willing to create them, brand them, trademark them, and charge accordingly."
>
> Today, the Office Survival Kit — Professional Edition™ represents the pinnacle of his vision: premium workplace tools, premium packaging, and a premium price point that reflects the premium nature of the premium experience.
>
> Brad Sterling is a three-time nominee for the International Workplace Innovation Awards and an occasional keynote speaker at conferences he organizes himself.

**Design note:** Brad openly describes his customer base's misery as his business opportunity, lists trademarking as one of his core competencies alongside creating, and uses "charge accordingly" as if it's a public service. The word "premium" appears five times in one sentence — intentionally. His bio ends with credentials that undercut themselves: "three-time nominee" (never won — but the front face award badge says WINNER, which means he gave himself the award between writing the bio and printing the box), and "keynote speaker at conferences he organizes himself." Headshot: Brad himself — purple satin shirt, oversized gold rope chain, aviator sunglasses, big cheesy teeth-too-white smile, gold frame. This is consistent with every other Brad photo on the product. He always looks like this because he always looks like this. Small credit beneath: "PHOTO COURTESY OF ELEVATE CO."

**Treatment:** Standard product compliance block. Functional, clean. Must include a clear 4" × 6" zone for Amazon's FNSKU sticker.

**Placement:** Center of the surface — leave maximum clear space for Amazon's label application

> [UPC BARCODE — secondary placement]
>
> [FNSKU BARCODE — or clear 4" × 6" zone for Amazon's sticker]
>
> Elevate Co. | Workplace Solutions Division
> Designed in a co-working space. Manufactured on purpose.
> Distributed by Elevate Co.
> Contents: 1 Office Survival Kit — Professional Edition™
> Gross Weight: [TBD]
> Net Weight: Depends on your definition of "net"
>
> WARNING: This product is not a substitute for actual workplace reform, a living wage, competent management, or therapy. Elevate Co. assumes no liability for any outcomes including but not limited to: improved morale, worsened morale, lateral morale, disciplinary action, promotion, termination, or the sudden urge to update your LinkedIn profile.

The final sentence is Brad's customer service philosophy distilled to its essence — he has created a system for receiving feedback that is architecturally incapable of producing an outcome. "Taken very seriously" in scare quotes is the tell: Brad knows the exact phrasing companies use when they have no intention of doing anything. He's using it unironically. The entire bottom label reads as fine print that nobody is supposed to read — which is exactly why the people who DO flip the box will be rewarded.

**Design note:** This is the single most important piece of Brad copy in the entire product. Every line reveals character:

- "a co-working space — which I was not technically a member of" — Brad's origin story begins with him somewhere he doesn't belong. He drops this detail casually, as if trespassing in a WeWork is normal entrepreneurial behavior.
- "these people are in prison. Then I thought: someone should sell them something." — Brad's entire business model in two sentences. He identified human suffering, felt nothing, and saw revenue. He tells you this proudly.
- "organizations I founded that same week" — Brad registered award-giving bodies specifically to give himself awards. He confesses this in a letter he thinks makes him sound industrious.
- "the single most confident product launch he had ever been asked to resign over" — Brad reprints his advisor's resignation as a compliment. The advisor called it confident. Brad heard the adjective and missed the rest of the sentence.
- "a real one, from a website" — Brad's concept of photographer credibility. "From a website" is his quality seal. He thinks this adds legitimacy.
- "not a write-off" — The accountant's judgment. Brad includes it because he thinks it demonstrates how seriously he takes the brand. The accountant was saying "you cannot deduct this."
- "a different advisor, also since resigned" — Brad has burned through multiple advisors. He mentions this like it's a footnote. It is, in fact, the entire story.
- "cross a line that does not need to be crossed" — The advisor's exact words, which Brad quotes because he thinks they show how edgy and boundary-pushing he is. The advisor was begging him to stop.
- "$300 product at a price I have been personally asked not to print" — Brad is still mad about this. He genuinely believes this is worth $300. The "personally asked" implies multiple people had to tell him to stop.
- The credential stack in the sign-off — the self-organized conference award, now a permanent part of his signature. Brad signs everything this way.
- The P.S. is the bridge to the interior: Brad knows the briefcase looks nothing like premium packaging. Instead of addressing this honestly, he's retroactively claiming the scuffed, duct-taped aesthetic was his deliberate design choice. He's taking credit for something that actively undermines his brand, and he's doing it with complete confidence. "Please do not contact us about it" is Brad preemptively shutting down the complaint he knows is coming — which means he knows it looks bad, but he'd rather gaslight the customer than admit he didn't design the interior.

**This letter should be the last thing read before opening the briefcase.** The next thing the recipient sees is matte black cardboard, duct tape, and "CLEARED FOR OFFICE USE (BARELY)." The whiplash is the product.
