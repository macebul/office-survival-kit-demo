# Rap Sheets — Complete Box Copy

**Last Updated:** June 4, 2026
**Status:** Draft v1 — Full copy for all 6 surfaces + tuck flap
**Purpose:** Every text element on the Rap Sheets item box, organized by surface.
**Voice:** Brad Sterling / Elevate Co. — Mode 1 (institutional, enforcement). Played 100% straight.
**Brand:** Elevate Co. — Interpersonal Accountability Division
**Product:** Rap Sheets™ — Interpersonal Feedback Delivery System
**Division:** Interpersonal Accountability Division
**SKU:** EC-IAD-001

> **The Rule:** The format is the straight man. The content is the comedian. This box looks like it came from an internal affairs office that processes real workplace violations. The comedy is that the violations are things like leaving time on the microwave. The more seriously the box takes itself, the funnier the petty offenses become.

> **Naming Hierarchy (LOCKED):** "RAP SHEETS™" dominant. "Interpersonal Feedback Delivery System" subordinate. Matches Lifer Ink / Turf Tags pattern.

## SURFACE 1 — FRONT FACE (4.0" × 2.5")

*The face visible when the box sits in Tray 2. First thing the recipient reads. This surface establishes the enforcement world in one glance — government-inspected compliance product with QC seals and severity classifications.*

---

### RS-1: Brand Header
**Treatment:** Small, spaced uppercase sans-serif at top center. Official. Institutional.

**Placement:** Top center, above everything

> **ELEVATE CO.**
> Interpersonal Accountability Division

**Design note:** "Interpersonal Accountability Division" is Brad's enforcement arm. It mirrors the euphemistic corporate weight of "Territorial Compliance Division" (Stickers) and "Dermal Achievement Division" (Tats). "Accountability" is a funnier word than "Compliance" because it implies guilt. Brad created a whole division to formalize the act of complaining about coworkers. He considers this his most serious product line.

---

### RS-2: QC Inspection Badge
**Treatment:** Small circular badge in red and cream/off-white at the top of the front face. Government inspection mark aesthetic — the kind of stamp you'd see on military-issue equipment or a passed regulatory inspection.

**Placement:** Top area, near the brand header — either overlapping it slightly or positioned just below-right

> INSPECTED
> **QC**
> PASSED

**Design note:** The QC badge establishes the box as something that has been through an official quality assurance process. Brad had this "inspected" and "passed" by himself. The badge is modeled on government-issue inspection marks — circular, numbered, minimal. It implies a quality standard exists for complaint cards. It doesn't.

---

### RS-3: Product Name (Hero Copy Element)
**Treatment:** Large, dominant, bold condensed sans-serif. Industrial, high-impact. This is the text that reads from across the room. Dark red or cream on black.

**Placement:** Centered, dominant position on the face — the largest text element

> **RAP SHEETS™**

**Design note:** "Rap Sheets" works on two levels: (1) your documented criminal history — the paper trail of everything you've done, and (2) a complaint sheet — a formal document you file against someone. Brad uses the term in sense #2 while the prison context makes sense #1 land simultaneously. The prison term is dominant per the locked naming hierarchy. Brad doesn't know what a rap sheet actually is.

---

### RS-4: Corporate Rebrand Subtitle
**Treatment:** Smaller, lighter weight. Subordinate to "RAP SHEETS™" but clearly present. This is Brad's formal product classification.

**Placement:** Directly below "RAP SHEETS™", centered

> Interpersonal Feedback Delivery System

**Design note:** "Interpersonal Feedback Delivery System" is Brad's corporate euphemism for "formal complaint cards about your coworkers." Every word does work: "Interpersonal" (it's between people), "Feedback" (complaint, but polite), "Delivery System" (Brad thinks the physical card IS the delivery mechanism — like a missile guidance system, but for grievances). He named this like a defense contractor naming a weapons platform.

---

### RS-5: Tagline
**Treatment:** Set apart by thin divider lines or framing elements above and below. Reads as a product value proposition, completely straight-faced.

**Placement:** Below subtitle, centered, with breathing room

> Structured Accountability Cards for the Modern Workplace

**Design note:** "Structured Accountability" sounds like an HR initiative. "For the Modern Workplace" makes it sound like this product was developed to meet the evolving needs of today's organizations. It wasn't. It was developed because Brad once saw someone leave 0:03 on the microwave and decided documentation was the answer.

---

### RS-6: Card Count & Classification
**Treatment:** Small, clean, factual. Official product specification.

**Placement:** Below tagline, centered

> Contains 15 Documented Infractions · Classified: INTERDEPARTMENTAL

**Design note:** "Documented Infractions" instead of "cards." "Classified: INTERDEPARTMENTAL" is a security classification that means nothing — Brad invented it. He thinks classifying things makes them more important. The word "INTERDEPARTMENTAL" implies these complaint cards have been cleared for use across all departments, which is both unnecessary and exactly the kind of authorization Brad would seek.

---

### RS-7: Authorization Seal
**Treatment:** Official-looking seal with eagle emblem. Government/military aesthetic — the kind of authorization mark you'd see on a federal document or military supply crate. Dark red, cream, and black.

**Placement:** Bottom center, above fine print

> [Eagle emblem]
> AUTHORIZED FOR INTERDEPARTMENTAL USE
> Interpersonal Accountability Division
> Elevate Co. · Est. 2024

**Design note:** The eagle seal is the enforcement counterpart to Cell Talk's "CLINICALLY ADEQUATE™" stamp and Lifer Ink's "BOARD APPROVED" seal. Brad wanted something that looked like it came from a government agency. He designed it himself in PowerPoint. The "AUTHORIZED FOR INTERDEPARTMENTAL USE" text implies that someone — a board, an agency, an authority — reviewed these complaint cards and cleared them for deployment. That someone was Brad.

---

### RS-8: Fine Print & SKU
**Treatment:** Very small text at the bottom of the surface. Real packaging fine print.

**Placement:** Bottom edge, full width

> Filing a complaint does not constitute legal action, HR intervention, or actual accountability. Elevate Co. assumes no liability for retaliatory filings, counter-complaints, or awkward elevator rides.
>
> EC-IAD-001 | Elevate Co. Interpersonal Accountability Division

**Design note:** The disclaimer is the first real crack in the enforcement facade — it acknowledges that filing one of these cards doesn't actually DO anything. "Retaliatory filings" implies people will file Rap Sheets back at each other, which is exactly what will happen. "Awkward elevator rides" is specific and universal. The SKU follows the taxonomy: EC = Elevate Co., IAD = Interpersonal Accountability Division, 001 = first product.

## SURFACE 2 — BACK FACE (4.0" × 2.5")

*The "read more" surface. Visible when the recipient flips the box. This is where Brad appears as the enforcement authority. The format is an incident summary report — the kind of document internal affairs produces after an investigation. Dark, formal, data-dense.*

---

### RS-9: Report Header
**Treatment:** Bold header with thin divider. Enforcement document energy. Red text on black, or cream on black.

**Placement:** Top of surface, spanning width

> **INCIDENT SUMMARY REPORT**
> Interpersonal Accountability Division · Case Review

**Design note:** "Incident Summary Report" is a real document format used in HR, law enforcement, and corporate investigations. Brad formats everything as an official report because official reports imply that investigation happened. No investigation happened. Brad wrote this on a Tuesday.

---

### RS-10: Enforcement Data Block
**Treatment:** A structured data block — fields with labels and values, like an incident report or case file summary. Tabular, official, information-dense.

**Placement:** Upper half of surface, below header

> **Infractions Documented:** 15
> **Severity Range:** Moderate to Hostile
> **Field Tested:** 3 Departments, 1 Break Room, 1 Parking Lot
> **Reported Outcomes:**
> &nbsp;&nbsp; Behavioral correction ........... 2%
> &nbsp;&nbsp; Momentary guilt ................. 34%
> &nbsp;&nbsp; Retaliatory filing .............. 67%
> &nbsp;&nbsp; Complete denial ................. 89%
> **Compliance Status:** Pending
> **Recommended Action:** Immediate Distribution

**Design note:** The data block mirrors Cell Talk's clinical outcomes but in enforcement register. The outcomes tell a story: almost nobody corrected their behavior (2%), a third felt briefly guilty, two-thirds filed complaints BACK, and 89% denied everything. "Field Tested: 3 Departments, 1 Break Room, 1 Parking Lot" is specific enough to be funny — Brad tested these in a break room and a parking lot, which are the two places workplace grievances actually originate. "Compliance Status: Pending" means nobody has complied yet. "Recommended Action: Immediate Distribution" means Brad's solution to non-compliance is to hand out more complaints.

---

### RS-11: Brad Sterling — Enforcement Authority
**Treatment:** Small photo of Brad (professional headshot, dark clothing, serious expression, direct eye contact — "I've reviewed your file" energy), with his title and a quote. Framed as the division chief.

**Placement:** Center-lower portion of the surface

> [Brad headshot — serious enforcement variant]
>
> **Brad Sterling**
> Chief Accountability Officer
> Interpersonal Accountability Division
> Elevate Co.
>
> *"Documentation isn't passive-aggressive. It's proactive-aggressive. There's a difference. The difference is a filing system."*

**Design note:** Brad's title changes per division. On Cell Talk he's "Chief Normative Optimization Officer" (wellness). On Stickers he's "Chief Territorial Officer." Here he's "Chief Accountability Officer" — the man in charge of documenting everyone's offenses. His quote reveals that Brad sees formal complaint-filing as a *superior* form of aggression — not passive, but proactive. He has a filing system. He's organized about his grievances. The word "proactive-aggressive" is Brad coining a term he thinks is motivational.

---

### RS-12: Disclaimer
**Treatment:** Very small text at the bottom. Real legal disclaimer format.

**Placement:** Bottom edge of surface

> Reported outcomes based on field deployment across three (3) Elevate Co. departments. Participants were not informed they were participants. "Behavioral correction" defined as any visible change in behavior, including avoidance. Sample included Brad, who filed all 15 infractions himself and then reviewed his own complaints favorably.

**Design note:** The disclaimer systematically dismantles every claim on the surface. Participants didn't know they were in a study. "Behavioral correction" is defined so broadly it includes people avoiding Brad entirely — which counts as a change in behavior. And the kicker: Brad filed all 15 infractions himself, then reviewed his own complaints and gave them a favorable rating. He is the filer, the reviewer, and the subject.

## SURFACE 3 — TOP LONG EDGE (4.0" × 0.75")

*Narrow strip visible when box is sitting in the tray. One strong beat.*

---

### RS-13: Classification Stamp
**Treatment:** Centered text, bold stencil/stamp aesthetic. Reads like a government classification mark or evidence locker label. Red or amber on black.

**Placement:** Centered on the narrow strip

> FILED UNDER: HOSTILE

**Design note:** This is a severity classification stamp. The hero image cards use a severity system (including "HOSTILE"), and this element extends that system to the box itself. "FILED UNDER: HOSTILE" implies the entire box has been classified at the highest severity level — which means Brad considers ALL 15 offenses hostile. Leaving time on the microwave is hostile. Saying "sounds good" is hostile. Brad does not have a proportionate response system.

## SURFACE 4 — BOTTOM LONG EDGE (4.0" × 0.75")

*Narrow strip on the underside. Reward for the close reader.*

---

### RS-14: Statute Line
**Treatment:** Small centered text, clean sans-serif. Dark red or cream on black.

**Placement:** Centered on the narrow strip

> NO STATUTE OF LIMITATIONS. NO APPEALS. NO EXCEPTIONS.

**Design note:** Three phrases, escalating finality. "No statute of limitations" means your office crimes never expire — Brad remembers the microwave incident from 2019. "No appeals" means you can't contest the charge. "No exceptions" closes every loophole. This reads like the sentencing guidelines for a military tribunal, applied to complaint cards about replying "sounds good" to emails. The rhythm is important — each phrase gets shorter and more absolute.

## SURFACE 5 — SHORT SIDE A (2.5" × 0.75")

*One of the two narrow ends. Visible when box sits sideways in the tray.*

---

### RS-15: Chain of Custody Warning
**Treatment:** Small text, enforcement warning format. Bold header, body text beneath. Red header, cream body on black.

**Placement:** Full width, centered vertically

> **CHAIN OF CUSTODY:**
> This product has been used as
> evidence in 14 workplace disputes,
> 6 uncomfortable elevator rides,
> and 1 all-staff meeting that went
> off the rails. Handle accordingly.

**Design note:** "Chain of custody" is an evidence-handling term from law enforcement — the documented trail of who had possession of evidence and when. Brad is applying it to a box of complaint cards. The statistics are specific and escalate: workplace disputes (expected), uncomfortable elevator rides (the aftermath of filing a complaint against the person you share an elevator with), and one all-staff meeting that derailed (someone brought their Rap Sheet to the meeting). "Handle accordingly" is the kind of vague official instruction that means nothing but sounds important.

## SURFACE 6 — SHORT SIDE B (2.5" × 0.75")

*The other narrow end.*

---

### RS-16: Filing Procedure Icon Strip
**Treatment:** 5 simple line-art icons in a horizontal row, each with a tiny label beneath. Same format as Cell Talk (WAKE > DRAW > READ > BREATHE > REPEAT) and Turf Tags (PEEL > STICK > HOLD COURT > ACCEPT PARADE > EXIT). Enforcement/bureaucratic icon style — clipboard, document, handshake-refusal, walking away.

**Placement:** Full width, icons evenly spaced

> **FILING PROCEDURE:**
>
> 📋 → 📄 → 🤝 → 🚶 → 🙅
> IDENTIFY · DOCUMENT · PRESENT · EXIT · DENY

**Design note:** Replace emoji with clean line-art icons in production: clipboard (identify the offender), document/stamp (file the complaint), hand presenting card (deliver it), figure walking away (exit the scene), crossed arms or "stop" hand (deny involvement). The sequence tells the recipient how to deploy a complaint card. It starts with identification (you know who this is for), moves through documentation (pick the right card), presentation (hand it over), and ends with EXIT and DENY — because after filing a workplace complaint via novelty card, you leave and deny everything. The last two steps are the comedy — it's what everyone actually does after confrontation.

## SURFACE 7 — TUCK FLAP (interior, visible when box is opened)

*The first text the recipient sees when they open the box. One line. The threshold moment.*

---

### RS-17: Tactical Instruction
**Treatment:** Bold, centered, cream or dark red text on black. Direct. Commanding.

**Placement:** Centered on tuck flap interior

> Start with Kevin.

**Design note:** The design brief recommended "START WITH KEVIN." and it's the right call. Kevin is the recurring character across the kit — the coworker everyone has. The instruction is tactical: you know who your Kevin is. Start there. File the first complaint against the most obvious offender. It works because every recipient has a Kevin. The specificity of a name (not "start with your worst coworker" but "start with Kevin") makes it immediately personal and immediately funny. The period makes it a command, not a suggestion.
