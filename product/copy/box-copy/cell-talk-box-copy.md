# Cell Talk — Complete Box Copy

**Last Updated:** June 4, 2026
**Status:** Draft v2 — All surfaces reviewed and locked with stakeholder
**Purpose:** Every text element on the Cell Talk item box, organized by surface.
**Voice:** Brad Sterling / Elevate Co. — Mode 1 (institutional, clinical wellness). Played 100% straight.
**Brand:** Elevate Co. — Center for Normative Optimization
**Product:** Cell Talk™ — Daily Affirmation Deck
**Division:** Center for Normative Optimization

> **The Rule:** The format is the straight man. The content is the comedian. This box looks like a legitimate wellness product from a corporate optimization program. The more sincerely it takes itself, the funnier it becomes in context.

> **Naming Hierarchy (LOCKED):** "CELL TALK™" dominant. "Daily Affirmation Deck" subordinate. Matches Lifer Ink / Turf Tags pattern.

## SURFACE 1 — FRONT FACE (4.0" × 2.5")

*The face visible when the box sits in Tray 2. First thing the recipient reads. This surface establishes the entire wellness world in one glance.*

---

### CT-1: Brand Header
**Treatment:** Small, spaced uppercase sans-serif at top center. Understated. Institutional.

**Placement:** Top center, above everything

> **ELEVATE CO.**
> Center for Normative Optimization

**Design note:** "Center for Normative Optimization" is Brad's fake wellness division. It sounds exactly like a real corporate initiative — the kind of department that gets announced in a company-wide email nobody reads. "Normative" is a word Brad uses because he thinks it means "making things normal." It doesn't. He doesn't care.

---

### CT-2: Enso Circle Motif
**Treatment:** A single open-stroke circle (ensō) in sage green, centered. Clean, meditative. The kind of symbol you'd see on a mindfulness app icon or a yoga studio business card.

**Placement:** Centered, between brand header and product name. Generous whitespace around it.

**Design note:** The enso is a real zen symbol representing enlightenment, the void, and the beauty of imperfection. Brad chose it because he Googled "zen symbol" and it was the first result. He has never drawn one by hand. He thinks the gap in the circle represents "room to grow your brand."

---

### CT-3: Product Name (Hero Copy Element)
**Treatment:** Large, dominant, clean sans-serif or light serif. This is the first text the recipient actually reads. It should feel like the name of a wellness program, not a product.

**Placement:** Centered, below enso circle, spanning most of the width

> **CELL TALK™**

**Design note:** "Cell Talk" works on two levels: (1) the things you say to yourself in your cell to get through the day, and (2) a play on "self-talk," the actual therapeutic concept. Brad has no idea about the first meaning. He thinks "Cell Talk" is a clever brand name for a self-talk product. He trademarked it immediately.

---

### CT-4: Corporate Rebrand Subtitle
**Treatment:** Smaller, lighter weight. Subordinate to "CELL TALK™" but clearly present. This is Brad's formal product classification.

**Placement:** Directly below "CELL TALK™", centered

> Daily Affirmation Deck

**Design note:** Clean, short, tells you what this is. Doesn't try to be clever — the product name and tagline carry the humor. This line just communicates format. Three words.

---

### CT-5: Tagline
**Treatment:** Italic or light weight, set apart by thin divider lines above and below. Reads as a brand promise.

**Placement:** Below subtitle, centered, with breathing room

> *Finally, a reason to talk to yourself at work.*

**Design note:** This is the central joke of the product — reframing self-talk (something people are embarrassed about) as something officially sanctioned. Brad genuinely believes he's solved a problem. The tagline is warm and encouraging, not cynical. It makes the recipient feel seen, not judged.

---

### CT-6: Card Count
**Treatment:** Small, clean, factual.

**Placement:** Below tagline, centered

> 15 Cards. All Essential.

**Design note:** "All Essential" is Brad. He doesn't believe in filler. Every card in this deck was personally approved by Brad, which means every card was written by Brad, reviewed by Brad, and rated "essential" by Brad. The count may change to 30 — update this line accordingly.

---

### CT-7: Certification Seal
**Treatment:** Small circular seal or badge. Clinical, official. Not a fake award — a fake certification. Looks like a wellness board approval stamp.

**Placement:** Bottom center, above fine print

> EXCEEDS CLINICAL EXPECTATIONS™
> Elevate Co.
> Center for Normative Optimization
> Est. 2024

**Design note:** "EXCEEDS CLINICAL EXPECTATIONS" is corporate performance review language ("exceeds expectations") with "clinical" grafted on. Brad rated his own product like an employee review — and gave it the highest possible mark. The ™ means Brad trademarked this rating. Of course he did.

---

### CT-8: Fine Print
**Treatment:** Very small text at the bottom of the surface.

**Placement:** Bottom edge, full width

> Designed by someone who has never once doubted himself.

**Design note:** This line tells you everything about Brad without saying his name. It's warm — self-doubt is relatable, and the fact that the designer has NONE is absurd and endearing. It also sets up the back face, where Brad appears in person and confirms this assessment completely.

## SURFACE 2 — BACK FACE (4.0" × 2.5")

*The "read more" surface. Visible when the recipient flips the box. The top half is a program brief — describing what Cell Talk is in institutional format. The bottom half is Brad, appearing as the wellness authority. The format is a wellness product back panel — the kind of thing you'd see on the back of a mindfulness card deck, but written by Brad and filtered through his institutional lens.*

---

### CT-9: Section Header
**Treatment:** Clean, centered, slightly larger than body text. Warm but institutional.

**Placement:** Top of surface

> **WORDS FROM YOUR INNER VOICE**

**Design note:** Works on two levels: (1) the affirmations are words from YOUR inner voice — encouraging, warm, self-directed, and (2) Brad thinks HE is your inner voice now. He designed the cards, so in his mind, he IS the voice in your head. The reader gets the warm reading. Brad gets the delusional one.

---

### CT-10: Program Brief
**Treatment:** A structured data block — field labels with values, like an institutional program description or intake form. Clean, clinical, tabular.

**Placement:** Upper half of surface, below header

> **PROGRAM:** Cell Talk™ Daily Affirmation Initiative
> **ADMINISTERED BY:** Center for Normative Optimization
> **ELIGIBLE:** Full-time, part-time, and involuntary.
> **FREQUENCY:** Daily. Results compound. Side effects are yours to keep.
> **PURPOSE:** Regulated optimism for confined professionals.
> **STATUS:** Mandatory voluntary.

**Design note:** The program brief is formatted like an institutional bulletin board notice — the kind of thing posted in a break room or a corrections facility rec room. Every field escalates: "Eligible" includes "involuntary" (you didn't choose to be here). "Frequency" promises results will compound — then notes side effects are yours, like a pharmaceutical benefit. "Purpose" is "regulated optimism" — implying optimism was happening in an uncontrolled way and Brad decided it needed oversight. "Confined professionals" works for both cubicles and cells. "Mandatory voluntary" is the punchline — a real thing in both prisons and offices. Brad sees no contradiction.

---

### CT-11: Brad Sterling — Wellness Authority
**Treatment:** Small circular photo of Brad (professional headshot, turtleneck or open-collar, serene expression, slightly tilted head — "I'm listening" energy), with his title and a quote. Framed as the program director.

**Placement:** Lower portion of the surface, below program brief, separated by a thin divider

> [Brad headshot — serene wellness guru variant]
>
> **Brad Sterling**
> Chief Normative Optimization Officer
> Elevate Co.
>
> *"Someone told me they didn't need these. I taped 30 to his desk. No one's said that since. Clearly the value speaks for itself."*

**Design note:** Brad's quote is the anchor of the back face. The joke: someone rejected Brad's affirmation cards. Brad's response was to aggressively deploy 30 of them onto that person's desk. Nobody has complained since — not because they recognized the value, but because they're afraid of getting 30 more. Brad reads the terrified silence as market validation. He genuinely believes "the value speaks for itself." The gap between what happened and what Brad thinks happened IS the joke.

## SURFACE 3 — TOP LONG EDGE (4.0" × 0.75")

*Narrow strip visible when box is sitting in the tray. One strong beat.*

---

### CT-13: Dosage Instruction
**Treatment:** Centered text, clean institutional format with middot separators. Reads like a dosage strip on pharmaceutical packaging.

**Placement:** Centered on the narrow strip

> ONE PER DAY · MORE IF NEEDED · FEWER IF THRIVING

**Design note:** Three phrases that escalate in a satisfying way: "one per day" (clinical), "more if needed" (compassionate), "fewer if thriving" (aspirational — Brad genuinely believes some people are so optimized they need fewer cards). The middots give it a pharmaceutical label feel. It's the most human thing on the box.

## SURFACE 4 — BOTTOM LONG EDGE (4.0" × 0.75")

*Narrow strip on the underside. Reward for the close reader. Visually and tonally distinct from the top edge — a guided breathing exercise instead of a text strip.*

---

### CT-14: Guided Breathing Exercise
**Treatment:** Small centered text. "BREATHE IN" and "BREATHE OUT" as labels, followed by the content. A genuine guided breathing moment — the kind of thing you'd see on a wellness poster in a therapist's office.

**Placement:** Centered on the narrow strip

> BREATHE IN: I am a valued member of this organization. BREATHE OUT: This too shall pass. Probably.

**Design note:** The inhale is a genuine corporate affirmation — warm, encouraging, the kind of thing you'd read on a motivational poster. The exhale starts sincere ("this too shall pass") and then deflates with "Probably." The format is real (guided breathing instructions exist on wellness products). The content starts positive and lets just enough reality leak through on the exhale. It's warm — "probably" isn't cynical, it's honest. Everyone's had that moment.

---

## SURFACE 5 — SHORT SIDE A (2.5" × 0.75")

*One of the two narrow ends. Visible when box sits sideways in the tray.*

---

### CT-15: Product Testing Certification
**Treatment:** Small text, certification/compliance format. Bold, centered, completely straight-faced.

**Placement:** Full width, centered vertically

> TESTED ON EMPLOYEES.
> NOT THE OTHER WAY AROUND.

**Design note:** Uses the pharmaceutical/product testing format as the straight man. "Tested on employees" sounds like a real product claim — like "tested on humans" or "clinically tested." "Not the other way around" flips it — implying that normally, employees are the ones getting tested (performance reviews, evaluations, assessments). Brad framed it as a reassurance: for once, the product is being tested, not you. The line also carries subtle prison energy — testing on inmates vs. testing on employees. Two sentences. No wasted words.

## SURFACE 6 — SHORT SIDE B (2.5" × 0.75")

*The other narrow end.*

---

### CT-16: Before/After Wellness Battery
**Treatment:** Two small battery graphics side by side or stacked. The "BEFORE" battery is nearly empty. The "AFTER" battery is full and exceeds 100%. Clean line-art style, with percentage labels.

**Placement:** Full width, centered vertically

> BEFORE: [▪░░░░] 12%
> AFTER:  [▪▪▪▪▪] 140%

**Design note:** A visual wellness rating using the universally understood battery icon. The "BEFORE" battery is at 12% — a precise number (very Brad) for something unmeasurable. The "AFTER" battery is at 140% — you can't be at 140% battery. Brad thinks you can. That's the joke. It's optimistic, warm, and absurd. The specificity of both numbers makes it feel like Brad actually measured this. He did. On himself. The format is visually distinct from every other surface on this box — it's a graphic element, not a text strip, which gives the recipient something different to look at.

## SURFACE 7 — TUCK FLAP (interior, visible when box is opened)

*The first text the recipient sees when they open the box. One line. The threshold moment.*

---

### CT-17: Opening Moment
**Treatment:** Clean, centered, sage green text on white. Simple. Quiet. Warm.

**Placement:** Centered on tuck flap interior

> *You opened this for a reason.*

**Design note:** Six words. Warm, direct, and true — the recipient DID open it for a reason, even if the reason is just curiosity. After an entire box of institutional language, program briefs, dosage instructions, and Brad's self-mythology, the tuck flap speaks directly to the person holding the cards. It implies they needed this (in a kind way, not condescending). It's the most genuine thing on the box.
