## BRAD STERLING — TWO MODES

**Mode 1: Product Copy Brad** — Competent. Dry. Institutional. Actually good at marketing. The FORMAT is the straight man (real packaging language), the CONTENT is the comedian (absurd/dark things stated in that format). This is the primary voice on all packaging.

**Mode 2: Personal Brand Brad** — Hustle-culture, zero self-awareness, motivational-speaker cadence. Completely sincere. Never winks. "Premium" is his comma. He trademarks everything. This voice appears on Brad-specific elements (founder quotes, personal endorsements).

**The gap between the two Brads is the joke.** Same person: competent product copy AND delusional self-promotion. The reader sees the contradiction. Brad never does. Brad has never done office time — he's adjacent to this world, fluent in its language but not a veteran.

## COMEDY MECHANICS (summary — full version is in the comedy-writing skill)

**Humor elements (every piece needs at least 2 of 6):**
Clever, Bizarre, Cruel, Naughty, Recognizable, Cute. "Recognizable" should almost always be one of them.

**Escalation pattern:**
Line 1 plausible → Line 2 slight edge → Line 3 clearly a joke → Line 4 full absurd. **Format never cracks.**

**Kill on sight:** No puns. No exclamation points in product copy. No winking at the reader. No actual workplace advice. No making Brad self-aware. No generic insults (every insult needs absurd specificity).

---

## THE VOICE — CRITICAL REQUIREMENTS

### What the voice IS
A warm, cheerful, slightly conspiratorial friend who is genuinely excited to sell you something ridiculous. The brand KNOWS what it's selling and is DELIGHTED about it.

**Tone markers:** "Look," "Seriously," "Trust us," "We're not going to ask," "We believe in you," "Which is to say."

### What the voice is NOT
- NOT dry, clinical, or neutral
- NOT professional or corporate-sounding
- NOT a one-liner comedian rattling off disconnected jokes
- NOT confused about its own product
- NOT instructing the reader to be threatening

### The peppiness requirement
We have repeatedly fallen into writing copy that is too professional and too serious. The copy needs enthusiasm, warmth, personality, levity, and a "we're going to make your life so great" vibe. Compare:

**TOO NEUTRAL:** "Won't bleed through paper. Can't speak for anything else."
**CORRECT:** "Other pens leak in your pocket. This one doesn't leak anywhere. Not in your pocket. Not in your hand. Not in Kevin. Completely leak-proof design."

The correct version is chatty, building, listing things off like someone excitedly showing you features.

## THE 7 MECHANICAL RULES

1. **Joke lands on the LAST word or LAST phrase.** Nothing after the punchline.
2. **Setup is a REAL product copy format.** Feature bullet, spec, disclaimer, care instruction, FAQ, contents list. The reader's brain recognizes the format BEFORE the joke hits.
3. **Punchline word must be CONCRETE and UNCOMFORTABLE.** Not "concerns" — instead: specific nouns, specific actions, specific situations.
4. **Talk TO the buyer.** "Your ___." "You'll ___." Make the reader complicit.
5. **Be CHEERFUL about something you shouldn't be cheerful about.** The brand is your fun, slightly irresponsible friend.
6. **Never instruct the reader to be threatening.** Describe the product in a way that makes the threat OBVIOUS without directing it.
7. **Rule of three when using lists.** Two normal items, third breaks the pattern. The third must be a REAL THING, not a concept.

**Additional:** Strongest lines are 5-13 words. Ratio: ~60-70% normal product copy, 30-40% absurd. The second sentence makes the first worse.

### Structural feedback
- Rule of three: "Normal, normal, crazy. Three of the same category isn't funny. Two normal things followed by one crazy thing IS funny."
- Setup: "A user who is using the pen normally, and then it turns murderous is the right setup/structure."
- Boss wants lines that are DARK — "BATHES IN RED" level darkness, delivered with warmth.

### Universal rejection
The entire v01 box mockup (all items, all surfaces) was rejected: "not funny enough, doesn't meet the standards set by existing Witty Yeti products." Issues: too serious, too professional, too neutral, not enough personality.

## WHAT NOT TO DO (comprehensive — all hard-earned lessons)

- Do NOT generate lines where the copy is aware of its own joke
- Do NOT add buttons, tags, or reactions after the punchline
- Do NOT use Kevin as a punchline — Kevin appears unremarked in lists
- Do NOT write office observational humor ("meetings suck," "survived another Monday")
- Do NOT leave the office/prison world (no military, government, international law)
- Do NOT repeat the same structural trick endlessly
- Do NOT explain the joke in any way
- Do NOT lead with violence and pivot to product — lead with product
- Do NOT use abstract/corporate punchline words ("concerns," "responsibility")
- Do NOT write dry, clinical, neutral copy — the voice is WARM and CHEERFUL
- Do NOT write three things from the same category in a rule-of-three
- Do NOT make it tame or pull punches — the boss wants it DARK, delivered with warmth
- Do NOT repeat verbs/concepts across bullets
- Do NOT use puns — Brad is dry
- Do NOT use exclamation points in product copy
- Do NOT wink at the reader or say "get it?"

## THE FULL WORKFLOW (follow this order)

This is the process we developed and refined across the Cubicle Shank sessions. Follow it for every item.

### Phase 1: Context Gathering
1. Read the item's working document (`product/copy/box-copy/[item]-working-doc.md`)
2. Read the item's existing box copy file (`product/copy/box-copy/[item]-box-copy.md`) if it exists
3. Read the item's design brief (`product/vibe-design/briefs/[item]-box.md`) if it exists
4. Read the interior layout spec (`product/interior-layout-spec.md`) for physical dimensions and placement
5. Read the world rules (`product/copy/world-rules.md`)
6. Read the brand context (`product/vibe-design/shared/brand-context.md`)
7. Check for existing v01/v02 mockup images in `product/images/0 - Images/`
8. Read the completed Cubicle Shank surface map (`product/copy/box-copy/cubicle-shank-surface-map.md`) as the format example

### Phase 2: Inventory & Evaluate
1. Pull ALL existing copy from the working doc — every line, every status tag
2. Evaluate each line against the comedy-writing skill checklist (invoke the skill)
3. Flag what's strong enough to lock, what needs refinement, what should be cut
4. Identify gaps — what copy types are missing for this item's surfaces?
5. Present the inventory with honest assessments to the boss for direction

### Phase 3: Copy Development
1. Generate new copy to fill gaps, following the 7 mechanical rules
2. Every line must serve a real packaging purpose (description, feature bullet, care instruction, warranty, FAQ, fine print, etc.)
3. Test every line against the comedy-writing review checklist before presenting
4. Present full surface drafts, not disconnected one-liners
5. Get boss decisions on placements, cuts, and locks

### Phase 4: Surface Mapping
1. Create a surface map document: `product/copy/box-copy/[item]-surface-map.md`
2. **Every word of copy must be spelled out in code blocks.** No references to other documents. No "see Section 3.2." The surface map is completely self-contained — a designer must be able to execute from this document alone.
3. Each element gets a numbered entry with exact placement, full copy in a code block, and design notes
4. Include a decisions log recording what was placed where and why
5. Include a backup reserve section with alternate/bench copy spelled out in full
6. Include a section for copy not on this item (available for other items)
7. Follow the exact format used in `cubicle-shank-surface-map.md`

### Phase 5: Image Prompts
1. Write Midjourney v6.1 prompts for each surface (front, back, sides as needed)
2. **CRITICAL: Every word of copy designated for that surface must appear in the prompt.** This is the #1 lesson from the Cubicle Shank process. If copy is not explicitly in the prompt, it will not appear in the mockup. Do not summarize. Do not reference. Spell out every word.
3. Prompt format: Single continuous text block. Describe the physical object, then walk through every text element in order with the exact copy in quotes. End with style/mood description and `--no` exclusions.
4. Use `--ar 5:4 --s 250 --v 6.1` as default parameters
5. Add the prompts to the surface map document AND provide them directly in conversation for copy-paste
6. Include prompt notes explaining what's intentionally omitted (hidden elements, deferred treatments, etc.)

### Phase 6: Working Doc Update
1. Update the item's working document with all session decisions
2. Lock/cut/bench lines as decided
3. Add comedy mechanics notes explaining why lines work
4. Update the "Last Updated" date
5. Record anything deferred and why

## DOCUMENT MANAGEMENT RULES

These rules exist because we lost copy across session compactions. They are non-negotiable.

1. **The working doc is the master record.** Every line of copy — locked, kept, cut, benched — lives here with its status tag and comedy mechanics notes. Nothing from any session should exist outside this file.

2. **The surface map is the design execution doc.** Every word spelled out in code blocks. Self-contained. A designer can build from this alone.

3. **All three versions of any copy must be recorded.** If there's a full version, a condensed version, and a short version, all three go in the working doc with clear labels.

4. **Status tags are mandatory.** `[LOCKED]` `[APPROVED]` `[KEEP]` `[MAYBE]` `[BENCH]` `[INVENTORY]` `[NEEDS WORK]` — every line gets one.

5. **Nothing is boss-approved unless marked `[APPROVED]`.** The boss has to explicitly say yes. Silence = rejection.

6. **Save everything.** Cut lines get moved to a "Cut" subsection with strikethrough and the reason. Never delete copy — archive it.

7. **Cross-item ideas go in** `product/copy/box-copy/cross-item-ideas-bank.md` — lines that don't fit this item but might work elsewhere.

## LESSONS LEARNED (from the Cubicle Shank process — do not repeat these mistakes)

1. **Spell out EVERY word of copy in the surface map.** No section references, no "see working doc." Every word in code blocks. A designer who has never seen the working doc must be able to execute from the surface map alone.

2. **Spell out EVERY word of copy in the image prompts.** If a footnote says "The Cubicle Shank Premium Experience™ includes: holster (thigh or desk-mount), burner phone (pre-loaded), getaway car (compact), and one (1) character witness named Doug. Doug has been briefed. Doug will not break." — then EVERY ONE OF THOSE WORDS goes in the prompt. Not a summary. Not a paraphrase. The exact copy.

3. **Record all versions of copy.** If there's a full version and a condensed version, BOTH go in the working doc with labels. Copy has been lost across session compactions because only one version was recorded.

4. **The image prompt is NOT the image generator's fault.** If copy is missing from the mockup, check the prompt first. The prompt is the source of truth for what appears.

5. **Provide image prompts directly in conversation** for copy-paste, in addition to saving them in the surface map file. The user needs to be able to grab them immediately.

6. **Do not blame the AI image generator** for missing text that wasn't specified in the prompt. Own the mistake.

7. **Ask about placement BEFORE building the surface map.** Get explicit decisions on what goes where. Don't assume.

8. **Characters and elements that were cut stay cut.** If the boss said no to Gary QC stamps on this item, don't keep suggesting them.

9. **The working doc date must be updated** every session. The surface map date too.

10. **Be fast with file operations.** Don't spend 5 minutes on what should take 30 seconds. The user will do it themselves if you're too slow.
