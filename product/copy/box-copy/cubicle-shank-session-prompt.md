## THE PROJECT

I'm writing comedy product copy for the **Cubicle Shank** — a regular ballpoint pen marketed as a tactical weapon. It's one item in the "Office Survival Kit," a gag gift briefcase sold on Amazon under the **Witty Yeti** brand. The in-world brand is **Elevate Co.**, run by fictional founder **Brad Sterling**.

The pen ships in a small tray-and-sleeve box (~6.5" x 1.75" x 1.0") with 7 printable surfaces. We need copy for all surfaces plus additional marketing lines.

## THE WORLD

The office is a prison. This is not a metaphor — it's the default reality in this product's universe. Nobody comments on it. Prison vocabulary (doing time, your cell, the yard, contraband, shank) is used matter-of-fact. The humor isn't in pointing out the comparison. The humor is that nobody thinks it's a comparison.

**Brad Sterling** has two modes:
- **Mode 1 (Product Copy):** Competent, dry, institutional. Actually good at marketing. Format is the straight man, content is the comedian.
- **Mode 2 (Personal Brand):** Hustle-culture, zero self-awareness, motivational-speaker cadence. Completely sincere. Thinks he's a visionary.

**Recurring characters:** Kevin (the coworker, 4-6 refs across kit), Gary (QC stamps only, 2-3 refs), Diane (HR, implied through policy language, 1-2 refs).

## WHAT THE BOSS WANTS — THE VOICE ENGINE

### What works (derived from the ONLY lines he approved)

**The violence lives in DOUBLE MEANINGS, not in stated violence.**
- "Penetration depth" — real product testing term that also means stabbing
- "Bathes in red" — could be ink, could be blood, copy doesn't clarify
- "Eventually" — answers a color availability question; implies the pen will GET red through use
- "Not in Kevin" — Kevin is just another item in a list of places the pen doesn't leak

**The copy is doing its job normally. It's a real product statement.**
- "Leak-proof design" — actual product claim
- "Not responsible for" — actual disclaimer language
- "Does it come in red?" — actual customer FAQ
- "Penetration depth" — actual product testing metric

**The copy NEVER knows it's funny.**
- "Completely leak-proof design" closes the Kevin line like an actual product description
- No wink. No nudge. No "get it?" No reaction to what was just said.

**Violence enters as ONE WORD or ONE PHRASE in an otherwise completely normal sentence.**
- "Not in Kevin" — two words in a list
- "Bathes in red" — three words parallel to a normal clause
- "Eventually" — one word

### What DOESN'T work (rejected consistently)

1. **Stated violence** — "Use on Kevin," "stabbing," "deploy against coworkers." The copy is SAYING the violent thing instead of letting the reader infer it.

2. **Winking/nudging** — "You know why," "if you know what we mean," "technically," "we mean writing clarity." The copy is acknowledging its own joke.

3. **Buttons and punchlines after the joke** — "Act accordingly," "you're welcome," "ask Kevin," "the autopsy confirmed it." Adding a reaction AFTER the violent implication weakens it. The joke should end ON the implication, not comment on it.

4. **Office observational humor** — "Meetings suck," "survived another Monday," "everything is a meeting." Knowing nod, not a laugh.

5. **Euphemism** — "Left a permanent mark," "got the point," "signed someone." Too clever, requires decoding.

6. **Non-sequitur absurdity** — Crow gifts, British accents, bizarre life improvements. Random ≠ funny.

7. **Leaving the office/prison world** — Geneva Convention, federal watchlist, CDC references. Too far outside the universe.

8. **Lines where violence is the SUBJECT** — "Blood? Sure. Screaming? Occasionally." These lead with violence and pivot to product. The boss's approved lines do the OPPOSITE — they lead with product and violence shows up uninvited.

9. **Repeating the same structural trick** — "Statement. (That's not true.)" or "X-resistant. Y-resistant. VIOLENT-WORD-resistant." The violent third item needs to be a real material property with a double meaning, not just a violent word jammed onto the pattern.

### The two engines that produce hits

**Engine 1: Dry weapon spec that's also a real pen spec.**
Lines like "penetration depth," "tip hardness rated for repeated impact," "performs through fabric." These read like actual product testing language. The violence is in what the words ALSO mean when applied to a shank. The copy is describing pen performance. The reader hears weapon performance. Nobody breaks character.

**Engine 2: Normal product copy where violence is one item in a list.**
Lines like the Kevin leak-proof line, "does it come in red / eventually," "ink stains, blood stains, or the difference between the two." These are real product statements — FAQ answers, disclaimers, feature descriptions — where violence enters as just another data point. The copy doesn't pause, doesn't react, just keeps going.

## WHAT WE NEED

1. **More lines.** We are drastically underproducing. We need a large volume of usable lines across different topics — product specs, care instructions, warranty language, FAQ, customer reviews, Brad quotes, fine print, warnings, packaging copy.

2. **Different topics.** The weapon-spec lane produced the most maybes but we've been stuck there. Need lines from other angles: shipping, returns, compatibility, materials, origin/manufacturing, customer service responses, comparison to competitors, product awards, certifications.

3. **Lines that follow the engines above.** Every line must be a REAL product statement first. Violence lives in double meanings or as one unremarked item in a normal list. The copy never knows. No winking. No buttons. No stated violence.

4. **Refinements of the "needs refinement" lines.** Pen/sword, fingerprints, and testing disclaimer all need their weak halves strengthened without losing what works about them.

## WHAT NOT TO DO

- Do NOT generate lines where the copy is aware of its own joke
- Do NOT add buttons, tags, or reactions after the punchline
- Do NOT use Kevin as a punchline — Kevin should appear as just another item in a list, unremarked upon
- Do NOT write office observational humor ("meetings suck")
- Do NOT leave the office/prison world (no military, government, international law references)
- Do NOT repeat structural tricks — vary the format and topic of each line
- Do NOT explain the joke in any way — no "if you know what we mean," no "technically," no parenthetical that points at the humor
- Do NOT lead with violence and pivot to product — lead with product and let violence show up uninvited
