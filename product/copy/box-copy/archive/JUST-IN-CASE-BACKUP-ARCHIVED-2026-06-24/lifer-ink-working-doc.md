### 4.5 Tagline

**STATUS: IN DEVELOPMENT** — No tagline locked yet. Eight rounds of development across two sessions. Requirements established through iterative feedback.

**Tagline Requirements (established June 19):**
1. Must be **product-specific** — explicitly about ink/tattoos/marks/skin/body art (like "The pen that ends sentences" is about a PEN)
2. Must have **subversion / brain pivot** — reader's brain does work (like "ends sentences / extend yours")
3. Must **flow smoothly** — easy to read, say, hear aloud
4. **Wordplay / puns / double meanings encouraged** — EXCEPTION to general "no puns" rule
5. Must go **absurd** — not descriptive, not predictable
6. Does NOT burn **one-use jokes** — "3-5 business days" and "shower it off" are each single-use, save for strategic placement
7. Must talk **TO the buyer** (rule 4)
8. Punchline **lands last** (rule 1)
9. **WY voice** — warm, cheerful, conspiratorial

**Quality bar:** "The pen that ends sentences. May also extend yours." (Cubicle Shank SUB-1) and "Writes in black. Moonlights in red. Racks up PTO and a body count spread." (Cubicle Shank primary tagline)

**ACTIVE CANDIDATES:**

**TAG-R9.** `[MAYBE]` "Goes on like confidence. Comes off like evidence."
*"Goes on" = application / behaves like. "Comes off" = removes / appears to be. "Confidence" → "evidence" = the pivot. Product-specific (application/removal), smooth flow, two parallel clauses. User said "maybe" — leaning toward it for flow/smoothness but not fully sold. June 19 session.*

**TAG-12d.** `[KEEP]` "Probably not the second worst decision you'll make this week."
*User explicitly likes this but says it "doesn't feel like a tagline." Repositioned as non-tagline packaging copy — placement TBD. Strong line, wrong slot.*

**ATMOSPHERIC LINE (not a tagline — placement TBD):**

**ATM-1.** `[KEEP — PLACEMENT TBD]` "Skin in the game. Blood in the water. Soap in the shower. Employee of the quarter."
*Four beats, escalating: corporate jargon → danger → evidence disposal → ... corporate award. "Quarter" rhymes with "water." The punchline IS the absurd deflation — all that darkness leads to a plaque on the wall. Too long for box front tagline. Best as blister card front headline, blister card back opener, or box back atmospheric header. June 19 session.*

**TAG-16.** `[NEEDS REWRITE]` "Look like you've been somewhere. Remove the evidence by Thursday."
*Evidence disposal concept is strong. Needs WY warmth injection — currently too cold/noir.*

### Tagline — Development History (Rounds 1-8, June 19)

**Round 1-2 (from pool, pre-session):** All existing taglines in the pool were descriptive, tame, and lacked subversion. User feedback: "it doesn't feel like any of these go absurd at all."

**Round 3 (subversion focus):** 10 candidates generated with explicit subversion mechanics. User feedback: some had potential but missed flow/wordplay requirements. "Skin in the game" identified as a strong phrase.

**Round 4 (building on "Skin in the game"):** 4-beat structure explored. "Skin in the game. Blood in the water. Soap in the shower. Gone by Monday." — User liked the flow of the first three beats but said "Gone by Monday" "doesn't flow at all and way too abruptly ends without a punchline." Led to "Employee of the quarter" as the fourth beat (rhymes with "water," absurd deflation).

**Round 5 (testing "Employee of the quarter"):** User agreed #8 was the winner but asked: "does it actually make sense as a tagline or is it just some random tangent?" Analysis concluded: strong line, NOT a tagline. Doesn't reference the product. Too long for box front. Repositioned as atmospheric copy for a larger surface.

**Round 6 (product-specific, dual-coherent wordplay):** 10 candidates generated with both product reading AND dark reading in every clause. User feedback: "too literal," "too descriptive or objective," "not nearly as funny as it could be." Missing the absurd lens.

**Round 7 (absurd lens — what tattoos DO to people):** 10 candidates exploring consequences, social disruption, behavioral shifts. User feedback: "I don't feel like many of these make sense because technically we're saying you have to do something to get these tattoos." Also: "it has to be about the product" — tagline must center the PRODUCT, not the person. The Cubicle Shank tagline makes the PEN the star.

**Round 8 (ink as character):** 10 candidates with the ink/tattoo as the subject with its own agenda. TAG-R9 "Goes on like confidence. Comes off like evidence." emerged as a soft maybe. User said: "I don't think it necessarily makes sense especially... well no, that actually does make sense." Decision: save as MAYBE, move on to other material. The tagline may reveal itself after the surrounding copy voice is more developed.

Visual concept: Professional headshots of suited people with visible prison-style tattoos, styled like employee-of-the-month. PHOTOS do the comedy.

**Design note:** The visual of Karen with teardrop + starburst face tattoos in a dead-serious professional headshot, and Dennis with "LIFER" knuckle tattoo visible in a thoughtful chin-rest pose — THAT is the joke.

**Full section as locked:**

> **OUR MOST DECORATED**
>
> **Karen Whitfield — Associate Account Manager (Formerly Assistant) — 3 Years**
> "I earned my first teardrop last week. I'd rather not say how. But I will say my cubicle neighbor's desk is available if anyone needs one. Anyway — hoping to earn a second one soon."

> **Dennis Rourke — Senior Operations Analyst — 14 Years (Consecutive)**
> "People think the tattoos are about expression. They're not. They're about efficiency. I tell every new hire the same thing: skip the icebreakers. Get the teardrop. It's an introduction, a boundary, and a conversation starter. Three birds. One tattoo."

**Character voice contrast (by design):**
- **Karen:** Bright, eager, enthusiastic. Three years in and already decorated. Treats the commendation program like the best thing that's happened to her at this company. Her cubicle neighbor disappeared and she's cheerful about it. The subversion is tone mismatch — warm delivery of dark content.
- **Dennis:** Strategic, measured, mentoring. Fourteen years taught him the tattoos are a workplace TOOL. He coaches new hires. He's optimized his entire workflow around face tattoos. The comedy is in his complete sincerity about prison tattoos as a productivity hack.

**Job title comedy layers:**
- Karen's "(Formerly Assistant)" — implies promotion after cubicle neighbor's disappearance. Reader connects the dots.
- Dennis's "(Consecutive)" — implies 14 straight years is unusual enough to note, like a prison sentence being served without parole.

**Development history (June 24):**
- Round 1: Name treatments and fine print. User: "not funny, no subversions, tame, just listing information."
- Round 2: Character quotes introduced. Karen and Dennis both sounded the same — dry, sardonic.
- Round 3: User feedback — "use different voices, different personalities, lean into unique people."
- Round 4: Karen as overachiever, Dennis as world-weary. User: "Dennis ones are too short, just not funny. Karen okay maybe with the first one."

## SECTION 13: KEY LEARNINGS FROM ALL SESSIONS

### From v01 assessment (June 11)
- v01 was rejected as part of a blanket rejection. No Lifer Ink-specific feedback. User assessment: at or near WY quality.
- "IF SWALLOWED, CONTACT YOUR SUPERVISOR" is one of the best lines in the entire kit.
- Reconcile "12 Commendations" with actual tattoo count (4-6 per spec).

### From copy development (June 16, 9 rounds)
1. **The setup IS the joke.** BRAD-21 shortened versions lost the comedy. Cutting setup kills the punchline.
2. **Descriptive is not funny.** Every line needs a reversal, subversion, or comedic technique.
3. **Downer energy kills it.** Brad should be SELLING temporariness as premium, not coping.
4. **Escalate into absurdity, not tame down.** CARE-5 kept going safe when the user wanted absurd.
5. **Brad quotes need clear comedic targets.** Story to wrong conclusion. Each sentence advances.
6. **"May contain traces of Kevin"** keeps regardless of which ingredient list wins.

### From WY voice re-evaluation (June 19)
7. **WY voice is warm, cheerful, conspiratorial — NOT dry/clinical/neutral.** The entire Lifer Ink pool was built in the wrong voice. Reference Shart Kit and Dehydrated Water as benchmarks.
8. **7 mechanical rules apply to every line.** (1) Joke lands last, (2) Setup is real format, (3) Punchline is concrete, (4) Talk TO buyer, (5) Be cheerful about bad things, (6) Never instruct threat, (7) Rule of three: two normal, third breaks with a real thing.
9. **Rule 6 matters for Shank crossovers.** Describe what happens cheerfully ("We're very proud of both products") instead of directing action.

### From WY voice sweep (June 19)
17. **Almost nothing passes.** 9 definite keeps, 5 borderline, ~8 rewritable concepts, 3 categories killed entirely. The pool needs wholesale new material in the correct voice.
18. **The biggest gap is peppiness.** The pool has zero Shart Kit energy — warm, chatty, building, excited-to-show-you-features. Everything was built in dry institutional Brad mode.
19. **Kill weak categories instead of forcing them.** Cross-sell, Gary QC, and Removal all killed after 2-3 rounds of no keeper.

### From tattoo design & achievement card session (June 22-23)
20. **"Laugh, not nod" is the tattoo filter.** A tattoo concept that gets recognition ("oh that's clever") fails. It must trigger an actual laugh at conversational distance on someone's skin. Subversion and absurdity are the primary humor engines.
21. **Image-first comedy.** The visual must be funny before any copy is read. If the concept needs explanation to land, it doesn't work as a tattoo. Concepts that describe a mood ("DAYS WITHOUT INCIDENT: 0") get a nod, not a laugh.
22. **Physical constraints kill concepts.** At 5-7cm, anything requiring legible text + multiple visual elements + recognition of a specific reference (e.g., "Hang In There" cat) has too many failure points. The strongest tattoos are simple images that read at a glance.
23. **Prison/office dual-language is the achievement copy engine.** Every locked achievement line uses words that simultaneously mean something in prison AND something in the office. The reader's brain does the flip without being told. Single-layer lines ("for losing someone who didn't die") don't work — they need both readings active at once.
24. **Setup lines stand alone. Follow-up lines dilute.** Every attempt to add 2-3 joke lines after a strong setup either explained the joke, repeated it, or got less funny. The format is: image + one line. Period.
25. **Taglines dilute achievement lines.** Short caption taglines attempted and rejected — same problem as follow-up lines. When the achievement line is doing its job, adding more copy weakens it.
26. **Don't recycle rejected concepts.** Multiple batches accidentally resurfaced concepts the user had already killed (ball and chain on chair, mouse in mousetrap, INMATE name badge). Track what's been cut and don't bring it back in different clothes.
27. **Stop grinding when stuck.** After 7+ batches of replacement tattoo concepts with diminishing returns, the right move was to ship with fewer tattoos rather than force weak concepts to fill a quota.
28. **One surface, one job.** Achievement card lines and packaging taglines were doing the same job on different surfaces — small tattoo image + short copy. Combining them into one surface (achievement card only) eliminated redundancy.

### From copy development (June 24)
29. **One-liners that don't build are not funny.** "Just like every other promise your company has made" — depressive, no levity, no playfulness. Comedy needs to build beats that escalate to a punchline, not land a single flat line.
30. **Characters need distinct voices.** Karen and Dennis must feel like different people, not the same sardonic narrator. Karen = bright, eager, evangelical. Dennis = strategic, measured, mentoring. The contrast IS the comedy.
31. **User direction unlocks breakthroughs.** Both Karen's quote (teardrop earned through cubicle neighbor's disappearance) and Dennis's angle (tattoos as workplace strategy) came from user suggestions after multiple failed AI-generated rounds. Listen harder.
32. **Multi-meaning words are gold.** "Decorated" does triple duty without being a pun. "On file / on your face" creates a flip. Find words that live in multiple worlds simultaneously.
33. **Short headers let visuals work.** "OUR MOST DECORATED" is four words. Every attempt to add a second half diluted it. When photos are doing the comedy, the header just needs to frame them.
34. **Build beats, don't land zingers.** The product description works because each sentence makes the next one worse: institutional → branding iron → Legal → grudging compromise. The comedy is in the accumulation, not in any single line.
35. **Kill categories that can't carry comedy.** Manufacturer info, like removal and Gary QC before it, doesn't have enough inherent comedy potential to justify forcing material into it. Straight text is fine.

### From tagline development (June 19, 8 rounds)
10. **Taglines must be product-specific.** The Cubicle Shank tagline is about THE PEN. Lifer Ink tagline must be about THE INK/TATTOO. Not about situations, consequences, or the person.
11. **"Literal" is not "absurd."** Describing what tattoos actually do (mark skin, wash off, look real) is literal. Finding an absurd LENS on what they do is the goal.
12. **The tagline may reveal itself after other copy is locked.** Sometimes the right tagline becomes obvious once the surrounding voice is nailed. Don't force it.
13. **One-use jokes must be tracked.** "3-5 business days" and "shower it off" are each single-use. Burning them on the tagline wastes them.
14. **Wordplay/puns ARE encouraged for taglines.** This is a specific EXCEPTION to the general "no puns" comedy-writing rule.
15. **Flow matters for taglines.** Must be smooth, easy to read/say/hear aloud.
16. **The ink needs to be the CHARACTER.** Like the pen has agency in Cubicle Shank taglines ("writes," "moonlights," "racks up"), the ink needs to be the star with its own personality.
