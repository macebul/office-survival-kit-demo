## SECTION 13: KEY LEARNINGS FROM SESSION (June 23)

### Comedy and Craft

1. **"Delivery system" repetition must be caught.** The v01 had "Morale Delivery System" in the rebrand AND "NERVE AGENT DELIVERY SYSTEM" in the contains line. Same phrase doing the same job on the same face. Scanning for repetition across elements on the same surface is part of the review process.

2. **Split compound lines to give each idea its own punchline.** The v01 contains line ("CONTAINS: 1 NERVE AGENT DELIVERY SYSTEM | 12 FL OZ CAPACITY") crammed two ideas together. Splitting into contains + capacity gave each line space to breathe and enabled the capacity line's three-part escalation (oz / shots / alibi).

3. **Visual solutions beat copy solutions when copy can't carry the weight.** The front face bottom area went through multiple text-only attempts. None were strong enough. The booking photo visual of the mug being processed through intake carried more comedy than any text line could.

4. **Don't add content below a strong closer.** Side B's effects list ends on "A following" -- the mug creates a movement. The v01 had two additional sections below it ("IF SYMPTOMS PERSIST" and "RECOMMENDED DOSAGE") that stepped on the punchline. Cut them.

5. **Consolidate for density, not for brevity.** The back face went from 8 timestamps to 5, but the 5 are individually stronger. Fewer lines that each carry comedy > more lines where some are filler.

6. **The stapler thread works because it escalates through institutional understatement.** "Chased someone with a stapler" is reported with the same energy as "Quiet." Then the second stapler reveal (the subject PLANNED for confiscation) is the turn. The comedy is in the report's refusal to change tone while the content goes sideways.

7. **"Allegedly" does maximum work with minimum letters.** One word transforms "Chased someone with a stapler" from a statement of fact into institutional plausible deniability. The report is covering itself.

8. **Three-part escalation is this item's core mechanic.** Subtitle (drink / composure / court), capacity (oz / shots / alibi), and the effects list all use the same pattern: mundane > plausible > absurd, without the format acknowledging the escalation.

### V01 Assessment

9. **Much of v01 was already strong.** Top face (lineup), bottom face (holding the box), mug front (height chart), mug back (intake note), Brad's quote, result block, and effects list were all kept as-is. The session focused development on the surfaces that needed work: front face naming/structure, back face density, and Side B pacing.

10. **Mug Shot had the highest v01 quality across all items.** Several surfaces needed zero changes. The session was more about refining strong material than rescuing weak material -- opposite of the Lifer Ink experience where the pool needed wholesale voice injection.

### Process

11. **"Verbal" isn't a timestamp entry.** The word alone ("0630 -- Verbal.") doesn't paint a picture. Compare to "Chased someone with a stapler. Allegedly." -- the reader can SEE the scene. If a timestamp line isn't visual, cut it.

12. **Chair vs. stapler specificity.** "Seen leaving the building with a chair" (v01) is funny but generic -- you can imagine it but it doesn't ESCALATE. "Chased someone with a stapler" + "Second stapler produced" creates a two-beat thread where the second beat reveals premeditation. Threading beats across timestamps > standalone observations.

## SECTION 14: DESIGN PRINCIPLES (established this session)

1. **The mug is the character.** It gets booked, processed, identified from a lineup, assessed at intake. The mug has a criminal record.
2. **The Field Report format carries the back face.** The institutional report format means the comedy is in WHAT gets reported, not HOW it's reported. The format never cracks.
3. **Icons replace cut sections.** When text sections are removed (Side B's lower sections), paired icons per list item fill the space and add visual interest without stepping on punchlines.
4. **"Kept as-is" is a valid session outcome.** Multiple surfaces were already at quality and didn't need development time. Knowing when to stop is part of the craft.
