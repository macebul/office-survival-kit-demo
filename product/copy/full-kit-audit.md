## Voice Calibration Rules (Reference)

These 10 rules were established during the Cell Talk v2 review session and serve as the evaluation criteria for every surface in this audit:

1. **Format first** — pick a real format, fill with escalating content, format never breaks
2. **Straight face always** — no winking, no exclamation points
3. **Escalation structure** — plausible → edge → joke → absurd (within the format)
4. **Warm underneath** — sardonic, not cynical. Validating, not mean.
5. **Prison vocabulary as water, not costume** — dual-meaning words used naturally, never bolted on
6. **Specificity over generality** — precise numbers, named characters
7. **Short** — small boxes, readable font, every word earns its space
8. **Don't repeat the same format on the same surface across different boxes** — differentiation required
9. **Each box has its own personality** — wellness/enforcement/elegant/territorial/ceremonial/tactical
10. **Don't sabotage product copy to make Brad look dumber** — the BETTER the product copy, the funnier his self-promotion becomes

## 1A. RAP SHEETS — Box Copy v1 Audit

**Overall Grade: B+**
**Personality: Enforcement / Internal Affairs**
**Division: Interpersonal Accountability Division**

### What's Working

The enforcement register is strong and distinct from Cell Talk's wellness world. ### Surface-by-Surface Issues

---

#### FRONT FACE — Issues Flagged

**RS-5 (Tagline): "Structured Accountability Cards for the Modern Workplace"**
- **Problem (Rule 7 — Short):** This is 8 words of corporate filler that doesn't earn a laugh. Compare to Cell Talk's tagline: "Finally, a reason to talk to yourself at work." — that's warm, specific, and lands immediately.
- **Problem (Rule 3 — Escalation):** The tagline occupies prime real estate but doesn't escalate anything. It reads as genuine product copy with no comedy payload.
- **Proposed revision:**
  > *Because "let it go" wasn't working.*
- **Why:** Shorter (6 words). Warm — validates the frustration. The prison vocabulary ("let it go" = what people say to you when you complain, but also resonates with incarceration concepts of release). It sets up the product as the alternative to suppressing your feelings. The period makes it definitive.

**RS-6 (Card Count): "Contains 15 Documented Infractions · Classified: INTERDEPARTMENTAL"**
- **Problem (Rule 7):** "Classified: INTERDEPARTMENTAL" is a Brad flourish that doesn't land hard enough for the space it takes. The word "INTERDEPARTMENTAL" is 18 characters of setup for a joke that's "Brad invented a security classification."
- **Proposed revision:**
  > Contains 15 Documented Infractions · Filed: Chronologically and Emotionally
- **Why:** "Chronologically and Emotionally" is the kind of unnecessary precision Brad would add. It's funnier because it implies Brad organized his grievances by feeling, not just by date.

**RS-7 (Authorization Seal): "AUTHORIZED FOR INTERDEPARTMENTAL USE"**
- **Problem (Rule 8 — Don't repeat formats):** This seal is structurally identical to Cell Talk's "EXCEEDS CLINICAL EXPECTATIONS" seal — same circular format, same sub-brand, same placement. The seal concept is right, but the copy needs to differentiate harder.
- **Problem:** "INTERDEPARTMENTAL" appears on both the seal AND the card count line (RS-6). Repetition on the same surface.
- **Proposed revision:**
  > EVIDENCE GRADE
  > Interpersonal Accountability Division
  > Elevate Co. · Est. 2024
- **Why:** "Evidence Grade" is funnier than "Authorized for Interdepartmental Use." It implies the cards have been rated for evidentiary quality — as if you could submit them in a workplace tribunal. It's specific to the enforcement personality and doesn't overlap with Cell Talk's clinical language.

**RS-8 (Fine Print): Full disclaimer paragraph**
- **Problem (Rule 7):** The fine print is 2+ lines of dense text. Compare to Cell Talk's fine print: "Designed by someone who has never once doubted himself." — one sentence, one joke, clean exit.
- **Problem (Rule 10):** The current fine print undercuts the product by admitting the cards don't do anything ("does not constitute legal action, HR intervention, or actual accountability"). This violates Rule 10 — Brad's product copy should be competent. The disclaimer should be funny without making the product sound useless.
- **Proposed revision:**
  > Results not guaranteed. Grudges, however, are permanent.
- **Why:** Short (9 words). Doesn't undercut the product. "Grudges are permanent" is warm and true — everyone relates. The contrast between results (not guaranteed) and grudges (permanent) is the joke.
