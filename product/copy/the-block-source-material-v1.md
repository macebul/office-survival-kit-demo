## RAW LOCATION COPY (OLDER VERSION)

> **Voice note:** This copy was written for a different version. The voice is grittier and more first-person survival guide than ELEVATE's polished editorial. The jokes and details are usable — the voice needs rewriting.

### The Map (Intro)

You'll need a map. GPS stopped working here years ago. They say it was voluntary. Official versions stop at HR — for "safety reasons." This one doesn't.

Keep it folded. Don't let them see you tracing routes. Knowing your exits counts as treason here.

---

### The Grid

The Grid is where the cubicles live. If you've ever waved to the wrong person, welcome home.

Every desk is identical, which makes losing your mind incredibly efficient. They say one used to be different. HR dealt with it.

**Rules:**
- Rule #1: Keep moving. Standing still means you're "available," and the hunters switch to thermal.
- Rule #2: Never make eye contact with a coworker who says "Got a sec?" Last guy who did now runs the newsletter.
- Rule #3: If your computer freezes, freeze with it. HR can't fire what looks like performance art.

At 3 PM, the lights flicker to remind everyone they're still alive. Celebrate responsibly.

---

### The Canteen

Smells like every lunch you've ever regretted — and at least one that isn't yours.

It's the only place they let you take off your lanyard — so naturally, it's where all the crimes happen.

**Rules:**
- Rule #4: The Fridge is Neutral Ground. Violating it starts wars that last fiscal years. If you find a Tupperware older than your promotion, salute it and back away.
- Rule #5: Coffee is Contraband. It arrives in unlabeled thermoses through shadow channels. Trust no one who drinks decaf — they're clearly wired on something else.
- Rule #6: Microwave Justice is swift and arbitrary. One whiff of burnt popcorn and Facilities calls for sanctions.

At 12:01 the stampede begins. Accounting moves in formation. Marketing flanks from the left — then calls it a brand activation.

Don't drop your fork — it marks you as a casualty.

When the dust settles, someone's lunch is always left in the fridge. That's how legends form.

---

### The Panic Pods

Your last safe space. Unless someone jiggles the handle.

Here, time moves differently. A minute inside equals ten in The Grid. It's called "bathroom math."

- Rule #14: Always bring your phone. Without it, you're just a person alone with their employment choices.

Flush before you cry. It helps cover the sound.

The stall graffiti tells the real company history. "Steve was here." "Steve still is."

When you emerge, don't make eye contact with anyone waiting. They know. And so do you.

---

### The Commissary

It's not a room. It's a marketplace. You don't take supplies here. You earn visitation rights.

Paperclips trade at two for one. Printer ink? Priceless. Post-its? Currency of the desperate.

No one controls it, but everyone owes someone.

There's a guy named Cliff who claims to have scissors. No one's seen them, but everyone believes.

The lucky leave with pens. The brave leave with both hands.

- Rule #17: Never ask for a stapler by name. It's how they mark you for collection.

---

### Internal Affairs (HR)

That's what we call HR — just never to their faces.

It's quiet here. The HR Rep lights a candle labeled "Boundaries." The sound machine responds with "Gentle Panic."

- Rule #7: Enter only when summoned. They can smell guilt, and fear voids your warranty.
- Rule #8: Never start a sentence with "To be honest." They already know.
- Rule #9: Sign nothing. Even eye contact counts.

The HR Rep will smile. Don't smile back — it counts as consent.

They'll hand you a stress ball, a tissue, and a pen that only writes apologies. According to the survivors.

Speak softly, confess strategically, and for the love of your paycheck — don't joke. They write those down too.

When it's over, they'll thank you for "sharing." You'll thank them for "listening." The cameras will thank you for your cooperation.

Leave a mint on the desk. It's tradition.

### The Kennel (Sales — reassigned from Marketing)

Home of the Yard Dogs. The loudest part of The Block — and the easiest to smell. Energy drinks, desperation, and something burning.

Phones never stop ringing here. No one remembers who started the calls, only that they can't end until someone bleeds quota.

- Rule #29: Keep your head down and your badge visible. They respect hierarchy, not weakness.
- Rule #30: Never enter the circle. They'll sense the quiet on you.

Every few minutes, a cheer erupts from nowhere — a primal chorus of "LEADS! FEEDS! NO ONE LEAVES!" It echoes through the vents, setting off the motion sensors again.

Their desks form a circle, like an ancient ritual ground. At the center: a trophy rack built from shattered headsets — some even signed.

Crossing The Kennel takes patience, silence, and a spare coffee as tribute.

If you survive, you'll emerge into silence — and that's how you'll know you made it out.

---

### The Network (IT)

No one's really invited, but everyone ends up here eventually.

It hums. It flickers. No one's sure if that's the equipment or the people.

Empty energy-drink cans form small defensive walls. The air tastes like stale pizza and unpatched updates.

If something stops working, it's already on the list. No one's seen the list. They say it keeps adding names on its own.

Every problem that enters The Network arrives "in progress." Sometimes they close a ticket just to see what happens.

There's a shrine made of dead mice and retired keyboards. Someone once lit a candle there — and the Wi-Fi improved.

- Rule #31: Never ask when the system will be back up. That's how they choose their sacrifices.
- Rule #32: If a tech sighs, back away slowly. You've triggered a prophecy.

Some say the vending machine is part of the network now. Others say it runs the company. No one's been brave enough to unplug it.

---

### The Vault (Accounting / Finance)

Where numbers rule, and compassion has been reclassified as a liability.

It's colder here. They say that's how the budget stays frozen.

The accountants work in near silence — every click of a key, another offering to balance.

Every spreadsheet is a living document. Every receipt, a souvenir from a battle already lost. The brave call it record-keeping. The smart call it survival.

- Rule #41: Never ask what "miscellaneous" means. That's where they bury the bodies.

Some say The Vault predates the company. Others say it IS the company.

If you find a missing penny, don't touch it. That's bait. The last guy who picked one up was "volunteered" for a full audit.

When the lights flicker, it's not the power. It's The Vault adjusting for inflation.

---

### The Underbelly (Facilities)

Facilities keeps the building standing through a delicate mix of guesswork and denial.

- Rule #50: Their motto is "If it ain't broke, give it a minute."
- Rule #51: If you see a wet floor sign, that means Facilities is hunting nearby.

Their closet runs on superstition, not inventory. A single roll of duct tape sits on the top shelf, next to a sign that says "In Case of Everything."

They polish floors so slick, you can watch yourself fall in real time. Then they send it to HR as proof of "engagement."

Years ago, they fixed a ceiling leak using a plunger, three binder clips, and whatever was left of someone's lunch. It's still there — now considered a structural feature.

There's a guy named Earl who's been here longer than the building. He doesn't talk much, but he nods when he sees you.

Last month, Earl stopped nodding at Patterson. We don't talk about Patterson anymore.

---

### Disputed Territory (Printer / Common Space)

The floor plan labels this zone 'Common Space.' The incident reports suggest otherwise.

They said the new copier was 'high-fidelity.' They didn't say what it was copying. Ask the Chens. All four of them.

Last quarter, Martinez taped a sign to the copier that said "Reserved." The sign lasted eleven minutes. Martinez lasted three more weeks.

Delgado controls the thermostat. Delgado also sells blankets from his trunk. Unrelated.

The water changes flavor every quarter. So does our life insurance policy. Best not to connect those.

- Rule #55: What's said at the water cooler didn't happen. What's overheard at the water cooler definitely did.

A mug has lived in the sink since Q3. No one claims it. No one moves it. At this point, it's load-bearing.

No one remembers how it started. Everyone remembers how it ended. Been here longer than the carpets smell. The carpet's union.

---

### The Coliseum (Performance Reviews)

Every quarter, they gather us here. They call it 'performance review.' The Romans had a word for it too.

The ladder in the center isn't metaphorical. Neither is the fall.

Gary does play-by-play now. No one asked him to. No one's stopped him either.

They don't tell you who you're up against. They just move you to adjacent cells. Dress code says business casual. The mouth guards say otherwise.

Everyone gets entrance music now. IT set it up as a joke. Peterson chose 'Jailbreak.' Peterson doesn't work here anymore.

They pushed everything into a circle last quarter. Chairs. Cabinets. All of it. 'Collaboration,' they said. The sand was harder to explain.

Staplers aren't allowed anymore. Not since the incident. Letter openers either. Or Nguyen.

Alliances form the week before. They dissolve the week after. The ones that don't are called departments. At least gangs don't require two weeks' notice.

---

### Visitation Rooms (Conference / Meeting Rooms)

This is where the outside world visits. They don't stay long. Neither would you.

- Rule #58: Book the room with windows. Visitors need to see daylight exists. It helps with the questions.
- Rule #59: Never take a call in the glass room. Everyone sees your mouth move. No one hears the lie.

Visitors wave through the glass when they leave. Some employees wave back. The ones who've been here longest press their hand to it.

The rooms have official names. 4A. 4B. Conference Suite. We use different ones. 'Where Marketing Went.' 'Greg's Last Stand.' 'No Witnesses.'

Vendors bring donuts. It's not kindness. It's how you smuggle goodwill past procurement.

Family visited once. Saw the cubicle. The lighting. The mug I've had for six years. They don't ask how work is going anymore.

Some meetings end with handshakes. Some end with badge collection. The room doesn't judge. It's seen it all.

---

### The Lot (Parking Lot)

Facilities found a tunnel last March. Started under the Commissary. Ended at row 12. They filled it in. Another one started in June.

There's a Corolla in row 7 that hasn't moved since 2019. Badge still scans every morning. No one's seen the driver in years.

The Brass park by the door. The rest of us park by the exit. We call it a head start.

The average employee sits in their car four minutes before clocking in. The record is three hours. Dunn still made his 9 AM meeting. Never said what he decided in there.

The Lot has seen promotions. Terminations. Two weddings. One exorcism. Facilities says the stain is oil.

The back row touches the fence. Beyond that, the highway. Some days you can hear it. Some days that's enough.

Kim quit in The Lot. Didn't go inside. Sent the email from her car. We watched from the windows. Some say she's still out there. Living.

Park facing out. Tank full. Body double warming the seat. Some call it paranoia. The fence isn't for keeping people out.
