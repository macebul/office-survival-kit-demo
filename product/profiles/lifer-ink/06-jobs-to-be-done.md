# Artifact 6: Jobs To Be Done

## Primary JTBD

**When** I discover the tattoo sheet and achievement card inside the Lifer Ink box
**I want to** immediately find a design funny enough to apply on the spot
**So that** I get to be the person at the office wearing a hilarious temporary tattoo and fielding questions about it all day

## Supporting JTBD

**Job 2 - Give Me a Reason to Apply**
- When I see a sheet of temporary tattoos, I want the achievement card to frame application as something I've earned, so that applying a tattoo feels like a reward rather than a random impulse

**Job 3 - Make Each Tattoo Stand Alone**
- When a coworker sees my tattoo and doesn't know about the kit, I want the design and copy to be funny on their own, so that the joke lands without explanation

**Job 4 - Give Me Something to Share**
- When I apply a tattoo, I want it to be photogenic and captionable, so that I can share the moment digitally and generate reactions beyond the room

**Job 5 - Make the Box Funny Before I Open It**
- When I pick up the Lifer Ink box in the tray, I want the exterior to be entertaining on its own, so that the box experience is a comedy piece even if I never apply a tattoo

**Job 6 - Let Me Choose My Commitment Level**
- When I'm deciding which tattoo to apply, I want a range of sizes and visibility levels, so that I can go bold (forearm, hand) or subtle (wrist, behind the ear) based on my comfort

## Underserved Jobs (How Competitor Temporary Tattoos Fail)

- The "adult context" job - most temporary tattoos are designed for children or party favors. No premium packaging, no character voice, no comedy framing that makes adults feel like they're participating in something clever
- The "reason to apply" job - no competitor temporary tattoo product includes an achievement mechanic. You get a sheet, you look at it, you maybe apply one, and the sheet goes in a drawer
- The "standalone humor" job - most novelty tattoos require explanation ("it's from this kit I got..."). These should be funny on sight to anyone who sees them
- The "earned identity" job - prison tattoos mean something because they're earned. No competitor product borrows this mechanic to make temporary tattoos feel like accomplishments
