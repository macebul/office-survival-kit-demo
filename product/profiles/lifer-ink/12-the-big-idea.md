# Artifact 12: The Big Idea

**Lifer Ink isn't temporary tattoos. It's a commendation system for office survival.**

Every prison tattoo tells a story - time served, things survived, status earned. Lifer Ink takes that exact mechanic and applies it to the workplace. The tattoos aren't decoration. They're documentation. Each one represents a specific corporate scenario the recipient has endured, and the achievement card tells them exactly which commendation they've earned.

The comedy isn't "look, funny tattoos." The comedy is Brad Sterling treating temporary face tattoos with the same institutional seriousness as a Distinguished Service Medal. The cream-and-gold box, the Dermal Achievement Division branding, the commendation requirements card - every element sells the fiction that this is a legitimate professional recognition program. And it works because Brad believes it completely.

If the tattoos are just novelty shop filler, nobody applies them. If they're genuinely funny earned commendations backed by a game mechanic, they go on skin and start conversations.

**Implication for all content creation**
- Never position Lifer Ink as "comes with temporary tattoos." Position it as "comes with a commendation system you earn through office survival"
- The achievement card should be as visible in listing images as the tattoo sheet itself - it's what reframes the product
- Tattoo designs must be funny enough to motivate application. An unapplied tattoo is a failed product. Every design needs to pass the "would someone actually put this on at work?" test
- The box copy should be visible in at least one listing image because the "IF SWALLOWED" line and the premium packaging are doing heavy comedy and quality-signaling work simultaneously
