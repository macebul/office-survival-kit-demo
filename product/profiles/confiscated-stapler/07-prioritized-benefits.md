# Artifact 7: Prioritized Benefits

Ranked by impact on the recipient's experience and the kit's perceived design quality.

**Tier 1 - Must Communicate (drives immediate reaction and design perception)**
1. The empty recess is an intentional comedy beat, not a missing item - the chalk outline makes this clear in under 2 seconds
2. The deadpan institutional label ("CONFISCATED - WEAPONIZED OFFICE EQUIPMENT. INCIDENT #147.") escalates the visual gag into narrative comedy
3. It's the most retellable single detail in the kit - "there was a chalk outline where a stapler USED TO BE" sells the concept in one sentence
4. It costs zero dollars to produce but generates one of the biggest laughs in the entire unboxing

**Tier 2 - Should Communicate (builds narrative depth and multi-character comedy)**
5. Three separate characters reference the stapler independently, creating a conspiracy of silence that's funnier than any explanation
6. Brad's corporate spin ("The Minimalist Desk Sculpture(TM)") demonstrates his character voice at its most absurd
7. The OG's refusal to discuss it ("They took it. I don't want to talk about it.") is the emotional counterweight to Brad's corporate denial
8. "INCIDENT #147" implies 146 previous incidents, creating a backstory that the recipient fills in themselves

**Tier 3 - Nice to Communicate (rewards deep engagement)**
9. Gary's exterior box note plants a seed before the kit is opened that pays off when the recess is found
10. The Activity Guide dedicates two pages to the stapler's backstory and "The Moment of Silence" / "The Shrine" activities
11. The stapler shape echoes the Red Stapler from Office Space in the cultural subconscious without making an explicit reference
12. The empty recess is the single best proof that every element of the kit was designed, including the things that aren't there
