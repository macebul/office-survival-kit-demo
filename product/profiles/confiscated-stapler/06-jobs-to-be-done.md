# Artifact 6: Jobs To Be Done

## Primary JTBD

**When** I open Tray 2 and discover an empty recess with a chalk outline of a stapler
**I want to** immediately understand that the absence is the joke
**So that** I feel like the kit's designers thought of everything - even the things they didn't include

## Supporting JTBD

**Job 2 - Break My Pattern Expectations**
- When I've been pulling items out of recesses for 5+ minutes and every slot has contained something, I want one slot to be empty, so that the kit surprises me in a way I didn't see coming

**Job 3 - Give Me a Story to Tell**
- When I'm describing the kit to someone who hasn't seen it, I want one detail so vivid and absurd that it sells the entire product in a single sentence, so that "there's a chalk outline where a stapler used to be" becomes the pitch

**Job 4 - Reward My Attention to Detail**
- When I read Gary's note on the exterior box ("I don't want to talk about the stapler"), I want to eventually find the crime scene that Gary was referencing, so that paying attention to small details feels worthwhile

**Job 5 - Make Me Believe There's a Backstory**
- When I read "INCIDENT #147," I want to feel like something genuinely happened, so that I'm curious enough to look for more information in the Activity Guide

**Job 6 - Show Me the Designers Were Smart**
- When I'm forming my overall opinion of the kit's design quality, I want the empty recess to demonstrate that even the negative space was deliberate, so that I trust the quality of everything else I've seen

## Underserved Jobs (How Competitor Gag Kits Handle Empty Space)

- The "intentional absence" job - no competitor gag gift kit uses empty space as comedy. Empty space is always a manufacturing gap or cost-cutting evidence, never a deliberate joke
- The "multi-character conspiracy" job - no competitor has multiple fictional characters referencing the same missing item, creating a sense of shared institutional history
- The "crime scene visual" job - nobody puts a chalk outline in consumer product packaging. The visual language of police investigation applied to an office supply is unique
- The "zero-COGS comedy" job - competitors add more physical items to justify price. Nobody demonstrates that the best joke in the kit can be the one thing that isn't there
