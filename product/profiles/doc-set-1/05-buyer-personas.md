# Artifact 5: Buyer Personas

Note: Doc Set 1 is not sold individually. These personas describe how each buyer type interacts with this specific item as part of the kit evaluation, gift-giving moment, and unboxing experience.

## Persona 1 - "The Experience Builder"

**Who they are**
- 28-45, any gender
- Gives gifts that feel like events, not transactions
- Curates the moment: wrapping, timing, presentation all matter
- Has given experience gifts before (escape rooms, tasting boxes, subscription kits)

*Purchase trigger*
- "This isn't just a box of stuff - there's a whole narrative arc in here"

*Deepest fear*
- The recipient rips through everything in 30 seconds and misses the layers

**How Doc Set 1 wins them**
- The booklet forces a paced unboxing
- It creates the slow-build experience this buyer is looking for
- They can say "read the booklet first" and know the recipient will be entertained for minutes before touching an item box
- The three-act structure gives the gift a beginning, middle, and end

---

## Persona 2 - "The Comedy Connoisseur"

**Who they are**
- 25-50, any gender
- Has strong opinions about what's actually funny
- Appreciates writing craft, not just premise
- Can tell the difference between a one-liner product and sustained comedic voice

*Purchase trigger*
- "The writing is actually good - this isn't just puns on a box"

*Deepest fear*
- The humor is surface-level and doesn't hold up past the first flip-through

**How Doc Set 1 wins them**
- Brad's voice is sustained, layered, and consistent across all 16 pages
- The comedy operates on multiple levels: the office-as-prison vocabulary, Brad's outsider perspective, the format subversions (loadout diagram, decoder card, legal fine print)
- Jokes reward re-reading (the cubicle vs. death row math, the carpet's union, the patent that was claimed but not filed)
- This buyer will read the fine print on page 16 and feel rewarded

---

## Persona 3 - "The Flip-Through Tester"

**Who they are**
- 30-55, any gender
- Evaluates physical products by handling them
- In a retail setting, picks things up, flips through, puts them back
- Online, zooms into every listing image and reads every review that mentions quality

*Purchase trigger*
- "Every page I flipped to had something on it - not just filler"

*Deepest fear*
- The first two pages are great and the rest is padding to hit a page count

**How Doc Set 1 wins them**
- No page is filler
- Every spread has unique content, unique visual layout, and a distinct comedic beat
- The format changes keep the flip-through interesting (narrative pages, loadout diagram, decoder card, portrait spread, fine print)
- If this buyer could flip through it in a store, they would buy the kit on page 3 ("A cubicle is 6x8. Death row is 6x9. Enjoy the upgrade.")

---

## Persona 4 - "The Detail Hoarder"

**Who they are**
- 25-45, any gender
- Finds easter eggs in movies, reads liner notes, checks footnotes
- Loves products that reveal more the closer you look
- Will spend 10 minutes on a single page if there's enough to find

*Purchase trigger*
- "Wait, even the legal fine print on the back is funny"

*Deepest fear*
- The product only works at surface level and there's nothing to discover on a second pass

**How Doc Set 1 wins them**
- The booklet has discovery layers at every depth
- Surface level: the main copy and vignettes
- Second pass: the visual details in the illustrations (cubicle dimensions, union card, barcode tag)
- Deep read: the fine print (patents claimed but not filed, intern compensated in exposure, ambitions settling during employment)
- The booklet also plants references that only pay off later when specific items are found
- This buyer will connect "I left a pen in Conference Room B" to the Cubicle Shank box and feel like they cracked a code
