# Artifact 7: Prioritized Benefits

Ranked by impact on unboxing experience, kit perception, and purchase confidence.

**Tier 1 - Must Communicate (drives unboxing engagement and kit-level value perception)**

1. The booklet is a narrative experience that makes the reader want to explore the kit, not an instruction manual or product catalog
2. It is the first thing seen when the briefcase opens, and it controls the pacing and emotional trajectory of the entire unboxing
3. The three-act structure builds from "ha, that's my life" through escalating comedy to "now open the kit," targeting 90%+ unboxing completion
4. Premium production (gold foil cover, editorial illustrations, matte stock, three-family typography) signals that the entire kit is high quality before a single item box is touched

**Tier 2 - Should Communicate (builds depth, shareability, and narrative integration)**

5. The booklet references specific kit items through story rather than naming them, creating curiosity gaps that only close when the items are discovered
6. Brad's voice is sustained across all 16 pages including the legal fine print, with no character breaks or fourth-wall winks
7. Individual pages work as standalone shareable images while the full narrative makes people describe the entire booklet to others
8. The comedy operates at multiple depths: surface copy, visual details in illustrations, format subversions, and hidden fine print jokes

**Tier 3 - Nice to Communicate (differentiates for detail-oriented buyers and recipients)**

9. The office-as-prison vocabulary established in the booklet echoes across every item box in the kit, creating a unified world
10. Format subversions (tactical loadout diagram, decoder card, legal disclaimer comedy) keep the reading experience unpredictable page to page
11. The tonal flip on the final spread - from dark comedy to aspirational hype - is a genuine surprise that converts entertainment into unboxing motivation
12. The back cover fine print is the densest comedy in the booklet and the content most likely to be read aloud to someone else
