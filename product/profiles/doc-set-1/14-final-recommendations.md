# Artifact 14: Final Recommendations

---

## 1. Lock the narrative copy before starting illustration generation

- The copy document defines what happens on every page, and illustrations must serve the narrative, not the reverse
- Changing copy after illustrations are generated creates expensive rework or forces visual-narrative mismatches
- The three-act structure, format subversions, and indirect item references should all be final before any image prompts are written
- Copy lock should include the fine print - it is the most quoted section and must be reviewed for comedy timing at final length

---

## 2. Test a physical mockup at actual print size before committing to production

- Digital layouts cannot replicate paper stock feel, foil reflectivity, or ink density on cream interior pages
- Saddle-stitch binding at 16 pages has specific spine behavior that affects how the booklet lays open - test this with the intended paper weight
- The cover's matte black surface will show fingerprints differently depending on coating - a physical sample reveals this immediately
- A mockup also tests whether the booklet feels substantial enough to signal "this is content" or thin enough to signal "this is an insert"

---

## 3. Prioritize pages 2-3 as the hook that earns the remaining 14 pages

- If pages 2-3 do not land, no one flips further - the entire narrative investment collapses at the threshold
- These pages must establish Brad's voice, the world of Elevate Co., and the satirical tone in under 30 seconds of reading time
- The opening spread should reward the recipient's decision to pause and read rather than skip to the items
- Test these pages in isolation with people unfamiliar with the product - if they laugh or want to keep reading, the hook works

---

## 4. Design the loadout spread (pages 8-9) as a standalone shareable image

- The tactical loadout diagram is the most visually distinctive format subversion in the booklet and the most likely to be photographed and shared
- It should be designed to work at two scales: full-spread inside the booklet and cropped-to-phone-screen for social sharing
- Labels, callouts, and illustration elements must be legible in a photograph taken from arm's length under normal indoor lighting
- This spread has potential as a standalone social content asset for listing images, social media, and influencer kits

---

## 5. Test fine print readability at 7pt on the actual paper stock

- The back cover fine print is the highest-density comedy in the booklet, but only if people can read it
- 7pt type on cream or white stock is legible under controlled conditions but may fail on textured matte stock or under warm lighting
- Print a test sheet at final size on the intended stock and read it in the conditions the recipient will encounter (living room, office, bar)
- If readability fails, increase the point size before cutting content - every line of fine print earns its space

---

## 6. Treat individual booklet pages as a social content library

- The format variety (performance review, loadout, decoder, portrait, fine print) means each spread can serve as a distinct piece of content
- Listing image galleries should include 2-3 interior spread photos that showcase different formats
- Social content planning should map specific pages to specific content moments (launch tease, product deep-dive, humor highlight, unboxing clip)
- Each format subversion is a potential standalone post - the booklet is not one content asset, it is six or seven bound together

---

## 7. Confirm gold foil COGS before committing to the cover specification

- Gold foil on matte black is the cover treatment that signals premium production quality
- Get a quoted price from at least two print partners at the expected order volume before locking the spec
- If foil COGS exceeds the booklet's allocated budget, evaluate alternatives (spot UV, debossing, metallic ink) against the same "this is real, not filler" signal requirement
- Do not downgrade the cover finish without understanding the downstream effect on the filler-perception objection - the cover is the booklet's first and most important argument that it is worth reading
