# Artifact 6: Jobs To Be Done

## Primary JTBD

**When** I open the briefcase and find a booklet resting on top of everything
**I want to** read something entertaining enough to hold my attention before I dig into the items below
**So that** the unboxing feels like an experience with a beginning, not just a box I ripped open

## Supporting Jobs

**Job 2 - Set the World Before I Enter It**
- When I start reading the booklet, I want to understand the tone, the vocabulary, and the character behind this kit, so that every item I discover afterward feels connected to something larger

**Job 3 - Make Me Feel Seen**
- When I read about Day 1 intake, the cubicle, the break room, and the survival instincts, I want to recognize my own office life on the page, so that the comedy feels personal rather than generic

**Job 4 - Build Anticipation for What's Below**
- When the narrative references a pen in Conference Room B, earned ink, and decoded office speak, I want to wonder what those items actually look like, so that I'm motivated to explore the full kit

**Job 5 - Give the Gift-Giver a Win**
- When the recipient reads the booklet and starts laughing before they've opened a single item box, the gift-giver wants that reaction to validate their choice, so that the gift feels like it's landing

**Job 6 - Reward the Close Reader**
- When I flip to the back cover and find dense legal fine print, I want to discover escalating jokes hidden in the format, so that the booklet rewards my attention at every depth

**Job 7 - Make Me Want to Show Someone**
- When I finish a page that makes me laugh, I want to hold it up or photograph it or read it aloud, so that the comedy becomes social rather than solitary

## Underserved Jobs (How Competitor Printed Materials Fail)

- **The "real publication" job:**
  - Most printed inserts in gift products look and feel like inserts
  - Cheap paper, clip art, one-note humor
  - They signal "afterthought" instead of "centerpiece"
  - Doc Set 1 is designed to feel like a publication you'd pick up on its own
- **The "sustained narrative" job:**
  - Most novelty product booklets are lists of jokes with no connective tissue
  - Individual pages are interchangeable because there's no arc
  - Doc Set 1 has a three-act structure where skipping a page means missing setup for a later payoff
- **The "unboxing pacing" job:**
  - Most gift kits have no mechanism for controlling discovery order
  - The recipient opens whatever they grab first
  - Doc Set 1 physically sits on top of the items and narratively builds to "now open the kit"
  - It is both a gate and an invitation
- **The "multi-depth comedy" job:**
  - Most gag gift inserts operate at one depth: surface-level jokes
  - They don't reward re-reading
  - Doc Set 1 has surface comedy (the main copy), visual comedy (the illustration details), structural comedy (the format subversions), and hidden comedy (the fine print and cross-references to kit items)
- **The "character commitment" job:**
  - Most novelty products break character on the first page (wink to the audience, "just kidding," legal disclaimers in normal voice)
  - Doc Set 1 never breaks character
  - Even the legal fine print is in Brad's voice
  - The sustained commitment is what makes the comedy build rather than deflate
