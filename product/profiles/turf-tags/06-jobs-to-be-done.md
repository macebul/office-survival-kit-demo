# Artifact 6: Jobs To Be Done

## Primary JTBD

**When** I discover the Turf Tags box in the kit tray
**I want to** find sticker designs funny and specific enough that I actually deploy them in the office
**So that** my workspace has visible evidence of my personality and territorial claims that generate conversations

## Supporting JTBD

**Job 2 - Overcome My Low Expectations**
- When I see stickers included in a gag gift kit, I want the packaging and designs to surprise me, so that I don't dismiss them as cheap filler before giving them a chance

**Job 3 - Give Me a Deployment Target**
- When I open the box, I want a specific suggestion for where to start, so that I'm not staring at a sticker sheet wondering what to do with them. "START WITH THE FRIDGE." solves this

**Job 4 - Let Me Mark My Territory Without Being Aggressive**
- When I put a sticker on my monitor, lunch container, or desk, I want it to communicate "this is mine" in a way that's funny rather than hostile, so that coworkers get the joke instead of filing an HR complaint

**Job 5 - Give Me Material to Share**
- When a coworker sees a deployed sticker and asks about it, I want the design to be self-explanatory, so that the joke lands without me having to explain the kit

**Job 6 - Make the Box Worth Keeping**
- When I've deployed all the stickers, I want the box itself to be entertaining and displayable, so that the product's value extends beyond the sticker sheet

## Underserved Jobs (How Competitor Sticker Products Fail)

- The "reframe the category" job - most sticker products don't try to overcome the "just stickers" perception. The packaging is minimal and confirms the low-value expectation
- The "deployment activation" job - no competitor sticker product tells you exactly where to start. They assume motivation, which most recipients don't have
- The "territory marking" job - most office stickers are decorative, not territorial. Flowers, motivational quotes, and cute animals don't claim space. These do
- The "box as entertainment" job - sticker packaging is universally disposable. Nobody keeps a sticker sheet's packaging on their desk. The yellow box is designed to be kept
