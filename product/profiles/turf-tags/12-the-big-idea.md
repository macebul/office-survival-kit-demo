# Artifact 12: The Big Idea

**Turf Tags aren't stickers. They're territorial claims filed with the Department of You.**

Every office has contested territory - the good fridge shelf, the quiet desk by the window, the mug that everyone "borrows." Turf Tags give the recipient official documentation of ownership. Not legally binding. Not enforceable. But branded, laminated, and sanctioned by Brad Sterling's Territorial Compliance Division, which is more authority than most HR departments have.

The comedy isn't "funny stickers in a box." The comedy is a fully branded corporate program for marking territory with adhesive property markers, complete with an institutional assessment report, IKEA-style deployment instructions, and a self-rated 9/5-star review from the founder.

If the stickers feel like filler, nobody deploys them. If they feel like officially sanctioned territorial claims, they go on fridges, monitors, and lunch containers within 24 hours.

**Implication for all content creation**
- Never position Turf Tags as "also includes stickers." Position it as "includes a territory-marking system from Elevate Co.'s Territorial Compliance Division"
- The yellow box should be visible in listing images because the color and illustration do more instant communication than any text
- The dog-on-chair illustration should be prominent enough to be legible in Amazon thumbnails - it's the fastest concept-delivery element in the entire kit
- Deployment is the success metric. Content should make the recipient want to deploy, not just admire. "START WITH THE FRIDGE." is the operational template - specific, actionable, funny
