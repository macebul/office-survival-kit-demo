# Artifact 6: Jobs To Be Done

## Primary JTBD

**When** I discover the Cubicle Shank in the kit
**I want to** immediately feel that this is a well-made, funny, keepable item
**So that** I trust the rest of the kit is going to be equally good

## Supporting JTBD

**Job 2 - Give Me Material to Perform**
- When I pick up the pen box in front of coworkers, I want to find something on the packaging worth reading aloud, so that the group gets a laugh and I get to be the entertainer

**Job 3 - Make Me Look Good at My Desk**
- When I put this pen in my pen cup or on my desk, I want it to look like a cool tactical pen (not a cheap novelty), so that coworkers notice it and I can tell the story of how I got it

**Job 4 - Justify the Kit's Price**
- When I'm evaluating whether this kit is worth $25-30, I want to see a pen that looks like it could cost $10-15 on its own, so that the price feels reasonable for everything combined

**Job 5 - Create a Reveal Moment**
- When I slide the sleeve off the pen box, I want the interior reveal to feel intentional and satisfying, so that the physical act of opening it becomes part of the entertainment

**Job 6 - Give Me Something I'll Actually Use**
- When the laughter dies down and the unboxing is over, I want a pen that writes well and feels good in my hand, so that the gift has daily utility beyond the initial joke

## Underserved Jobs (How Competitor Gag Pens Fail)

- The "quality surprise" job - most novelty pens feel like vending machine toys
- The "packaging as content" job - most pens come in plastic clamshells or plain boxes with zero personality
- The "desk artifact" job - most gag pens are embarrassing enough that they go in a drawer, not on display
- The "ongoing conversation" job - most funny pens have a visual gag that gets old; an engraving like "FOR OFFICE USE ONLY" stays funny because it's subtle
