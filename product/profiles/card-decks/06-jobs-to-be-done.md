# Artifact 6: Jobs To Be Done

## Primary JTBD

**When** I discover three distinct card decks in the kit
**I want to** feel like I've unlocked a library of comedy content, not just a few throwaway cards
**So that** the kit keeps giving me new material to use long after the unboxing is over

## Supporting JTBD

**Job 2 - Give Me Ammo for Office Situations**
- When a coworker does something annoying (schedules a meeting about a meeting, leaves time on the microwave, takes credit for group work), I want to pull the right card from the right deck and deploy it, so that I can address the situation with humor instead of frustration

**Job 3 - Make the Kit Last**
- When the unboxing is over and the briefcase is on my shelf, I want reasons to open it again, so that the gift keeps working weeks after I received it

**Job 4 - Give Me Content That's Worth Sharing**
- When I want to show a coworker what I got, I want individual cards that work as standalone comedy, so that the humor translates without needing to explain the full kit context

**Job 5 - Validate That the Humor Is Real**
- When I'm browsing the Amazon listing and I see card text in the product images, I want to verify that the comedy is actually good, so that I'm confident the rest of the kit will be funny too

**Job 6 - Feel Like I Got Three Products, Not One**
- When I open Tray 2 and see three distinct boxes with different colors and designs, I want each one to feel like its own product, so that the kit feels like a curated collection rather than padding

## Underserved Jobs (How Competitor Card Products Fail)

- The "sustained use" job - most gag card sets get flipped through once and never touched again
- The "social deployment" job - most cards aren't designed for giving to other people in office contexts
- The "visual variety" job - most card sets come in one box with one look, making 45 cards feel like a pile
- The "Brad voice" job - no competitor has a character whose commentary on each card builds a comedic relationship over time
- The "dual-sided reveal" job - most cards have content on one side only, missing the flip-and-discover moment
