## MY PROJECT

I need a Design Brief for the **trays** — the two identical stacking trays inside the Office Survival Kit gag gift.

### What This Surface Is

Two identical die-cut trays (single mold) that hold the item boxes in Pelican-case style recesses. They stack on top of each other in the front zone of the briefcase. Each tray has landscape-oriented recesses sized for the item boxes (not loose items).

### Physical Specs

- **Quantity:** 2 (identical — single tray mold)
- **Exterior dimensions:** ~13.5" x ~6.5" (WORKING ESTIMATE)
- **Wall height:** ~2.0"
- **Material:** Paperboard with die-cut recesses
- **Recess style:** Pelican-case style, landscape orientation, sized for item BOXES
- **Interior color scheme:** Dark charcoal, prison orange accents, white copy
- **Lift mechanism:** Hybrid — decorative back-edge tabs + front finger notch (~1"–1.5")

### Tray Contents

| Tray | Items | Recess Layout |
|------|-------|---------------|
| Tray 1 (top) | Cubicle Shank (pen, matchbox box, narrow front recess), Lifer Ink (tattoos, wide back recess), Turf Tags (stickers, wide back recess) | 1 narrow front + 1 wide back (2 items side by side) |
| Tray 2 (bottom) | Cell Talk, Rap Sheets, Sweet Nothings (3 card deck boxes, wide back recess), Confiscated Stapler (empty recess with chalk outline, narrow front recess) | 1 narrow front + 1 wide back (3 items side by side) |

### Brand Context

**The World:** The office is a prison — played straight. The trays evoke tactical/military equipment cases — items stored with institutional precision.

**Character:** Brad Sterling (B.S.) — sole voice. Two modes: (1) competent product copy, (2) delusional personal branding.

**Comedy rule:** Format stays buttoned-up, content gets unleashed.

**Tone:** Sardonic, not cynical. Warm.

**Interior color scheme:** Dark charcoal base, prison orange accents, white copy text.

### The Trays' Role in the Experience

- Tray 1 is the first item layer — revealed after lifting Doc Set 1. This is the "premium tactical equipment" moment.
- Tray 2 is revealed after lifting Tray 1 — the second wave of items, plus the Confiscated Stapler empty recess (the absence IS the joke)
- The Pelican-case die-cut aesthetic is critical — it makes cheap novelty items look like precision equipment
- Lifting each tray is a physical interaction — the tabs and finger notch should feel intentional
- The confiscated stapler recess in Tray 2 has a chalk outline and institutional label — $0 COGS, pure comedy

### Design Consideration

The trays are the canvas that gives every item its "moment." The die-cut recesses, the dark charcoal + orange color scheme, and the Pelican-case aesthetic do most of the heavy lifting in making the kit feel premium/tactical despite being a gag gift. Design considerations include: recess edge treatment, any copy/labels printed on the tray surface between recesses, the lift tab design, the confiscated stapler recess treatment (chalk outline + label), and whether the tray surface carries any additional Brad copy or branding.

### Type of Project

- A. Packaging (interior surface)

### Complexity

- B. Moderate (constrained surface area, established visual language, but the confiscated stapler recess is a unique design challenge)
