## MY PROJECT

I need a Design Brief for the **briefcase exterior** of the Office Survival Kit — a novelty gag gift sold on Amazon.

### What This Surface Is

The briefcase is a matte black clamshell box that houses the entire kit. It's the SECOND thing the recipient sees (after the outer Amazon mailer box). The exterior has multiple surfaces with different design elements.

### Physical Specs

- **Overall dimensions:** 14" x 11.5" x 5.0"
- **Material:** 24pt paperboard, matte black exterior finish
- **Closure:** 4-magnet system + belly band (TBD) + optional snap-fit lip

**Exterior surfaces:**

| Surface | Dimensions | Key Elements |
|---------|-----------|-------------|
| Lid / top (+Z) | 14" x 11.5" | Main label (cream, kit name + tagline), "CLEARED FOR OFFICE USE" rubber stamp (red), barcode/evidence tag ("CASE NO. 9-5"), THIS SIDE UP |

### Brand Context

**The World:** The office is a prison — played straight, never commented on. Vocabulary: doing time, your cell, contraband, lifers, release day, the warden, etc.

**Character:** Brad Sterling (B.S.) — Chief Vision Officer & Founder, Elevate Co. Sole voice on all surfaces. Two modes: (1) competent product copy in straight-faced institutional formats, (2) delusional personal branding. Format stays buttoned-up, content gets unleashed.

**Visual identity:** Purple satin shirt, oversized gold chain, aviator sunglasses, big cheesy smile. Color palette: gold, purple, black.

**Tone:** Sardonic, not cynical. Warm under the dark humor.

**Interior color scheme:** Dark charcoal, prison orange accents, white copy.

### What's Inside (for context — not designing this now)

7 items + 1 empty recess in 2 stacking trays, hidden mug compartment behind a J-shaped compartment cover, 2 doc sets. Full reveal sequence is layered.

### The Briefcase's Role in the Experience

This is the recipient's second touchpoint (after the Amazon box). It needs to:
- Look like an actual (if slightly absurd) briefcase
- Communicate the prison-office world through institutional labels and stamps
- Build anticipation for what's inside
- Work as a gag gift reveal moment — funny when you first see it, funnier when you read the labels
