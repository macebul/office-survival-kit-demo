## ARTIFACT 1: DESIGN BRIEF

---

### 1. Goal

#### 1.1 The Task (What's Being Made)

- A tuck-end retail box for a 15-card deck of "compliment-front / absurd-insult-back" cards
- Brad Sterling's Elevate Co. branding — marketed as **"Ambient Communication Cards"**
- Full-color print on all 6 exterior faces + tuck flap
- ~4" x 2.5" x 0.75" (working estimate, accommodating 15 business-card-format cards)
- Visual direction already established: cream/blush pink palette, rose gold ornamental flourishes, elegant serif script, wax seal
- Production dielines and templates already exist (SVG/PDF)

#### 1.2 The Goal (What It Must Achieve)

- **Immediate goal:** Make the recipient laugh when they pick up this box in Tray 2 — the elegant, "classy" appearance should be funny in context alongside the dark Rap Sheets box and clinical Cell Talk box
- **Misdirection goal:** The box should look like a genuinely lovely product. The comedy is that it's packaging passive-aggression as a luxury stationery item. The recipient should think "oh, this one's pretty" before reading closer and realizing what it actually is.
- **Standalone goal:** Work as a funny, self-contained object even if separated from the kit — someone seeing this box on a desk should want to pick it up
- **Brand goal:** Look like a real, mass-produced retail product from a slightly unhinged luxury stationery brand
- **Deployment goal:** Prime the recipient for how to USE the cards — the box copy should teach them that these cards are deployed silently (left on desks, taped to microwaves, handed over with a straight face)

#### 1.3 The Goal Chain

```
Box noticed in Tray 2 — stands out as the "pretty one" among three very different boxes (ATTENTION)
  → Recipient picks it up, reads the elegant front face copy (FIRST IMPRESSION: "this is nice")
    → Reads tagline / subtext more carefully — realizes it's packaging passive-aggression as sophistication (THE TURN)
      → Opens box, reads a card front ("I enjoy working with you.") then flips to back ("You have the energy of a wasp trapped in a car...") (THE PAYOFF)
        → Immediately starts planning who to deploy cards on (ACTION)
```

#### 1.4 What "Success" Looks Like

- The box creates a **double-take moment** — looks lovely at first glance, reveals its true nature on closer reading
- The recipient understands the card mechanic (nice front / devastating back) from the box packaging before opening
- The wax seal ("HANDCRAFTED WITH PLAUSIBLE DENIABILITY") gets a laugh
- When seen alongside Cell Talk (zen) and Rap Sheets (dark), the three boxes read as the same product line without looking remotely similar — like Brad created a different "brand" for each deck
- The box is kept on a desk after the cards are deployed. It's an object people comment on.
- The box photographs well for social sharing — the elegant design + savage content contrast is inherently shareable

### 2. Audience

#### 2.1 The Buyer (Gift Giver)

- **Who:** Someone buying a gag gift for a coworker, friend, or family member who works in an office
- **Relationship to recipient:** Close enough to know the recipient will find workplace humor funny, not offensive
- **Purchase channel:** Amazon (FBA)
- **What they're buying:** A laugh. Social currency. "I saw this and thought of you" energy.
- **The buyer never sees this box directly** — they see the outer Amazon box, the briefcase exterior, and possibly product listing photos. The Sweet Nothings box is a discovery for the recipient.

#### 2.2 The Recipient (User)

- **Who:** An office worker currently "doing time"
- **Humor profile:** Appreciates the craft of a well-constructed insult. The kind of person who laughs at "you have the leadership qualities of the front shopping cart wheel that goes sideways while the other three try to move forward" — not because it's mean, but because it's *perfect*.
- **Before state:** Opening Tray 2 after already discovering Tray 1 items. They've seen the Cubicle Shank (tactical pen), Lifer Ink (tattoos), and Turf Tags (stickers). They're deep in the world, fully entertained, expecting more.
- **After state:** Armed with 15 cards they will actually deploy. They already have a coworker in mind for at least 3 of them.
- **Key insight:** Nobody's feelings get hurt by these cards because the insult images are too absurd and specific to take personally. You can't be offended by being compared to a Roomba or an expired coupon — you're too busy laughing at how perfect it is.

#### 2.3 Buyer vs. User Gap

- The buyer never interacts with this box directly
- All box design decisions serve the recipient's in-hand discovery experience
- The box needs to photograph well for social sharing (the recipient shows friends what they got)
- The elegant visual direction actually helps Amazon listing photos — it stands out from the rest of the kit's darker palette

### 3. Context

#### 3.1 Physical Context

- **Location:** Tray 2 of briefcase, wide back recess
- **Neighbors:** Cell Talk and Rap Sheets — three identical-dimension tuck-end boxes side by side. Confiscated Stapler empty recess (chalk outline) also in Tray 2.
- **Discovery moment:** Tray 2 is revealed when Tray 1 is lifted. Recipient sees all three card boxes and the empty recess simultaneously.
- **Visual contrast in tray:** Three boxes with radically different visual identities sitting side by side — sage green/white (Cell Talk), black/red (Rap Sheets), cream/blush pink (Sweet Nothings). This variety is intentional and correct.
- **Viewing conditions:** In-hand, close-up reading distance. Indoor lighting. The ornamental details and wax seal need to be legible at this scale.
- **Physical interaction:** Picked up, turned over, all faces read (the Stickers and Tats boxes prove that recipients examine every face). Opened, cards pulled out, individual cards flipped front-to-back.

#### 3.2 Experience Context (Where It Falls in the Reveal Sequence)

1. Outer Amazon box (Brad's marketing — full-color, over-the-top)
2. Briefcase exterior (matte black, duct tape handle)
3. Open briefcase → Doc Set 1
4. Lift Doc Set 1 → **Tray 1** (Cubicle Shank, Lifer Ink, Turf Tags)
5. Lift Tray 1 → **Tray 2 — THIS BOX** + Cell Talk + Rap Sheets + Confiscated Stapler recess
6. Lift Tray 2 → Doc Set 2 on compartment cover
7. Lift compartment cover → Hidden mug compartment (Mug Shot)

- By Tray 2, the recipient has experienced 4 layers and understands the world
- They've already seen box designs with strong personality (the bold yellow Stickers box, the refined cream Tats box, the premium Pen box)
- Comedy expectations are established — no setup needed, just payoff
- The three card boxes are the "middle act" — substantial discovery before the finale

#### 3.3 Brand Context

- **The World:** The office is a prison. Played 100% straight. "Sweet Nothings" = the passive-aggressive messages that circulate in every workplace. Whispered resentments made tangible.
- **Character:** Brad Sterling (B.S.) — sole voice on every surface
- **Brad's Mode 1 (Product Copy):** Competent, dry, institutional. This box leans HEAVILY into Mode 1 — the elegant packaging IS the competent product copy. Brad knows exactly what he's doing with this one.
- **Brad's Mode 2 (Personal Brand):** Delusional, aspirational. May appear on the back face (Brad quote, headshot, title) — consistent with how he appears on other box backs (Tats, Stickers).
- **Comedy Rule (LOCKED):** "Format stays buttoned-up, content gets unleashed." This box is the PUREST expression of this rule — the format is literally a luxury stationery product, the content is weaponized insults.
- **Tone:** Sardonic, not cynical. Warm. The insults are funny because they're craft, not cruelty.
- **Brad's Division for this product:** Not yet named. Other boxes use "Territorial Compliance Division" (Stickers) and "Dermal Achievement Division" (Tats). Sweet Nothings needs its own — something like "Interpersonal Clarity Division" or "Ambient Relations Bureau."

#### 3.4 What the Cards Actually Are (Critical for Box Copy)

The box copy must accurately represent the card mechanic. Here's what's inside:

- **15 cards**, business-card format (3.5" x 2")
- **Front:** A genuinely nice compliment — "I enjoy working with you." / "You're one of a kind." / "Happy birthday."
- **Back:** A hyper-specific, absurd insult built on everyday imagery — the comparison is so weirdly precise that the CRAFT of the insult is funnier than the meanness
- **Examples the box could reference or preview:**
  - "I enjoy working with you." → "You have the energy of a wasp trapped in a car that everyone's pretending isn't there."
  - "You always give 110%." → "You're like a car alarm in a Walmart parking lot. Absolutely going off. Nobody coming to check."
  - "Your feedback is always welcome." → "Getting feedback from you is like getting directions from a dog. You seem very sure. You are pointing at a squirrel."
- **Why they work:** Nobody's feelings get hurt because the images are too bizarre to take personally. The craft of the insult IS the joke.
- **Deployment:** Leave on a desk, tape to the microwave, hand over with a straight face. The nice front is the alibi. The back is the truth.

#### 3.5 Existing Visual Direction (From Hero Image)

The hero image already establishes:

- **Palette:** Cream/off-white base, blush pink accents, rose gold metallic for flourishes and borders
- **Typography:** Elegant serif script for "Ambient Communication Cards" — the kind of typeface you'd see on a wedding invitation or luxury stationery
- **Ornamental elements:** Rose gold corner flourishes, decorative dividers, thin ornamental borders — the visual language of formal correspondence
- **Wax seal:** Rose gold, reads "HANDCRAFTED WITH PLAUSIBLE DENIABILITY" — this is the box's signature comedy moment
- **Front face tagline:** "Say what you mean. Beautifully."
- **Left side copy (partially visible):** "Every workplace deserves a little more honesty. These cards provide it. Gently."
- **Card styling:** Cards have rounded corners. Front cards are blush pink with ornamental border, back cards are dusty mauve/rose with clean serif text.
- **Overall aesthetic:** Luxury greeting card brand. Think Papyrus meets passive-aggression. The box looks like it should cost $28 at Anthropologie.

- Full-color print on all 6 exterior faces + tuck flap

### 5. The Design Problem (Attention > Message > Action)

#### 5.1 Attention Problem — LARGELY SOLVED

The existing visual direction handles this well:
- The cream/pink/rose gold palette naturally stands out against Cell Talk's sage and Rap Sheets' black
- The elegant stationery aesthetic is visually arresting at this scale — the ornamental flourishes give it presence
- The box reads as "the pretty one" of the trio — recipients will notice it

**Remaining attention concern:** At 4" x 2.5" x 0.75", the ornamental flourishes and wax seal are small. All decorative detail needs to be legible and impactful at actual print size.

#### 5.2 Message Problem

- **Primary message:** "These are cards with a nice message on the front and a devastating (but hilarious) message on the back"
- The hero image previews this with the "You always know exactly what to say." / "Unfortunately." card pair — this is the right approach
- The tagline "Say what you mean. Beautifully." lands the premise without explaining the joke
- The box must teach the **deployment method** somewhere (leave on desks, hand over with a straight face) — likely on back face or side copy
- "Ambient Communication Cards" as a Brad rebrand needs no additional explanation — the name itself is the corporate euphemism joke

#### 5.3 Action Problem

- The box must make recipients want to open it and look at the cards
- The single-word back preview ("Unfortunately.") creates curiosity — recipients will want to see what the full backs say
- After reading a few cards, recipients should immediately think of specific coworkers for specific cards
- The box should be kept on a desk afterward — the elegant design makes it a conversation piece

### 6. Reference Points

#### 6.1 What This Box Is Parodying

- **Luxury stationery brands** (Papyrus, Rifle Paper Co., Crane) — the visual language of expensive cards you buy for people you theoretically care about
- **Wellness / mindfulness card decks** — the kind of product that comes in a lovely box and promises to improve your relationships
- **Wedding invitation suites** — ornamental flourishes, wax seals, elegant serif fonts, cream stock
- **"Thoughtful" corporate gifting** — the box looks like something HR would buy for an employee appreciation event

#### 6.2 What Makes It Funny

The humor comes from the gap between:
- **Format:** Luxury stationery product with ornamental flourishes, wax seal, and a tagline about beautiful communication
- **Content:** "You have the leadership qualities of the front shopping cart wheel that goes sideways while the other three try to move forward"

This is the purest possible expression of "format stays buttoned-up, content gets unleashed."

## ARTIFACT 2: RECOMMENDED APPROACH

---

### Strategic Direction

**"The Elegant Weapon."** The visual direction is already established and it's the right call — a luxury stationery product that happens to contain weaponized insults. The box is the most visually refined object in the entire kit, and that refinement IS the joke.

Where the Stickers box is loud (yellow, bold, a dog peeing on an office chair), and the Rap Sheets box is threatening (black, red, "AUTHORIZED FOR INTERDEPARTMENTAL USE"), the Sweet Nothings box is *beautiful*. It's the coworker who destroys you while smiling. That's the character of this box, and the hero image nails it.

### What's Already Done

- Visual direction locked (hero image)
- Card content drafted (15 cards, v8)
- Production dielines and templates created (SVG/PDF)
- 3D model exists (GLB)
- Tagline, wax seal text, and partial side copy visible in hero

### What Needs to Happen Next

#### Step 1: Finalize Box Copy for All Faces

The hero image establishes the front face and hints at side copy. The remaining faces need copy written in Brad's voice. Following the pattern established by Stickers and Tats:

| Face | Content Needed | Format Direction |
|------|---------------|-----------------|
| **Front** | Mostly established from hero. Confirm: Elevate Co., division name, "Ambient Communication Cards," tagline, wax seal, card count, SKU number | Luxury stationery front panel |
| **Back** | Brad headshot + quote + format parody. Could be: "Distinguished Communicators™" testimonials, a "Satisfaction Survey" with pre-filled answers, or a "Communication Efficacy Report" with absurd stats | Follow the Stickers/Tats pattern: themed report/testimonial + Brad callout |
| **Left side** | Partially visible in hero: "Every workplace deserves a little more honesty..." Finalize this. | Elegant copy block, small text |
| **Right side** | Deployment instructions as icon sequence. Stickers has PEEL→STICK→HOLD COURT→ACCEPT PARADE→EXIT. Tats has CUT→WET→30 SEC→DISPLAY. Sweet Nothings needs its own (e.g., SELECT→PLACE→RETREAT→DENY→REPEAT) | Ornamental icon strip — same concept as other boxes but rendered in rose gold line art |
| **Top** | Product name + tagline (visible when box is in tray) | Minimal, elegant |
| **Bottom** | Format parody — could be: "ingredients" (Contains: 15 Compliments (front-facing), 15 Truths (rear-facing), 0 Apologies), fine print warnings, or a "Certificate of Plausible Deniability" | Formal fine print, legal disclaimer parody |
| **Tuck flap** | Partially visible in hero. Finalize. | Small script text |

#### Step 2: Establish Brad's Division Name

Other boxes have specific Elevate Co. divisions:
- Stickers: "Territorial Compliance Division"
- Tats: "Dermal Achievement Division"

Sweet Nothings needs one. Candidates:
- "Ambient Relations Bureau"
- "Interpersonal Clarity Division"
- "Diplomatic Communications Division"
- "Office Harmony Bureau" (ironic)
- "Workplace Sentiment Department"

Should match the box's elegant-but-sharp personality. "Ambient Relations Bureau" feels right — bureaucratic enough to be funny, euphemistic enough to match "Ambient Communication Cards."

#### Step 4: Review Card Content Against Box Copy

The 15-card draft (v8) has assessment notes flagging:

- **Concerns:** Card 2 (check engine light) may run long. Card 7 (Roomba) has one beat too many. Card 14 (software update) may be too clever.
- If any card front is previewed on the box exterior, choose one that's universally relatable — "You always know exactly what to say." (from the hero image) is a strong choice

#### Step 5: Review the Trio Side by Side

Place all three card box hero images together and verify:
- They read as the same product line (Elevate Co. branding ties them)
- Each has a radically different personality (zen vs. threatening vs. elegant)
- The variety creates the impression of a company with range — Brad made a whole line
- No two boxes compete for the same "slot" — they're complementary, not overlapping

### Risks & Mitigation

| Risk | Mitigation |
|------|------------|
| Ornamental details illegible at actual print size (4" x 2.5") | Test all flourishes and wax seal at 100% print scale. Simplify if needed. |
| Box too pretty — recipient doesn't immediately get the joke | The tagline "Say what you mean. Beautifully." and the wax seal "HANDCRAFTED WITH PLAUSIBLE DENIABILITY" do the recontextualization. If the card preview on the exterior is clear, the turn happens fast. |
| Card back preview ("Unfortunately.") may not be enough to signal the flip mechanic | Consider whether the back face or side copy should include a second example — but don't oversell. The box should tease, not spoil. |
| Brad's back-face appearance may clash with elegant aesthetic | Brad appears on the cream/gold Tats box without breaking the elegance. Use the same approach: formal framing, Brad being Brad within the format. |
| Rose gold / blush palette reads too feminine | The content (absurd insults, workplace warfare) immediately counters this. The palette is "luxury stationery" not "feminine." If still a concern, lean the palette slightly warmer/more gold than pink. |

### What's NOT Needed

- A new visual direction — the hero image has it right
- A shared grid system across the three card boxes — they're intentionally distinct
- Extensive copy explaining the premise — the recipient is already 4 layers deep by this point
- Any reference to the OG character system (removed April 2026)
