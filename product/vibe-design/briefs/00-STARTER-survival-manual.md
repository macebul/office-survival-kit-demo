## MY PROJECT

I need a Design Brief for the **Office Survival Manual (Doc Set 2)** — the saddle-stitched booklet inside the Office Survival Kit gag gift.

### What This Surface Is

A 16-page saddle-stitched booklet titled "Office Survival Manual Volume II." It sits on top of the compartment cover and is discovered after lifting Tray 2. This is an editorial/print piece — not a box.

### Physical Specs

- **Format:** 16-page saddle-stitched booklet
- **Dimensions:** ~5.5" x 4.25" (quarter-letter landscape) (WORKING ESTIMATE)
- **Thickness:** ~0.25"
- **Print:** 2-color
- **Location:** Sits on compartment cover surface, discovered after lifting Tray 2

### What It Contains

- **Title:** Office Survival Manual Volume II
- **Content direction:** Activity guide format — interactive activities, quizzes, fill-in prompts, and survival tips all filtered through the office-as-prison world
- **Content direction source:** See `product/copy/activity-guide-recs-v2.md` for detailed content recommendations

### Brand Context

**The World:** The office is a prison — played straight. The manual is an institutional document — something you'd receive during intake/processing.

**Character:** Brad Sterling (B.S.) — sole voice. Two modes: (1) competent product copy, (2) delusional personal branding. The manual is published by Elevate Co. Brad would see this as a flagship thought-leadership publication.

**Comedy rule:** Format stays buttoned-up, content gets unleashed.

**Tone:** Sardonic, not cynical. Warm.

### The Manual's Role in the Experience

- Discovered in the later stages of the unboxing — after Tray 1, Tray 2, and before the hidden mug compartment
- "Volume II" implies a Volume I exists somewhere (it doesn't — or does it? This is a design decision)
- This is the most text-heavy, content-rich piece in the kit — it's where Brad gets to really write
- It's the piece most likely to be read cover-to-cover and shared with coworkers
- Acts as a palate cleanser between the item reveals and the final hidden compartment discovery

### Design Consideration

This is an editorial design challenge, not a packaging challenge. The medium is fundamentally different from the boxes — it's a printed booklet with spreads, pagination, typography, and a reading flow. The design needs to feel institutional (like an employee handbook or government-issued manual) while being genuinely fun to read. Consider how Brad's two modes play across 16 pages — does Mode 2 (delusional branding) creep in more as the pages progress?

### Type of Project

- D. Print / Editorial

### Complexity

- C. Complex/significant (16 pages of editorial design, interactive elements, content strategy)
