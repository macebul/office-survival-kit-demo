# Design Brief: Cubicle Shank Item Box

> **Project:** Cubicle Shank item box — individual packaging for the tactical pen
> **Product:** Office Survival Kit (Elevate Co.)
> **Type:** Packaging — carded blister pack
> **Complexity:** Moderate
> **Status:** Working draft with strong existing art direction
> **Date:** May 14, 2026

---

## 1. GOAL

### Task vs. Goal

- **Task (the deliverable):** Refine the packaging design for the Cubicle Shank item box — all exterior and interior surfaces of a matchbox-style tray-and-sleeve box
- **Goal (the change in the world):** When the recipient picks up this box from the tray, they feel a mix of "this is a real product," "this is absurd and I love it," and "wait... is this actually cool?" — then they hold it up and read it to the group

### Goal Chain

```
Upstream:   Kit sells on Amazon → recipient has a memorable unboxing experience
     ↓
This goal:  Cubicle Shank box captures attention in 2-3 seconds, rewards
            closer reading, and gets read aloud to the room
     ↓
Downstream: Recipient keeps the pen (it's genuinely good quality), box
            becomes a desk artifact or gets photographed for social
```

### Before State → After State

| | Before | After |
|---|--------|-------|
| **Recipient** | Sees a black box sitting in a die-cut tray recess | Has already read copy out loud to the group, is laughing, wants to slide it open |
| **Group** | Watching the unboxing | Laughing at what was just read aloud, leaning in to see more |

### Success Criteria

- Recipient picks up box and reads it BEFORE opening it (attention captured by exterior)
- At least one line gets read aloud or quoted to the room
- The slide-open moment feels intentional and premium, not fiddly
- Box is kept on a desk or shelf after the pen is removed (artifact value)
- The box works as a standalone funny object even outside the kit context

### Failure Criteria

- Recipient glances at box and immediately slides it open without reading
- Copy is too dense or too small to scan in a group setting
- The matchbox mechanism feels cheap or confusing
- The comedy relies on understanding context from elsewhere in the kit
- It reads as a generic "tactical pen" product box with no personality

## 2. AUDIENCE

### Primary Audience: The Recipient

- **Who:** An office worker currently "doing time" — the person receiving the kit as a gift
- **Context:** They're opening the kit, likely with coworkers watching
- **Before state:** They've already opened the briefcase and lifted Doc Set 1. They see items sitting in Pelican-case-style recesses. They're already primed — they know this product has a sense of humor. They're looking for the next thing to react to
- **Mindset:** Engaged, amused, performing for the group. Looking for the next laugh

### Secondary Audience: The Group

- **Who:** Coworkers gathered around during unboxing
- **Before state:** Watching, entertained, waiting for the next bit
- **Need from the box:** Text large enough to be read aloud. Jokes that land without explanation. Visual presence that reads from 3-4 feet away

### Buyer vs. User

- **Buyer:** The gift-giver (a coworker or friend who knows the recipient is "doing time"). They bought this on Amazon. They've already been sold — this box doesn't need to convince them
- **User:** The recipient. This box needs to delight them in the moment
- **Optimize for:** The recipient's in-the-moment experience, with group shareability as the key multiplier

### Key Psychological Insight

The recipient is performing. They're the center of attention during unboxing. The box needs to give them material — lines worth reading aloud, reactions worth having. The box is a prop in a social moment.

## 3. CONTEXT

### Physical Environment

- **Discovery moment:** Tray 1, narrow front recess (centered). First tray revealed after lifting Doc Set 1
- **Surrounding items:** Lifer Ink box (left) and Turf Tags box (right) in the same tray
- **Lighting:** Typical office/home lighting — not controlled
- **Viewing distance:** Arm's length when picked up, 3-4 feet for the group watching

### Sought vs. Served

- **Sought.** By the time someone picks up this box, they're actively exploring. Attention is given, not earned. But attention depth is shallow — they'll give you 2-3 seconds before deciding to read further or just open it
- **The design challenge:** Convert a 2-3 second glance into 5-10 seconds of reading. The goal is to make the exterior compelling enough that opening it feels like it can wait

### Attention Available

- **Realistic window:** 2-3 seconds initial scan, extendable to 5-10 if the first thing they see hooks them
- **Competition:** Other items in the tray are also competing for attention
- **Group dynamic:** Accelerates pace — people want to move through items. But a genuinely funny box slows the whole group down in a good way

### Physical Constraints

| Attribute | Spec |
|-----------|------|
| **Box type** | Tray-and-sleeve (matchbox style) — two components |
| **Outer dimensions** | 6.5" x 1.75" x 1.0" (165.1 x 44.45 x 25.4 mm) |
| **Sleeve material** | Printed paperboard (14-18pt) |
| **Print** | Full color, all surfaces |
| **Printable surfaces** | Sleeve: top, bottom, 2 long edges, 2 short ends (open). Inner tray: interior floor and walls visible when sleeve removed |
| **Largest surface** | Top face: 6.5" x 1.75" — long and narrow. This is the hero surface |
| **Narrowest surfaces** | Long edges: 6.5" x 1.0". Short ends: 1.75" x 1.0" |
| **Interior** | Die-cut recess shaped to the pen's geometry. Visible copy area around/below pen |

### Emotional/Mental State at Discovery

- Already amused (they've seen the briefcase, the Doc Set, the tray layout)
- Primed for the bit (they know the kit has a personality)
- Socially engaged (likely an audience present)
- Curious but impatient (want to keep exploring)

## 4. PROBLEMS (Attention > Message > Action)

### Causal Chain

The box must solve these problems in order. If any step fails, everything after it fails.

**Step 1: ATTENTION — Get picked up first (or early)**
- The box sits in a tray with two other items. It needs to visually distinguish itself
- It's the longest box and the only matchbox-style construction — structural differentiation is built in
- The matte black palette with bold white/red type already contrasts well against other boxes (Turf Tags is yellow, for example)
- **Current status:** Strong. The existing art direction handles this well

**Step 2: ATTENTION — Hold the eye for more than a glance**
- Once picked up, the recipient will scan the top face for ~2 seconds
- Typography hierarchy must guide the eye: biggest element first, then a hook line, then supporting detail
- **Current status:** Working. The existing layout has "EXECUTIVE TACTICAL STYLUS™" as the dominant type with supporting elements, but the visual hierarchy and eye-path could be evaluated for optimization
- **Key question:** Does the current hierarchy guide the eye in the right order, or does it present everything at once?

**Step 3: MESSAGE — Land something worth saying out loud**
- The recipient needs to encounter at least one line that's funny enough to read to the group
- That line needs to be scannable at arm's length, not buried in small type
- **Current status:** The "FIELD DEPLOYMENT" steps on the long edge are strong ("1. REMOVE FROM HOUSING / 2. ESTABLISH GRIP / 3. MAINTAIN EYE CONTACT"). The care instructions on the back are also solid. The question is whether the single funniest line is prominent enough to be the one they read first

**Step 4: ACTION — Slide it open (the reveal)**
- The matchbox mechanism is itself a moment. Sliding the inner tray out should feel deliberate
- The interior reveal ("THE PEN IS MIGHTIER THAN THE SWORD. QUIETER, TOO.") pays off the buildup
- The pen sitting in a die-cut recess should feel like opening a case — Pelican-case energy at miniature scale
- **Current status:** Strong. The interior copy and pen presentation are working well

### Problem Priority

1. **Hierarchy and eye-path on the top face** — this is where the 2-3 second window is won or lost
2. **Surfacing the funniest line** — making sure the best joke is scannable, not hidden
3. **Edge and bottom surfaces** — rewarding the flip-it-over instinct with escalating detail
4. **Interior reveal** — already strong, minor refinement only

## 5. MESSAGES

- This is a tactical pen, packaged with dead-serious institutional formatting
- The name (prison term vs. corporate rebrand — see Open Questions)

- Field deployment instructions played completely straight

### Priority 4 (the reveal)

## 6. EXISTING DESIGN DIRECTION

### What Exists

The box has a strong working draft with art applied to all surfaces. Key elements:

**Sleeve Top Face (Hero Surface):**
- "ELEVATE CO. — Tactical Writing Division" header
- "EXECUTIVE TACTICAL STYLUS™" as dominant display type
- "Writes in Black. Raises Concerns." tagline
- Field deployment steps running along the long edge

**Sleeve Bottom Face:**
- Care instructions: "WIPE DOWN BETWEEN USES. FINGERPRINTS ARE YOUR RESPONSIBILITY."
- "IF INK RUNS OUT, INTIMIDATION DOESN'T" note
- Prohibited activities icons (dice, running figure, etc.)

**Sleeve Long Edges:**
- "FIELD DEPLOYMENT:" numbered steps
- QC/site designation marks

**Interior (Tray):**
- "THE PEN IS MIGHTIER THAN THE SWORD. QUIETER, TOO."
- "FOR BEST RESULTS, REMOVE WHILE MAINTAINING EYE CONTACT WITH THE NEAREST COWORKER"
- Die-cut pen-shaped recess

**Color Palette:**
- Matte black ground
- Bold white primary type
- Red accent type for emphasis/hierarchy
- Consistent with briefcase interior scheme (dark charcoal, prison orange, white)

### What's Working

- The matte black with white/red type is strong and feels premium-tactical
- The "Field Deployment" steps are funny and scannable
- The interior reveal line is a genuine payoff
- The prohibited activities icons add visual interest and rewardable detail
- The overall voice is Mode 1 Brad — competent, straight-faced, institutionally formatted

### What to Evaluate

- **Typographic hierarchy:** Is the eye being guided in the optimal order, or does the density of information create a flat scan?
- **The "read aloud" line:** Which single line is the strongest candidate for being read to the group? Is it visually prominent enough?
- **Edge surfaces:** Are the long edges (1.0" tall) being used effectively, or is the type too small to matter?
- **Short ends:** The open ends of the sleeve are visible when the box sits in the tray. Are they being used?

## 7. OPEN QUESTIONS & DEPENDENCIES

### BLOCKING: Naming System Decision

**This brief has a dependency on a kit-wide naming system decision that has not been made yet.**

The question: Where does each name live across all item boxes?

- **Prison term** (Cubicle Shank) — the funny name, the one people say out loud
- **Brad's corporate rebrand** (Executive Tactical Stylus™) — the institutional veneer that makes the prison term funnier by contrast

Current instinct from the creator:
- The prison term should be the prominent display name (the one people read and say)
- Brad's rebrand should be the institutional header/designation above or below it
- The tray recess in the briefcase should label items by their prison term
- This system should be consistent across all 7 item boxes

**This decision needs its own dedicated prompt/session.** Once resolved, it will update the naming hierarchy in this brief and all other item box briefs. Until then, the current design (which leads with "EXECUTIVE TACTICAL STYLUS™") should be treated as a working layout subject to change.

### Open Questions

| # | Question | Impact |
|---|----------|--------|
| 1 | **Naming hierarchy** — Which name gets hero treatment on the box? | Affects the entire top face layout and typographic hierarchy |
| 2 | **Pen engraving** — "FOR OFFICE USE ONLY" is strong but open to refinement. Alternatives? | Low impact — current version works |
| 3 | **Interior copy** — "THE PEN IS MIGHTIER THAN THE SWORD. QUIETER, TOO." is strong but technically open. Any alternative must be stronger | Low impact — current version is near-final |
| 4 | **Short end treatment** — Are the open sleeve ends (1.75" x 1.0") designed? They're visible when the box sits in the tray recess | Medium impact — opportunity for additional detail or branding |
| 5 | **Tray recess label** — Should the briefcase tray recess itself have a label for this item? (Separate from the box) | Low-medium — enhances the Pelican-case feel |

### Assumptions to Validate

- 14-18pt paperboard is sufficient for the matchbox mechanism to feel premium and not flimsy

# Recommended Approach

> **Artifact 2** — Strategic direction and action plan for the Cubicle Shank box design refinement.

---

## Strategic Direction

### Core Principle: The 2-Second Hook

The entire design strategy hinges on one moment: the recipient picks up the box and gives it 2-3 seconds. Everything on the top face must be organized to convert that glance into 5-10 seconds of engagement. This is not a browsing context — it's a social performance context. The box needs to give the recipient a line to deliver.

### Design Approach: Military-Issue Product Packaging, Played Straight

The existing direction is correct — this should feel like institutional tactical gear packaging. Think: what if the Department of Defense had a Workplace Solutions Division and they issued standardized writing instruments to office personnel. Every surface formatted like it came from a government supply catalog. Every line of copy escalates the absurdity while the format stays locked.

### The Dual-Name Advantage

Once the kit-wide naming system is resolved, this box gets a powerful structural comedy device:
- The institutional header (Brad's rebrand) sets the tone of legitimacy
- The display name (prison term) delivers the laugh
- The gap between them IS the joke — you don't need to write a punchline, the juxtaposition does the work

### Surface Strategy

| Surface | Role | Priority |
|---------|------|----------|
| **Top face** | Hero surface. Hook in 2 seconds. Contains the name, the tagline, and the single strongest scannable line | Critical |
| **Long edges** | Reward the turn. Field deployment steps, operational specs, QC marks | High |
| **Bottom face** | Reward the flip. Care instructions, warnings, disclaimers, prohibited activities | High |
| **Short ends** | Visible in tray. Subtle branding, catalog numbers, or classification marks | Medium |
| **Interior floor** | The reveal. Payoff line + pen in recess | High |
| **Interior walls** | Supporting detail if space allows | Low |

---

## Action Plan

### Phase 1: Resolve Dependencies

- [ ] **Naming system decision** — Run a separate dedicated session to establish the kit-wide naming hierarchy (prison term vs. Brad's rebrand placement rules). This blocks the top face layout
- [ ] **Confirm box dimensions** with sourcing partner — 6.5" x 1.75" x 1.0" is a working estimate
- [ ] **Confirm paperboard weight** — matchbox mechanism needs to feel premium, not flimsy. Request 18pt minimum samples

### Phase 2: Top Face Hierarchy Refinement

- [ ] Evaluate current top face layout against the eye-path goal: What does the eye hit first? Second? Third?
- [ ] Once naming system is resolved, lay out the new hierarchy: institutional header → display name → tagline → supporting detail
- [ ] Identify the single "read aloud" line and test its prominence — can it be read from arm's length in 1 second?
- [ ] Test the layout at actual print size (6.5" x 1.75") — this is a narrow surface and density matters

### Phase 3: Secondary Surface Refinement

- [ ] Review long edge copy ("FIELD DEPLOYMENT" steps) for readability at 1.0" height
- [ ] Review bottom face copy density — is there room to breathe, or is it wall-to-wall?
- [ ] Design short end treatments — these are visible when box sits in tray; even minimal marks (catalog number, classification) add authenticity
- [ ] Confirm prohibited activities icons are reading clearly at print size

### Phase 4: Interior Refinement

- [ ] Evaluate interior reveal copy — "THE PEN IS MIGHTIER THAN THE SWORD. QUIETER, TOO." is strong. Only replace if something demonstrably stronger emerges
- [ ] Consider whether the sub-line ("FOR BEST RESULTS, REMOVE WHILE MAINTAINING EYE CONTACT...") needs size/placement adjustment
- [ ] Confirm die-cut recess shape works with actual pen samples

### Phase 5: Production Validation

- [ ] Print a physical mockup at actual size and test:
  - Can you read the top face in 2-3 seconds?
  - Does the matchbox slide mechanism feel good?
  - Does the interior reveal land?
  - Do the edges read or are they too small?
- [ ] Test with 2-3 people who haven't seen it before — do they read it or skip to opening?
- [ ] Adjust based on physical testing

## Risks

| Risk | Likelihood | Mitigation |
|------|-----------|------------|
| Naming system decision delays all box designs | Medium | Flag as priority; run the naming session before continuing any box refinements |
| Top face too dense at actual print size | Medium | Print at 100% scale early; test readability before finalizing |
| Matchbox mechanism feels cheap in paperboard | Low-Medium | Specify 18pt minimum; request structural samples from printer |
| Interior copy gets obscured by pen sitting in recess | Low | Ensure copy is positioned above/below the recess, not under the pen |
| Short ends get ignored in design process | Medium | Include them explicitly in the design template; even minimal treatment adds polish |
