# Doc Set 1 -- Photorealistic Image Prompts

**Created:** May 19, 2026
**Status:** Draft v1 -- Pages 2-15 (complete booklet)
**Visual style:** Photorealistic corporate stock photography. Internal communications aesthetic. Flat overhead fluorescent lighting, beige walls, drop ceilings, gray carpet, standard commercial office furniture. Photos look competent but not artful. Everyone looks slightly tired. Nobody reacts to absurdity.
**Generator:** Midjourney v6.1
**Booklet size:** ~5.5" x 7" portrait, 16 pages saddle-stitched. Images print at approximately 2"x2" as vignettes within body copy.

**Comedy technique:** "Format stays buttoned-up, content gets unleashed." The photograph treats everything it shows as completely normal. The viewer discovers the joke.

**Three absurdity layers per image:**
- Layer 1: First glance reads as normal corporate photo (1-2 seconds)
- Layer 2: Primary joke visible within 3 seconds
- Layer 3: 2-4 background details reward close inspection

**The cardinal rule:** Nobody in the frame reacts to the absurdity. Everyone treats everything as Tuesday.

**Brad's Visual DNA (when Brad appears):**
Late 30s white male, purple satin shirt unbuttoned to mid-chest, oversized gold rope chain (comically large, 80s rapper scale), aviator sunglasses (worn indoors), gold signet pinky ring. The only person in the frame who looks like he chose his outfit today.

**Expression rule:** Default is neutral/slightly tired. Nobody reacts to absurdity. Brad is the only character with a broader expression range.

**Text rule:** Keep in-image text SHORT (2-5 words, all caps). Longer text goes to InDesign post-production.

## GENERATION NOTES

### Grid Prefix
Every prompt begins with "Show me a 3x3 grid of" per the established generation workflow. Select the strongest tile from each grid.

### Iteration Strategy
If a grid does not produce a usable tile on first generation:
1. Check which absurdity layers rendered and which did not
2. Strengthen the missing layer's description (more words, more specific placement)
3. Weaken competing elements if the generator is prioritizing the wrong details
4. Re-run with adjusted prompt

### Known Challenges for Photorealistic Style
- **Text rendering:** Short all-caps text (2-5 words) in standard signage positions renders reliably in MJ v6.1. Longer text or unusual placements should go to InDesign post-production.
- **Multiple characters with specific behaviors:** Dense scenes (Prompt 5A) may need multiple generation attempts to get all behaviors visible. Accept "most behaviors present" rather than demanding every single one.
- **Orange jumpsuit in office context:** The generator may resist placing prison clothing in an office. Describe the jumpsuit as a wardrobe element rather than emphasizing its prison origin.
- **Brad's chain:** The "comically large" gold rope chain is the hardest Brad element to render at the right scale. If it renders as normal jewelry, add "extremely thick heavy gold chain that is impossible to miss" to the next iteration.
- **Deadpan expressions:** Photorealistic faces default toward pleasant/neutral. "Slightly tired" and "half-lidded eyes" help push toward the desired flat affect.
- **Brad's tactical vest over satin (6A):** The generator may render only one layer. If the purple satin disappears under the tactical vest, emphasize "clearly visible underneath" or describe the shirt collar/sleeve edges specifically.
- **Hazmat suits (6B):** Full-face respirators sidestep character consistency concerns. If the generator removes respirators to show faces, add "completely sealed suits with no exposed skin or faces" to negatives.
- **Senate hearing room (6D):** The pen-on-witness-table is a challenging scale gag. If the pen renders too large relative to the microphone, add "the pen is the size of a standard ballpoint approximately fifteen centimeters long" to anchor scale.
- **Security checkpoint with four absurd items (8C):** Dense scene with specific items per character. May need 2-3 generations. Accept "three of four items correct" and patch the fourth compositionally if needed.
- **Technical diagrams (9A-9D):** These break the photorealistic container intentionally. The military field manual style is a separate visual language. If the generator drifts toward photorealism, strengthen "technical diagram" and "clean white background" language. If labels garble, all four diagrams have InDesign fallback plans for vector text overlay.
- **Elevator door reflection (10A):** Brushed stainless steel reflections are hit-or-miss. If the reflection doesn't render, the image still works -- the hollow mood carries the scene without it.
- **Break room artifacts at scale (10B):** The overhead angle with multiple small objects may lose detail at 2"x2" print size. Prioritize the lunch container and tally marks as the most legible comedy beats.
- **Hollow expressions (12A):** "Eyes not smiling" is a subtle photorealistic challenge. If the generator renders genuinely pleasant faces, add "polite but hollow" and "the engagement is performative" more prominently. The body language (weight shifted away, foot angled) is more reliable than facial nuance.
- **Brad holding a document (12B):** The generator may render garbled text on the document -- this is expected. The document is composited in InDesign. Only the document SHAPE and Brad's presentation pose matter in the generation.
- **Brad's hero portrait (14A):** This is the highest-stakes single image in the booklet. The cinematic lighting treatment sets it apart from every other image. If the warm directional lighting doesn't render (reverts to flat), strengthen "key light from above and to the left" and "rim light separating subject from dark background."
- **Briefcase dimensions (14A):** The matte black cardboard box may render as a leather briefcase. Add "cardboard construction visible" if needed. The gap between Brad's presentation and the actual product quality IS the joke.

### Diverse Casting
Pages 10-13 feature non-Brad characters. Casting is diverse across spreads -- no two adjacent vignettes should default to the same demographic. Prompts leave specific ethnicity flexible where not plot-critical, but generators should be guided toward variety across the full set.

### Brad Consistency
Brad appears in four prompts total: 2B (market research), 6A (tactical pen drop), 6C (QVC infomercial), 12B (translation guide), and 14A (hero portrait). His visual DNA is identical in all -- purple satin shirt, gold chain, aviators, pinky ring -- but camera treatment varies. In 2B, 6C, and 12B the camera is flat and corporate. In 6A and 14A the camera elevates him cinematically. This contrast is intentional.

### Cinematic Elevation Budget
Only 3 prompts in the entire booklet receive cinematic lighting treatment: 6A (Mission Impossible), 6C (QVC set), and 14A (hero portrait). Every other image uses flat corporate fluorescent. Do not add cinematic treatment to additional images -- the scarcity makes the elevated moments land.
