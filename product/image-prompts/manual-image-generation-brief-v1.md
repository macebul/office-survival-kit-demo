# Office Survival Manual — Image Generation Brief

## Project Purpose

This booklet is the first thing the customer sees when opening the **Office Survival Kit**.

Its job is to:

- immediately establish the world
- make the reader laugh
- make the product feel premium and shareable
- function as an **office survival manual**, not just a storybook
- treat the premise as literal:

> **The office is a prison. This is not a metaphor.**

---

# Core Creative Direction

## Tone

- edgy
- unhinged
- tilted
- absurd
- dark but controlled
- highly shareable
- hilarious over clever
- bureaucratic/manual structure used as the straight man

## Humor Rule

The strongest contrast is:

> **insane office-prison reality described in calm, confident, institutional language**

## Content Rule

Each page/spread should be mostly self-contained, so a reader can open anywhere and still get the joke.

The full read-through should also reward the reader with recurring lore and escalating worldbuilding.

## Readability / Layout

- booklet is small, roughly 5×8 or 6×9
- text must be efficient
- use horizontal space well
- avoid long prose
- avoid over-compressing so much that the joke disappears
- each page/spread should have an actual comedic turn, not just worldbuilding

## Visual Rule

Avoid static, generic images whenever possible.

Images should ideally include:

- action
- tension
- a moment in progress
- visual contradiction
- aftermath
- evidence
- weird corporate normalcy

The image should not merely illustrate the joke.

It should help deliver the punchline.

# Modules Currently Refined

## Module 1 — This Was Never an Office

### Status

Locked conceptually with working copy.

### Title

**THIS WAS NEVER AN OFFICE**

### Purpose

Open the manual by immediately establishing the premise: the office is a prison disguised as a workplace.

### Core Joke

The office did not hide the cage.

It furnished it.

### Locked Working Copy

**THIS WAS NEVER AN OFFICE**

You thought it was work  
because they gave it carpet.

The cage was never hidden.

It just matched  
the furniture.

Prisons use bars.  
Offices use beige.

Both know exactly  
where you are.

### Notes

Avoid weaker lines such as:

- “That was the trick.”
- “Welcome to the sentence.”

Those were rejected.

### Image Direction

Need **1 image** for this module.

#### Image concept

A bland beige office/cubicle environment that subtly reads like a disguised cell. It should show the **moment of recognition**.

Possible visual traits:

- cubicle walls subtly reading like bars
- beige furniture creating “furnished cage” logic
- normal office scene at first glance
- on second glance, it feels institutional / prison-like
- a worker realizing something is wrong / seeing through the disguise

The image should support:

> The cage was never hidden. It just matched the furniture.

## Module 2 — Cell Assignment

### Status

Locked conceptually with working copy direction.

### Title

**CELL ASSIGNMENT**

### Purpose

Show that the reader is not being given a desk.

They are being assigned a cell with history.

### Important Note About the Chair

The chair is meaningful **as the sit-down trigger**, not because the chair itself matters as an object.

The joke is: the reader sits down before they understand the danger.

### Working Spread Structure

This module works as a **2-page spread**.

### Working Copy — Left Page

**CELL ASSIGNMENT**

They walk you to your desk.  
You sit down.

That was Karen’s chair.  
Karen was a snitch.

### Working Copy — Right Page

They found her badge  
in the parking garage.

They never found Karen.

Dave has her parking spot,  
her mug, and her cat.

Dave seems relaxed about it.

Don’t be Karen.

### Notes

- “Your first day” was removed because it isolated the reader too much.
- The Karen/Dave/disappearance lore is important and should be preserved.
- “Don’t be Karen.” is preferred over “Don’t be a Karen.”

### Image Direction

Need **2 images** for this spread, one per page.

#### Left page image concept

The assignment / sit-down moment.

- someone being shown to a desk/cubicle
- mid-sit or just sitting down
- subtle tension
- nearby coworkers watching too quietly
- the chair/desk has history
- subtle Karen residue / traces are okay
- this image should support the reveal: **That was Karen’s chair.**

#### Right page image concept

The aftermath / evidence / suspicious normalcy.

- Dave too comfortable
- Karen’s mug / cat / parking spot energy
- missing-person flyer is strong
- office behaving as if nothing unusual happened
- image should support:
  - Karen is gone
  - Dave inherited too much
  - everyone is weirdly fine with it

## Module 3 — Cell Law

### Status

Locked with working copy direction.

### Title

**CELL LAW**

### Purpose

Teach the reader that once they have been assigned a cell, they must defend it.

### Core Joke

The company calls it a workstation.

That’s cute.

A workstation does not need territorial markings.

A cell does.

### Working Copy

**CELL LAW**

They call it  
your workstation.

Adorable.

Workstations don’t need  
territorial markings.

Cells do.

Mug: nerve agent.  
Drawer: contraband storage.  
Pen: expense-approved shank.

If a hand crosses the wall,  
that’s not borrowing.

That’s a breach.

Act accordingly.

### Notes

- This module needed to feel more possessive / territorial / “mine.”
- Earlier versions that felt too clever, too objective, or too political were rejected.
- The final ending **must stay active**:

> That’s a breach. Act accordingly.

- “File nothing, remember everything.” was rejected.

### Image Direction

Need **2 images** for this spread, one per page.

#### Left page image concept

Show why this is not a normal workstation.

- desk/cubicle visibly marked as defended territory
- clear territorial markings
- labels, warnings, boundary energy
- could include mug placement, tape lines, warning markers, drawer warnings, etc.
- the image should justify:

> Workstations don’t need territorial markings. Cells do.

#### Right page image concept

Show the breach.

- a coworker’s hand crossing into the cubicle territory
- reaching for mug / pen / drawer / desk territory
- the occupant noticing calmly but dangerously
- pen visually framed as the “expense-approved shank”
- this image should justify:

> If a hand crosses the wall, that’s not borrowing. That’s a breach. Act accordingly.

## Module 4 — Case the Floor

### Core idea

Learn the block. Recon the space. Identify exits, blind spots, unsafe zones, lifers, snitches, and choke points.

## Module 5 — The Yard

### Core idea

The break room is prison-yard territory:

- food theft
- hierarchy
- yard law
- fridge law
- microwave offenses
- coffee politics

### Main lessons

- Know what is yours.
- Know what is not yours.
- Know who runs the room.
- Never violate yard law publicly.

## Module 6 — Contraband / Supply Law

### Core idea

Office supplies are not supplies.

They are contraband, leverage, comfort, and survival assets.

## Module 7A — Bait Language

### Core idea

Harmless phrases are traps.

Examples:

- Got a sec?
- Quick question.
- Can I grab you?
- Do you have bandwidth?

## Module 7B — Paperwork Violence

### Core idea

The paper trail has teeth.

Examples:

- Per my last email.
- Just to clarify.
- Looping you in.
- For visibility.
- As discussed.

## Module 8A — Snitches & Lifers

### Core idea

Learn functions, not names.

Suggested archetypes:

- The Snitch
- The Lifer
- The Optimist
- The Ghost

## Module 8B — Floor Politics

### Core idea

The org chart is fake. Learn the real power structure.

Suggested figures:

- The Yard Boss
- The Meeting Summoner
- The Gatekeeper
- HR-Adjacent

## Module 9 — The Walls Talk

### Core idea

Motivational posters and office signs are propaganda.

Working secondary line direction:

- Posters are orders with better fonts.

## Module 10 — Lockdown Procedure

### Core idea

Something happened.

Now the institution takes over.

This is about:

- witness control
- rumor control
- official messaging
- narrative management
- lockdown rules

Secondary phrase:

- The Official Story

## Module 11 — Authorized Contraband

### Core idea

How to deploy your illegal-but-approved survival assets when the sentence escalates.

Important note:

This should **not** be a static product catalog spread.

## Module 12 — Release Terms

### Core idea

Close with fake legal / institutional fine print.

It should feel like:

> the manual ends, but the world does not.

Preferred title:

**RELEASE TERMS**

Possible sub-line:

- Parole not guaranteed.
