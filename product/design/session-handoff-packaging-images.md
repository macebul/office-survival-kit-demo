## THE WORLD RULES (READ FIRST — NON-NEGOTIABLE)

**The office is a prison. This is not a metaphor.** In this universe, the comparison doesn't exist because there's nothing to compare — the office just IS this way. The language of incarceration is the language of corporate life. It's the water everyone swims in.

**The world is played straight.** Nobody winks. Nobody says "get it?" The moment anyone acknowledges the comparison AS a comparison, the world breaks.

**Shared vocabulary (used naturally, never explained):**
- Doing time / serving your sentence = Working at a company
- Your cell = Your cubicle or desk
- The yard = Break room, common areas
- Contraband = Anything that makes the day bearable
- Lifers = Long-tenured employees
- Release day = Last day at a job
- The warden = Upper management, HR, the boss
- Intake / processing = Onboarding
- Commissary = Vending machines, office supply closet
- Shank / shiv = Your pen
- Ink = Tattoos marking time served
- Turf = Your desk, claimed spaces

## BRAD STERLING — THE TWO MODES

Brad is the fictional founder of Elevate Co. He operates in two modes:

### Mode 1: Product Copy Brad
Competent. Sardonic. Institutional format played straight with escalating absurd content. Dry, never winks. Format is the straight man, content is the comedian. The reader laughs WITH this copy.

**Examples:**
- "PROPERTY OF: NOBODY ANYMORE"
- "Serving Size: 1 Monday / Servings Per Container: Too Many"
- "Not a significant source of work-life balance, upward mobility, or the feeling that any of this matters."

### Mode 2: Personal Brand Brad
Delusional hustle-culture LinkedIn post given physical form. 100% sincere. Uses words like "vision," "disrupt," "elevate," "curate," "premium." Refers to himself in third person. Purple satin shirt, gold rope chain, aviators, pinky ring.

**The gap between Mode 1 and Mode 2 is the joke.** The better the product copy is, the more absurd Brad's personal brand becomes by contrast.

## THE MAGAZINE VOICE (FOR EDITORIAL / MAGAZINE IMAGERY)

The magazine voice is **enthusiastic, upbeat, playful, persuasive** — a willing, polished participant in the insanity. Think of a magazine editor who is THRILLED about every piece of content. The comedy engine: **everyone is aggressively enthusiastic about the horror.**

The tone is **sardonic, not cynical.** The people in this world haven't given up — they've adapted. "This is absurd, we all know it's absurd, and here's how we get through it together." That warmth underneath makes it a gift someone gives to a coworker they actually like.

## THE UPGRADE SPREAD (PP. 14-15) — WHERE WE ARE NOW

We're actively building "The Upgrade" — a two-page spread comparing a prison cell (6x9 ft) to an office cubicle (6x8 ft), presented as a completely serious magazine property comparison. Like HGTV's "Which Home Should They Choose?" or a Zillow side-by-side.

### Layout (current):
- **Page 14 (left/image page):** Full-bleed comparison image with section label ("THE UPGRADE"), headline (TBD — placeholder is "COMPARABLE LISTINGS"), and deck ("Two Floor Plans. One Winner. Not the One You'd Expect.") overlaid with dark overlay + white text.
- **Page 15 (right/text page):** Editorial body, snippet note, 3-column specs table (Cell | Cubicle | Elevate's Take), verdict callout, Brad quote, DID YOU KNOW stat, kicker.

### The comparison image concept:
Side-by-side (or diagonally split) showing a prison cell and an office cubicle, both photographed and staged like aspirational real estate listings. Same warm lighting, same "listing photo" energy. The cell should arguably look slightly nicer (made bed, window, tidy shelf). The cubicle is generic and soulless but staged to look inviting. Wide aspect ratio for a two-page spread (2:1 or 16:9).

### Key creative decisions already made:
- Cell is 6x9 (death row standard), cubicle is 6x8
- The magazine is an enthusiastic real estate agent who genuinely believes the cubicle spin
- Three-column specs table format (Cell | Cubicle | Elevate's Take) — punchlines live in the third column
- Commute-as-freedom is the verdict tiebreaker angle ("Nobody can schedule a meeting during your commute. For forty-seven minutes, no one is telling you anything.")
- Brad quote: "People told me a closet wasn't an office. I said, 'Not with that attitude.'"
- DID YOU KNOW: 54 vs 12 pages of paperwork
- Kicker: "A cubicle is 6x8. A cell is 6x9. We like to think of it as an upgrade."

### Copy still being refined:
- Headline needs replacement (can't use "CHOOSE YOUR 6x8" because cell is 6x9)
- Specs table cells being trimmed for space — currently too wordy for the row heights
- Verdict callout needs major cut (currently 5 paragraphs, needs to be 3-4 sentences)
- Everything is being reduced to prevent wall-of-text on the text page

## IMAGE PROMPT FORMAT (CRITICAL — FOLLOW EXACTLY)

### For Magazine Images (Midjourney v6.1)

**Format rules:**
- One continuous paragraph for the scene description
- `--no` block is identical across ALL prompts (copy-paste, never modify)
- Always include `--s 250 --v 6.1`
- Aspect ratio varies per image need
- People (other than Brad/named characters) should use varying genders and races

### Standard --no Block (use on ALL magazine image prompts)

```
--no cartoon, illustration, drawing, painting, sketch, anime, manga, chibi, stylized, flat color, cel shading, vector art, mid-century modern, geometric faces, angular faces, pointed noses, exaggerated proportions, caricature, waxy skin, plastic skin, airbrushed skin, unnaturally smooth skin, symmetrical face, perfect features, model-beautiful, glamour lighting, fashion photography, Instagram filter, color grading, teal and orange, oversaturated, HDR, lens flare, vignette, film grain for style, collage, composite, split screen, floating elements, text overlay, watermark, stock photo watermark, Getty Images watermark
```

### Standard Trailing Params
```
--s 250 --v 6.1
```

### Aspect Ratios by Use
| Use | AR |
|---|---|
| Hero spread image | `--ar 2:1` or `--ar 16:9` |
| Hero listing photo | `--ar 4:3` |
| Square detail/vignette | `--ar 1:1` |
| Supporting medium image | `--ar 3:2` |
| Full page portrait | `--ar 3:4` |

### Real Estate Listing Style Prompts (relevant for packaging)

For any image that needs to look like a property listing photo:
- Warm inviting color grading applied to make sterile environments look cozy and desirable
- Slightly wide angle lens with centered composition
- Bright clean professional real estate photography style
- The staging is the joke — the listing agent has gone too far

**Example (cubicle hero listing photo from pp. 4-7):**
```
Show me a 3x3 grid of a photorealistic real estate listing photograph of a single office cubicle workstation shot from the opening of the cubicle looking in as though this is the front door of a studio apartment, the cubicle has short beige fabric-covered partition walls approximately five feet tall, a standard corporate desk with a computer monitor keyboard and mouse, an ergonomic office task chair in black mesh, a single overhead fluorescent panel light illuminating the small workspace evenly, industrial gray carpet tile floor, one small personal filing cabinet tucked under the desk, the cubicle is clean and tidy but completely generic and soulless with no personal items or decorations, neighboring cubicle partition walls are visible above and to the sides showing this is one unit in a large open office floor, warm inviting color grading has been applied to make the sterile corporate environment look cozy and desirable as though this is a charming studio listing on Zillow, slightly wide angle lens with centered composition, bright clean professional real estate photography style --no [standard block] --ar 4:3 --s 250 --v 6.1 --grid
```

## LESSONS LEARNED — IMAGE GENERATION

### What Works
- Each image needs something absurd/funny happening — not just a well-photographed mundane thing
- Don't just illustrate the joke in the caption — the image should have its own visual joke
- Find the middle ground: "absurd in commitment to the bit," not dark/edgy or over-the-top
- The listing agent has gone too far staging photos — that's the comedy frame for real estate imagery
- Three absurdity layers per image: (1) first glance reads as normal, (2) primary joke visible within 3 seconds, (3) 2-4 background details reward close inspection

### What to Avoid
- Nobody in the frame reacts to the absurdity. Everyone treats everything as Tuesday.
- Add "brand names, logos, brand labels" to negative prompts when food/drink items are present
- When items need to fit in a space, make sure they physically make sense
- Don't go dark or edgy — the tone is warm comedy, not horror
- No puns in visual gags

### Naming Convention
- Names should sound like branded amenity names a coworking space would use (positive, optimistic)
- Names should connect to prison vocabulary when possible
- Two-word names work best: "Panic Pod," "Turf Tags," "Lifer Ink"
- The comedy formula: aspirational language + mundane/awful reality = joke
