# Image Direction

Each deck needs two images: the Brad box (closed/product shot) and a sample card showing front and back.

## Rap Sheets — Image Concept
- **Box shot:** Brad's matte white box, dark sans-serif "Interpersonal Feedback Delivery System™" with gold accent line. Sitting slightly askew in the tray like it's been rifled through.
- **Sample card — front:** An official-looking infraction notice. Goldenrod or off-white card stock feel. Bold header, a severity checkbox or threat level indicator, and 2–3 lines max stating the violation. Think parking ticket energy.
- **Sample card — back:** The penalty. Same bureaucratic format, but the recommended action is absurd. Short enough to read in 3 seconds.
- **Overall vibe:** Institutional, authoritative, looks like it came out of an HR department that's lost its mind.

## Sweet Nothings — Image Concept
- **Box shot:** Brad's matte white box, "Ambient Communication Cards™" with gold accent. Maybe a single card peeking out of the box slightly.
- **Sample card — front:** Warm, friendly, almost greeting-card energy. Softer typography, maybe a gentle color or a small decorative element. The message on the front reads as genuinely kind. Think of the nicest card in a Hallmark aisle.
- **Sample card — back:** The tone shifts. Same card, but the back delivers the real message. Could be more stripped-down typography, more direct, or formatted like a translation/decoder. The visual contrast between front and back IS the joke.
- **Overall vibe:** The front should be disarmingly pleasant. The back should make you say "oh no."

## Cell Talk — Image Concept
- **Box shot:** Brad's matte white box, "Self-Actualization Protocol™ Cards" with gold accent. Clean, aspirational-looking.
- **Sample card — front:** Looks like a real affirmation card. Clean, centered typography. Maybe a calming design element — a border, a simple motif. The format says "wellness." Reads like something you'd find in a meditation app.
- **Sample card — back:** Same format, but the affirmation content is darkly funny. The visual design stays sincere — the card never winks. The comedy comes entirely from the gap between the earnest presentation and the bleak content.
- **Overall vibe:** Wellness industry aesthetic on the outside, existential crisis on the inside.

# Card Format Protocol — For Future Card Development

## Universal Rules (All Three Decks)
- **Card size:** 4" × 2.5" — roughly business card proportions
- **Double-sided:** Front is the setup, back is the punchline
- **Word budget:** Each side should read in under 5 seconds. If it takes longer, cut it.
- **The flip is sacred:** The back must reframe, subvert, or escalate what the front established. If the back is just "more of the front," the card fails.
- **Authority voice never breaks:** The card never winks at the audience. The format stays completely straight-faced. The comedy comes from the gap between format and content, not from the card acknowledging it's funny.

## Variety Within Each Deck
- Don't use the same structural template for every card in a deck
- Mix fully pre-printed cards with fill-in-the-blank cards (where fill-ins genuinely add something)
- Occasional use of illustration, diagrams, or visual elements where they elevate the joke
- OG handwriting/margin notes ONLY when it genuinely makes the card funnier — not as a default
- Goal: every 3rd or 4th card should feel structurally different from the one before it

## Deck-Specific Protocols

### Rap Sheets (Complaint Cards)
- **Front:** The offense — stated with bureaucratic precision
- **Back:** The penalty — disproportionate, disturbingly specific, or mirrors the crime at absurd scale
- **Variety formats available:** Standard citation, fill-in complaint form, fake award that's actually a complaint, evidence file/crime scene, multi-count indictment
- **Comedy rule:** Specificity over generality. Observed over invented. The more precise the detail, the funnier it reads.
- **Avoid:** The top 5 most-used office jokes (fish microwave, reply all, stolen lunch, etc.) unless the subversion is exceptional

### Sweet Nothings (Passive-Aggressive Cards)
- **Front:** Something genuinely pleasant — a compliment, a thank-you, encouragement, congratulations
- **Back:** The real meaning — what you're actually communicating underneath the politeness
- **Variety formats available:** Greeting card style, office memo, handwritten note, formal recognition, email printout
- **Comedy rule:** The front must be believable as sincere. If the front is already obviously sarcastic, the flip has no power. The wider the gap between surface and subtext, the funnier the reveal.
- **The test:** Could someone read just the front and think it was nice? If yes, the setup works.

### Cell Talk (Affirmation Cards)
- **Front:** A real-looking affirmation — centered, calm, sincere typography
- **Back:** The affirmation content — formatted like genuine self-help, but the substance is dark, defeated, or uncomfortably honest
- **Variety formats available:** Classic affirmation format, daily mantra, guided breathing/meditation prompt, inspirational quote format, morning checklist
- **Comedy rule:** The format stays earnest. The content is the subversion. These should feel like affirmation cards from a parallel universe where self-help books are written by people who've given up.
- **Note:** Some of these can be single-sided if the affirmation is strong enough to land without a flip. Not every card needs the reveal — sometimes the front IS the joke.
