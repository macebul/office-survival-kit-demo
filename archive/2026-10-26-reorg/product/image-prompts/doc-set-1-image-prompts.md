### PROMPT 2A — Vignette 1: The Intake

**Purpose:** The top image on Page 2. Shows a new employee being processed on their first day at work. The scene should read as both standard corporate onboarding AND prison intake simultaneously — without labeling it as either. The employee is being processed by the system. The mood is institutional, clinical, slightly dehumanizing.

> A stylized editorial illustration of a new employee's first day at a corporate office, rendered in a polished, slightly exaggerated style with warm muted tones on an off-white textured paper background. The scene shows a bewildered new hire — a man or woman in business casual, looking overwhelmed and slightly dazed — being processed through onboarding. An HR representative stands beside them with a clipboard, efficient and unsmiling, placing a lanyard with an ID badge around the new hire's neck like it's a collar being fastened. On a table nearby: a thick employee handbook (absurdly heavy-looking, possibly chained or padlocked for comedic effect), a stack of forms, and a cheap branded pen. The new hire's expression is the look of someone who just realized what they've signed up for — not panic, but the quiet resignation of intake. Behind them, other new hires wait in a line, each holding their own handbooks, each with the same expression. The lighting is flat fluorescent — institutional, not warm. The overall feel should be recognizable to anyone who's started a corporate job: the badge photo station, the paperwork assembly line, the forced smile. But the composition should also subtly evoke processing — the line, the ID photo, the cataloguing. Clean, polished art style — NOT hand-drawn or sketchy. Think editorial illustration for a premium publication. --ar 5:4

## PAGES 6-7 — "The Cubicle Shank Origin Story" | PHASE 2: DIAGNOSIS

### Spread Layout

Two-page spread. Four vignettes total across both pages, all organized around a single comedy engine: **the pen stays exactly the same while the institutional response around it escalates to preposterous scale.** Copy beat anchor: *"I didn't leave a pen. I left a cubicle shank."*

**Design note:** All four vignettes feature the same slim translucent-blue Bic-style ballpoint pen. The pen NEVER changes size, color, or detail between images. The joke lives entirely in how the world around it overreacts.

### Comedy Beats (one per vignette)

- **V1** — Action movie / Mission Impossible: pen drop requires tactical rappel from ceiling.
- **V2** — Crisis response / hazmat: pen triggers full biohazard containment protocol.
- **V3** — Commerce / QVC infomercial: pen sold on home shopping with Brad as pitchman.
- **V4** — Institutional gravity / Senate hearing: pen subpoenaed to testify before Congress.

All four use the locked Nano Banana 2 format (grid prefix, mid-century modern style anchor, red-nose negatives, trailing params). V1 and V3 feature Brad; V2 and V4 are Brad-free for visual variety.
