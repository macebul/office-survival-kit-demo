## THE VOICE — THIS IS CRITICAL

### What the voice IS

A **warm, cheerful, slightly conspiratorial friend** who is genuinely excited to sell you something ridiculous. Think: your fun, slightly irresponsible buddy who found something amazing and can't wait to show you.

The brand **KNOWS what it's selling.** It found a loophole. It's cheerfully proud. It's not confused, coy, or playing dumb.

**Tone markers:** "Look," "Seriously," "Trust us," "We're not going to ask," "We believe in you," "Which is to say." The brand talks TO you, not AT you.

### What the voice is NOT

- NOT dry, clinical, or neutral
- NOT professional or corporate-sounding
- NOT a one-liner comedian rattling off disconnected jokes
- NOT confused about its own product
- NOT instructing the reader to be threatening
- NOT observational office humor ("meetings suck," "survived another Monday")

### The peppiness requirement

The copy needs enthusiasm, warmth, personality, levity, and a casual conversational delivery. It should sound like someone TALKING to you, not a copywriter crafting clever lines.

**TOO NEUTRAL:** "Won't bleed through paper. Can't speak for anything else."
**CLOSER TO RIGHT:** "Other pens leak in your pocket. This one doesn't leak anywhere. Not in your pocket. Not in your hand. Not in Kevin. Completely leak-proof design."

## THE 7 MECHANICAL RULES

1. **Joke lands on the LAST word or LAST phrase.** Nothing after the punchline.
2. **Setup is a REAL product copy format.** Feature bullet, spec, disclaimer, FAQ, care instruction, etc.
3. **Punchline word must be CONCRETE and UNCOMFORTABLE.** Not abstract concepts. Specific nouns, actions, situations.
4. **Talk TO the buyer.** "Your ___." "You'll ___." Make the reader complicit.
5. **Be CHEERFUL about something you shouldn't be cheerful about.** Warm, enthusiastic about something alarming.
6. **Never instruct the reader to be threatening.** Describe the product so the threat is OBVIOUS without directing it.
7. **Rule of three when using lists.** Two normal items, third breaks the pattern with a REAL THING.

### Additional mechanics
- Strongest lines are 5-13 words
- Ratio: ~60-70% normal product copy, 30-40% absurd
- The second sentence makes the first worse
- Short beats long. 5 words max per chart cell, ideally 3 or fewer.

## DESIGN PRINCIPLES (established for this product)

1. **Content-first, dimensions later.** Write the best material. The box adapts.
2. **Every piece of text earns a laugh.** No dead space, no identity-only copy.
3. **Reversals.** Set up one expectation, deliver something else. Misdirection, not straight-line setups.
4. **Short beats long.** Especially in chart cells and small copy blocks.
5. **Specificity over concepts.** Concrete nouns, specific details, visual images.
6. **The professional packaging design IS the comedy technique.** The box looks like a real product. The copy inside the format is where the humor lives.

## WHAT NOT TO DO

- No puns. Brad is dry.
- No exclamation points in product copy. Keep it institutional and flat.
- No winking at the reader. No "get it?"
- No actual workplace advice.
- No making the brand self-aware about the joke.
- No generic insults — every insult needs absurd specificity.
- No buttons/tags/reactions after the punchline.
- No abstract/corporate punchline words ("concerns," "responsibility")
- No observational office humor ("meetings suck")
- No three items from the same category in a rule-of-three
- Don't lead with violence and pivot to product — lead with product
- Don't repeat the same structural trick across variations

## HOW TO WORK

1. Test every line against the 7 mechanical rules before presenting it.
2. If a line doesn't pass, kill it. Don't present weak lines.
3. Before presenting anything, ask: "Does this sound like the Shart Kit? Would it fit on Dehydrated Water packaging?" If not, rewrite.
4. Vary the angle and comedy mechanic across variations. If three subtitle options all use the same word trick, you need different approaches.
5. Keep it dark. "BATHES IN RED" dark. The trick is dark content delivered with warm, enthusiastic energy.
6. Every line should sound like someone TALKING to you, not a copywriter crafting lines.
7. Label each variation with a brief note on its comedy mechanic so reviewers understand what each one is doing differently.
