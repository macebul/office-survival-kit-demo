# Design Brief: Trays (Interior Stacking Trays)

> **Surface:** Two identical stacking trays inside the Office Survival Kit briefcase
> **Project Type:** Packaging (interior surface)
> **Complexity:** Moderate

---

## 1. GOAL

### What This Design Must Accomplish

- Make cheap novelty items feel like precision tactical equipment through Pelican-case die-cut presentation
- Deliver two distinct "reveal moments" in the unboxing sequence (Tray 1 after Doc Set 1, Tray 2 after Tray 1)
- Sustain the prison-office world-building through surface copy, color, and recess treatment
- Create a comedy payoff in the confiscated stapler recess (the absence IS the joke)
- Maintain premium/tactical perception — the trays are the primary vehicle for "this gag gift feels expensive"

### Goal Chain

- **Business goal:** Justify the kit's price point through perceived quality
- **Experience goal:** Each tray lift is a physical moment — anticipation, reveal, discovery
- **Comedy goal:** Brad Sterling's voice lives on the tray surfaces; the confiscated stapler recess is a standalone joke; recess floors hide comedy under every item
- **Manufacturing goal:** Single mold for both trays — identical die-cut, print runs may differ

### The Gap

- Without the trays, items are just loose novelties in a box — the Pelican-case aesthetic is what transforms them into "tactical office equipment"
- The tray surfaces are the connective tissue between individual items — they frame the world

---

## 2. AUDIENCE

### Primary Audience (Buyer)

- Gift-givers shopping for a coworker, friend, or office white elephant
- Looking for something that looks premium enough to justify the price and gets a reaction on opening

### Secondary Audience (User/Recipient)

- The person unboxing the kit
- They experience the trays as physical interactions — lifting, discovering, reading
- They will notice the details: the recess treatment, the copy, the hidden jokes on recess floors and tray undersides

### What the Audience Expects

- Buyer expects: looks worth the money, feels like a real product (not a cheap gag)
- Recipient expects: surprise, escalation, discovery — each layer reveals something new
- Both expect: the comedy comes from the contrast between serious presentation and absurd content

---

## 3. CONTEXT

### Physical Context

- **Quantity:** 2 identical trays (single die-cut mold)
- **Exterior dimensions:** ~13.5" x ~6.5" (working estimate)
- **Wall height:** ~2.0"
- **Material:** Paperboard with die-cut recesses
- **Color scheme:** Dark charcoal base, prison orange accents, white copy text

### Recess Layout (Same Mold, Both Trays)

| Zone | Description |
|------|-------------|
| Front recess (narrow) | Holds pen box (Tray 1) / empty stapler recess (Tray 2) — same size, same mold |
| Back recess (wide, shared) | Holds 2 item boxes side-by-side in Tray 1, 3 card deck boxes side-by-side in Tray 2 |

### Tray Contents by Position

| Tray | Front Recess (Narrow) | Back Recess (Wide, Shared) |
|------|----------------------|---------------------------|
| Tray 1 (top) | Cubicle Shank (pen + matchbox box) | Lifer Ink (tattoos) + Turf Tags (stickers) |
| Tray 2 (bottom) | Confiscated Stapler (empty — chalk outline + label) | Cell Talk + Rap Sheets + Sweet Nothings (3 card deck boxes) |

### Lift Mechanism

- Side cutout holes on the short sides (left and right walls) — one per side
- No front finger notch, no back-edge tabs
- Cutouts should feel intentional and clean

### Recess Floor Treatment

- Universal approach: outline the entire recess shape with comedy copy that works regardless of which items sit in that tray position
- Comedy lives under the items — a hidden discovery when someone lifts an item box out
- Silhouette treatment inside recesses

### Tray Surface (Between Recesses)

- Medium copy density
- Real item names near recesses (not equipment codes — the names are funny enough)
- A couple of Brad Sterling quips scattered on the surface
- Priority: looks premium and high-quality — too much copy undermines the tactical aesthetic

### Confiscated Stapler Recess (Tray 2, Front)

- Chalk outline of a stapler silhouette
- Institutional label (minimum: "CONFISCATED" or equivalent)
- If space allows: expand to evidence-tag treatment (case number, date, reason for confiscation, Brad commentary)
- This recess has $0 COGS — the joke is pure profit

### Tray Underside (Easter Egg)

- Hidden message on the bottom face of each tray
- Tone: mix of Brad Sterling personal branding + institutional/prison flavor
- Should be funny and an enjoyable find — rewards the person who flips the tray over
- Brad inserting himself into institutional formats (e.g., Brad-as-warden, Brad's personal stamp of approval, inspection notice with Brad's commentary)

### Brand Voice on This Surface

- **Character:** Brad Sterling (B.S.) — sole voice
- **Two modes:** (1) competent product copy near item labels, (2) delusional personal branding in quips and Easter eggs
- **Comedy rule:** Format stays buttoned-up, content gets unleashed
- **Tone:** Sardonic, not cynical. Warm.

### Experience Sequence

1. Recipient lifts Doc Set 1 off the top
2. **Tray 1 revealed** — first "premium tactical equipment" moment, items visible in Pelican-case recesses
3. Recipient removes item boxes from Tray 1, discovers recess-floor comedy underneath
4. Recipient lifts Tray 1 using side cutouts
5. **Tray 2 revealed** — second wave of items + the confiscated stapler recess (immediate visual joke)
6. Recipient removes item boxes from Tray 2, discovers more recess-floor comedy
7. Recipient may flip trays over — Easter egg discovery

### Attention > Message > Action

| Step | What must happen |
|------|-----------------|
| **Attention** | Dark charcoal + orange recesses immediately read as "tactical case" — premium perception established on first sight |
| **Message** | Item names and Brad copy reinforce the prison-office world; confiscated stapler recess delivers a standalone joke; recess floors reward curiosity |
| **Action** | Recipient interacts — lifts boxes, reads copy, flips trays, shares the discoveries with whoever is watching |

---

## 4. CONSTRAINTS & UNKNOWNS

### Constraints

- Single mold for both trays — identical die-cut layout
- Recess-floor comedy must be universal (same mold means same recess shapes, different items per tray)
- Medium copy density — premium look takes priority over joke density
- Dark charcoal + prison orange + white copy — established color scheme, no deviation
- Side cutout holes for lifting — must be clean and ergonomic

### Unknowns

- Final exterior dimensions (~13.5" x ~6.5" is a working estimate)
- Exact recess depths and wall thicknesses
- Whether print-per-tray differentiation is feasible (same mold, but can each tray have different print?)
- Available surface area between recesses for copy placement
- Final item box dimensions (affects recess sizing and how much floor is visible)
- Confiscated stapler recess: how much space is available for evidence-tag expansion beyond baseline chalk outline + label

# Recommended Approach

## Strategic Direction

**The trays are a stage, not a container.** Every design decision should serve the theatrical reveal — making cheap novelty items look like they belong in a $500 tactical case. The comedy comes from the gap between that serious presentation and the absurd contents.

## Design Priorities (Ranked)

1. **Pelican-case recess fidelity** — the die-cut edges, the dark charcoal wells, the precision fit. This is 70% of the premium perception. Get this right and everything else is polish.
2. **Confiscated stapler recess** — the single strongest comedy beat on the trays. Chalk outline + label at minimum, evidence-tag treatment if space allows. This should make someone laugh before they even touch anything.
3. **Surface copy (medium density)** — real item names near recesses, 1-2 Brad quips per tray. Restraint here is what keeps it feeling premium.
4. **Recess-floor comedy** — universal jokes/silhouettes that reward the person who lifts items out. Secondary discovery layer.
5. **Underside Easter egg** — Brad-meets-institutional hidden message. Tertiary discovery layer for the person who flips things over.

## Action Plan

### Phase 1: Structural Layout
- Finalize the single-mold recess layout with exact dimensions
- Design the side cutout holes — shape, size, edge treatment
- Confirm recess wall angles and edge radii for the Pelican-case look
- Determine how much printable surface exists between recesses

### Phase 2: Surface Design
- Design the tray top surface — item name placement, Brad quip placement, any orange accent lines or framing elements
- Keep copy sparse enough that the dark charcoal breathes — negative space is part of the premium feel
- Determine if both trays share identical surface printing or if print differentiation per tray is feasible and worthwhile

### Phase 3: Recess Floor Treatment
- Design the universal recess outline/silhouette approach — comedy that works regardless of which item sits in the recess
- Write the copy for each recess floor position
- Test readability against the dark charcoal at recess depth

### Phase 4: Confiscated Stapler Recess
- Design the chalk outline silhouette (stapler shape in the narrow front recess)
- Write the institutional label / evidence-tag copy
- Determine how much of the evidence-tag expansion fits given available space
- This is a hero design moment — give it focused attention

### Phase 5: Easter Eggs
- Write the tray underside hidden message(s)
- Tone: Brad inserting himself into institutional formats — funny, rewarding to find
- Can be identical on both trays or differentiated if print allows

### Phase 6: Prototyping & Validation
- Mock up flat tray layout at actual scale to verify copy fits
- Test the reveal sequence physically — does each tray lift feel intentional?
- Verify recess-floor comedy is actually visible when item boxes are removed
- Confirm confiscated stapler recess reads as immediately funny without explanation

## Key Design Principles for This Surface

- **Restraint = premium.** The temptation will be to fill the surface with jokes. Resist. Medium density. Let the recesses, color, and material do the heavy lifting.
- **Universal recess comedy.** Since both trays share one mold, recess-floor jokes must work for any item. Think "institutional storage" humor rather than item-specific references.
- **The stapler recess is a hero moment.** Give it disproportionate design attention relative to its surface area. It's the single biggest comedy beat on the trays.
- **Side cutouts should feel engineered.** Clean edges, intentional shape — they're part of the tactical aesthetic, not just functional holes.
- **The underside Easter egg should feel like a secret.** Not announced, not hinted at — just there for whoever turns it over.
