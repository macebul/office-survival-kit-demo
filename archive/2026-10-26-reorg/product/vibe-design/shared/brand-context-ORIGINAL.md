## The World

**The office is a prison. This is not a metaphor.** Nobody thinks it's clever to compare the office to prison. The comparison doesn't exist because there's nothing to compare — the office just IS this way. You serve your sentence. You do your time. You survive your shift.

The vocabulary of incarceration is the default language for everything in this product. It's not reserved for punchlines — it's the water everyone swims in.

### Core Vocabulary

| Term | Meaning | Tone |
|------|---------|------|
| Doing time / serving your sentence | Working at a company | Matter-of-fact |
| Your cell | Cubicle or desk | Normal |
| The yard | Break room, common areas | Normal |
| Mess hall | Break room / kitchen | Normal |
| Solitary | Working alone, headphones on, bathroom hiding | Normal |
| Contraband | Anything that makes the day bearable | Normal |
| Lifers | Long-tenured employees | Respectful |
| Release day | Last day, retirement, quitting | Aspirational |
| The warden | Upper management, HR, the boss | Normal |
| Intake / processing | Onboarding | Normal |
| Commissary | Vending machines, office supply closet | Normal |
| Shank / shiv | Your pen, your reliable tool | Affectionate |
| Ink | Tattoos marking time served | Earned |
| Turf | Your desk, your claimed spaces | Territorial |
| Rap sheet | Performance review history | Dreaded |

This vocabulary is used everywhere — item names, packaging copy, product descriptions. Stepping outside it should feel like stepping out of character. New terms should emerge naturally; the test is: would someone in this world use it without thinking twice?

### Tone

**Sardonic, not cynical.** People haven't given up — they've adapted. The attitude is: "this is absurd, we all know it's absurd, and here's how we get through it together." There's warmth under the dark humor. That's what makes this a gift someone gives to a coworker they actually like. It's validating, not mean.

### The World Is Played Straight

Nobody comments on the office-as-prison framing. Nobody winks. Nobody says "get it?" The moment anyone acknowledges it as a comparison, the world breaks. Absurdity comes from specific details within the premise, not from the premise itself.

## The Character: Brad Sterling

**Role:** Creator of all product packaging. Sole voice across every surface of the product.
**Company:** Elevate Co. — Workplace Solutions Division
**Title:** Chief Vision Officer & Founder (self-appointed)
**Initials:** B.S.

Everything in the product — outer box, briefcase, interior design, item names, item containers, booklets, copy — is Brad's creation and Elevate Co.'s product.

### The Two Brads

Brad operates in two modes. The comedy comes from both existing on the same product with zero self-awareness about the gap.

**Mode 1 — Product Copy Brad (competent)**
Dry, institutional, straight-faced. Takes real formats (nutrition labels, asset tags, QC stickers, care instructions) and fills them with escalating office absurdity. The format never breaks. Brad knows his audience and lands the jokes. The reader laughs WITH the copy.

- "PROPERTY OF: NOBODY ANYMORE"
- "Serving Size: 1 Monday / Servings Per Container: Too Many"
- "DO NOT REMOVE UNDER PENALTY OF MONDAY"
- "Not a significant source of work-life balance, upward mobility, or the feeling that any of this matters."

**Mode 2 — Personal Brand Brad (delusional)**
Aspirational, self-mythologizing, motivational-speaker cadence. Every surface is a billboard. He thinks this is what leadership looks like. Completely sincere. Never winks. The reader laughs AT Brad.

- "He didn't see contraband. He saw potential."
- "Premium workplace tools, premium packaging, and a premium price point."
- "Three-time nominee for the International Workplace Innovation Awards and an occasional keynote speaker at conferences he organizes himself."

### The Gap Is The Joke

The better Brad's product copy is, the funnier his personal branding becomes. Don't sabotage Mode 1 to make Mode 2 land harder. If everything were bad, Brad would just be incompetent. The fact that he's good at the job and blind about himself is what makes him feel real.

### Comedy Rule (LOCKED)

**"Format stays buttoned-up, content gets unleashed."** Official labels, FDA formatting, corporate letterhead stay polished. The words inside go for laughs on every line.

### Visual Identity (LOCKED)

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Shirt | Purple satin, always. Unbuttoned to mid-chest. |
| Jewelry | Oversized gold rope chain (comically large — think 80s rapper). Gold signet pinky ring. |
| Eyewear | Aviator sunglasses — always. |
| Expression | Big cheesy teeth-too-white smile. Always. |
| Color palette | Gold, purple, black — "luxury" as interpreted by someone with no taste budget |
| Photo style | Self-art-directed. He hired the photographer. He thinks they're incredible. NOT stock photos. |

### Comedy Patterns

1. **Format/Content Escalation** — First line plausible, each line escalates, final line fully absurd — format stays intact throughout
2. **The Confessional Drop** — Brad buries damning admissions in corporate language, framing them as achievements
3. **The Credential Stack** — Start almost-real, end obviously self-generated, present all with equal pride
4. **The Trademark Reflex** — Brad trademarks everything. Sentences. Product features. The unboxing experience.
5. **The Premium Stack** — "Premium" used like a comma. Stacked repeatedly and sincerely.

### Recurring Characters

| Character | Role | Target Density |
|-----------|------|---------------|
| **Kevin** | The coworker. Everyone has one. A known hazard. | 4-6 across full kit |
| **Gary** | The quality inspector. Has seen things. | 2-3 across full kit |
| **Diane** | HR. Her presence is the warning. | 1-2 across full kit |
| **Brad Sterling** | Sole voice across all surfaces. | All surfaces |

### Brad's Relationship to the World

Brad has never done time. He's an entrepreneur — always on the outside. But he speaks the office-as-prison vocabulary fluently because it's the language of his market. He's studied it. He deploys it with precision in product copy. He just hasn't lived it. He's the guy who opens a military surplus store without having served. He knows the language like a marketer, not a veteran.

### The Reader / Recipient

The reader is currently doing time. They're in it right now. The kit is a gift from someone who sees them, or a purchase by someone who sees themselves. The vocabulary isn't something the reader needs explained — they live it every day.

Brad's rebranded names appear on the outer Amazon box. The item names (Cubicle Shank, Lifer Ink, etc.) are the primary names used on item boxes and throughout the kit interior.

### Physical Structure

- **Briefcase:** Matte black clamshell, duct tape handle, 14" x 11.5" x 5.0"
- **Interior:** 2-zone side-by-side layout. Front zone: 2 stacking trays. Back zone: hidden mug compartment.
- **Trays:** 2 identical (single mold), landscape die-cut recesses (Pelican-case style), sized for item BOXES
- **Compartment Cover:** J-shaped, lift-off. Top: teaser copy. Underside: narrative beat. Vertical surface serves as divider wall.
- **Interior color scheme:** Dark charcoal, prison orange accents, white copy
- **Closure:** 4-magnet + belly band (TBD) + optional snap-fit lip

### Packaging Layers (reveal sequence, top to bottom)

1. Outer Amazon mailer box (100% Brad's marketing — first thing the recipient sees)
2. Briefcase exterior (Brad's design — matte black, labels, "premium")
3. Open briefcase → Doc Set 1 on top
4. Lift Doc Set 1 → Tray 1 (Cubicle Shank, Lifer Ink, Turf Tags)
5. Lift Tray 1 → Tray 2 (Cell Talk, Rap Sheets, Sweet Nothings, Confiscated Stapler recess)
6. Lift Tray 2 → Doc Set 2 on compartment cover
7. Lift compartment cover → hidden mug compartment (Mug Shot in shredded CLASSIFIED paper)

### Doc Sets

- **Doc Set 1:** On top layer (content direction WORKING)
- **Doc Set 2:** 16-page saddle-stitched "Office Survival Manual Volume II" on compartment cover (LOCKED)
