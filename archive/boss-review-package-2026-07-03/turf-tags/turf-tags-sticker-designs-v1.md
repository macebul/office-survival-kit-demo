# Turf Tags — Sticker Sheet Designs v1

**Last Updated:** July 1, 2026
**Status:** Locked — Ready for visual design development
**Purpose:** Final sticker concepts for the Turf Tags vinyl sticker sheet. Each sticker includes image description, copy, format/size, comedy layers, and deployment notes.
**Sticker Count:** 8 (box copy to update from 12 to 8: "CONTAINS: 8 ADHESIVE PROPERTY MARKERS")
**Sheet Size:** ~6.5" x 4.5" (die-cut vinyl)

---

## Design Principles (from session)

1. **The image must be funny before you read a word.** If the image needs the text to work, the image is wrong.
2. **The text must add a layer, not label the image.** Text should create contrast, understatement, or a second joke — not describe what the viewer already sees.
3. **Every sticker must function as an actual territory marker when deployed.** It marks ownership, warns intruders, or documents consequences. If it doesn't do one of these when stuck on a real object, it doesn't belong on the sheet.
4. **Layers over punchlines.** The best stickers reward a second look — animal behavior + office behavior + prison behavior stacked on top of each other.
5. **Format is the straight man, content is the comedian.** Consistent with every other surface in the kit.

---

## Comedy Rules (from .claude/rules/comedy-writing.md)

- No puns. Brad is dry.
- No exclamation points in product copy.
- No winking at the reader.
- At least 2 of 6 humor elements per piece: Clever, Bizarre, Cruel, Naughty, Recognizable, Cute.
- Specificity over generality.
- Sardonic and warm, not mean or cynical.

### STICKER 1 — DOG

**Image:** Black silhouette of a dog lifting its leg, peeing posture. Clean, bold, reads instantly at any distance. Same visual language as the dog on the Turf Tags box front (TT-3).

**Text:** MARKED.

**Format:** Die-cut to dog silhouette shape. ~1.5" at widest dimension. Black on yellow (matching box palette).

**Comedy Layers:**
- **Animal behavior:** Dogs mark territory by urinating on things. The most primal, universally understood territory-claiming behavior.
- **Office behavior:** Everyone wants to mark their desk/chair/stuff as theirs. The dog does what the office worker can't.
- **The period:** "MARKED." is past tense, declarative, final. It's not a request. It's done.
- **Box cross-reference:** The dog silhouette appears on the box front. The sticker is a deployable version of the box's hero illustration.

**Humor Elements:** Recognizable, Bizarre, Naughty (3/6)

**Deployment:** Desk chair, monitor, keyboard — anywhere you want to declare primal ownership.

**Status:** Locked.

### STICKER 2 — BEAR CLAWS

**Image:** Three diagonal slash/claw marks. Bold, black, looks like something large and dangerous scratched through the sticker surface. Optionally: a tiny tuft of fur caught on one claw edge (forensic detail — something big was here recently).

**Text:** PET FRIENDLY.

**Format:** Die-cut to claw-mark shape or square with claw marks breaking the border. ~1.25". Black on yellow or black on white.

**Comedy Layers:**
- **Animal behavior:** Bears claw trees to mark territory and warn other bears. The scratch marks are the universal "a predator was here" signal.
- **Office behavior:** Apply to a filing cabinet or desk drawer — three claw marks say "something lives in here and it will hurt you."
- **Text/image contrast:** "PET FRIENDLY." is cheerful office-policy language. The claw marks are evidence of a large predator. The mismatch is the joke.
- **Layered ambiguity in "PET FRIENDLY":**
  1. "This workplace is pet friendly" — standard policy sign, completely contradicted by predator evidence
  2. "The pet is friendly" — reassurance that whatever left these marks is approachable. It is not.
  3. "I'm friendly... if you pet me" — the occupant is the animal. Treat them nicely and there won't be problems.

**Humor Elements:** Recognizable, Clever, Bizarre (3/6)

**Deployment:** Filing cabinets, desk drawers, the side of a monitor. Anywhere a physical territorial claim makes sense.

**Status:** Locked.

### STICKER 3 — SQUIRREL

**Image:** Silhouette of a squirrel sitting on top of a pile of office supplies (stapler, pens, tape dispenser, scissors). Dragon-on-gold-hoard pose — guarding from above, hunched, possessive, suspicious eyes. The pile should contain enough recognizable office supply shapes that a close look rewards the viewer.

**Text:** WINTER'S COMING.

**Format:** ~1.5" square. Black silhouette on yellow or white.

**Comedy Layers:**
- **Animal behavior:** Squirrels hoard food for winter. Panic-driven, instinct-based, compulsive accumulation.
- **Office behavior:** The coworker who takes 47 pens from the supply closet and keeps a personal stash. Everyone knows this person. Some people ARE this person.
- **"WINTER'S COMING.":** Game of Thrones reference (pop culture layer the 25-45 target audience recognizes) + literal survival instinct (why squirrels hoard) + the implication that office supply access is about to get scarce.
- **Dragon-on-hoard pose:** The squirrel sitting on top of supplies like Smaug on treasure adds a fantasy/absurdity layer.
- **Visual density:** The pile of office supplies rewards close inspection — "is that a tape dispenser? Are those scissors?" The discovery of specific items is a secondary joke.

**Humor Elements:** Recognizable, Cute, Clever, Bizarre (4/6)

**Deployment:** Supply closet, desk drawer, anywhere you store your stash. The sticker declares: this hoard is strategic, not compulsive.

**Status:** Locked.

### STICKER 4 — SKUNK

**Image:** Silhouette of a skunk with tail raised in the "about to spray" position. The raised tail is essential — it's the universally understood "I'm about to deploy" signal. Without it, it's just a skunk. With it, it's a threat in progress.

**Text:** FINAL WARNING

**Format:** Die-cut to skunk silhouette shape. ~1.5" at widest dimension. Black and white (skunk's natural coloring) — breaks the yellow color scheme, which makes it stand out on the sheet.

**Comedy Layers:**
- **Animal behavior:** The skunk's spray is the nuclear option of territory defense. The raised tail is the last diplomatic signal before chemical warfare.
- **Office behavior:** "Final warning" is HR language for the last step before termination. The corporate disciplinary process and the skunk's defense mechanism use the same escalation structure.
- **Prison behavior:** "Final warning" is also what a guard says before things get serious. The institutional authority voice applied to a small mammal.
- **Text/image synergy:** Both the image (tail raised) and the text (final warning) communicate the same thing — you are at the last stage before consequences. But the image is animal and the text is institutional. The gap between the two registers is the joke.

**Humor Elements:** Recognizable, Clever, Cruel (3/6)

**Deployment:** Fridge shelf, shared equipment, any contested territory where diplomacy has already failed.

**Status:** Locked.

### STICKER 5 — CAT

**Image:** Silhouette of a cat in the act of swiping/pushing an object off the edge of a desk or shelf. The object is mid-fall. A snapshot of a crime in progress. The cat's posture is casual, deliberate — this isn't an accident.

**Text:** RESTRUCTURING.

**Format:** ~1.5" square. Black silhouette on yellow or white.

**Comedy Layers:**
- **Animal behavior:** Cats knock things off surfaces. It's deliberate. It's dominance. It's their most recognizable behavior after face-rubbing.
- **Office behavior:** "Restructuring" is the corporate euphemism for eliminating things (positions, departments, people). The cat is restructuring your desk by eliminating your things.
- **The corporate layer:** In the real world, "restructuring" means layoffs and reorganization. The cat is management. Your stuff is being downsized. The period makes it final — this isn't a proposal, it's been decided.
- **Deliberateness:** Cats don't knock things off by accident. It's intentional. So is your coworker who "reorganizes" shared spaces by displacing everyone else's things.

**Humor Elements:** Recognizable, Clever, Cute (3/6)

**Deployment:** Shared desks, communal workspaces, anywhere someone else's stuff keeps appearing in your space. The sticker announces that a reorganization is underway and it wasn't put to a vote.

**Status:** Locked.

### STICKER 6 — SKULL + PEN SHANK

**Image:** A skull (front-facing or three-quarter view) with a pen sticking out of it — the pen is the Cubicle Shank from the kit. Not a generic pen — it should be recognizable as the same pen silhouette from the Cubicle Shank item box if possible. The overall aesthetic should echo prison yard-wall graffiti or gang territory markings while remaining clean enough to read as a sticker.

**Text:** PREVIOUS OWNER.

**Format:** ~1.25" square or circle. Black on white or black on yellow.

**Comedy Layers:**
- **Prison behavior:** A shank in a skull is yard-wall graffiti. It's the kind of image scratched into concrete to mark gang territory. This is the most direct prison/office bridge on the sticker sheet.
- **Office behavior:** "Previous owner" is real estate and property transfer language. Applied to a skull with a pen in it, it documents a change in ownership that was not voluntary.
- **Cross-product reference:** The pen IS the Cubicle Shank — another product from the same kit. Someone who has opened other items recognizes the pen silhouette. An Easter egg that rewards kit exploration without requiring it.
- **Understatement:** The text is deadpan institutional language. The image is a skull with a weapon in it. The gap between the bureaucratic tone and the violent visual is where the laugh lives.
- **Territorial function:** Stick this on something and it says: this item has a history. The last person who had it is represented by this skull. You are being warned not to become the next previous owner.
- **Range:** This is the darkest sticker on the sheet, but the kit already lives in dark territory (Cubicle Shank copy: "Through a light jacket..." / "Does it come in red?" "Eventually."). "PREVIOUS OWNER." gives it the deadpan release valve.

**Humor Elements:** Cruel, Clever, Bizarre, Recognizable (4/6)

**Deployment:** Any personal item — the sticker implies a lineage of ownership, with the previous holder meeting an unfortunate end. Best on items people frequently try to "borrow."

**Status:** Locked.

### STICKER 7 — COFFIN LINEUP

**Image:** Three coffin/burial silhouettes in a row, left to right, escalating in absurdity:
1. **Standard casket** — simple, recognizable funeral silhouette. The "normal" option.
2. **Filing cabinet on its side** — an office filing cabinet turned horizontal. The drawers look like compartments. The office-specific coffin. You lived at this desk, you'll be buried in its furniture.
3. **Viking funeral** — a small boat/raft silhouette with flames. Floating out on water, lit on fire. The dramatic exit. The kind of send-off Brad would approve of.

**Text:** HELP YOURSELF. OPTIONS AVAILABLE.

**Format:** Horizontal strip, ~2.5" x 0.75". Black silhouettes on yellow or white. The three coffins evenly spaced, text below.

**Comedy Layers:**
- **"Help yourself":** A phrase that already lives in offices ("help yourself to the donuts"). Here it's sarcastic — help yourself to my stuff, and then help yourself to one of these options. The fake generosity is the menace.
- **"Options available":** Brad's product-catalog voice. The coffins are presented as a product selection, not a threat. There's a catalog. There are options. Customer service is available.
- **Escalation:** Standard casket (normal) → filing cabinet (office-specific, absurd) → Viking funeral (glorious, theatrical). The range of options implies a tiered service offering.
- **The filing cabinet:** The middle option is the funniest single image — a filing cabinet IS a coffin if you turn it sideways. The drawers even look like compartments. It's office furniture repurposed for its ultimate function.
- **Consequence-based territory:** Unlike the animal stickers that mark ownership, this one shows what happens if you violate someone else's claim. It's defensive through threat.

**Humor Elements:** Cruel, Clever, Bizarre, Recognizable (4/6)

**Deployment:** Shared items, contested territory, the fridge. Anywhere you want to communicate consequences with a menu of options.

**Status:** Locked.

### STICKER 8 — SANDWICH THEFT PICTOGRAM

**Image:** Four sequential panels in IKEA-instruction-style line art, arranged left to right:

1. **Panel 1:** A hand reaching toward a lunch container (the crime)
2. **Panel 2:** A stick figure being grabbed by the collar by a larger stick figure (the response — immediate)
3. **Panel 3:** The stick figure launched through the air via trajectory arc, with angle notation ("37°" written in small text, like a physics diagram — someone calculated the optimal launch angle for a food thief)
4. **Panel 4:** An empty desk with a small memorial photo frame on it (the aftermath — they're gone, we remember them fondly)

**Text (title above panels):** FYI. FOR REFERENCE.

**Format:** Horizontal strip, ~2" x 1". Black line art on white or yellow. Thin vertical dividers between panels, matching the IKEA deployment strip on the box's top long edge (TT-19).

**Comedy Layers:**
- **Format parody:** The IKEA instruction format is one of the most universally recognized visual languages. Using it for consequences of food theft is the format/content gap working at full power.
- **Box cross-reference:** The IKEA deployment strip on the box top edge (PEEL → STICK → HOLD COURT → ACCEPT PARADE → EXIT) is the kit's standout surface. This sticker is its evil twin — same format, but instead of "what happens when YOU deploy stickers," it's "what happens to the PERSON who touches your stuff."
- **"FYI. FOR REFERENCE.":** Both phrases mean the same thing. Using both is the email equivalent of someone who is furious but being professional about it. The bureaucratic doubling makes the contrast with the pictogram violence more absurd.
- **The angle notation in Panel 3:** The "37°" turns impulsive violence into something that was PLANNED. Someone did the math on the optimal launch trajectory for a food thief. The precision is the joke.
- **The memorial frame in Panel 4:** They didn't just get fired. They're being REMEMBERED. Past tense. The desk is empty. A photo remains. The office moved on. It's the most casually devastating panel.
- **Passive-aggressive escalation:** Panels 1-2 are plausible (someone gets caught). Panels 3-4 escalate into absurdity (physics-grade ejection, memorial service). The escalation follows the same pattern as the IKEA strip on the box.

**Humor Elements:** Recognizable, Clever, Cruel, Bizarre (4/6)

**Deployment:** The fridge (primary target — this is where food theft happens), lunch containers, shared kitchen areas. This sticker is a policy document in pictogram form.

**Status:** Locked.

## Sheet Layout Notes

- **5 animal territory behaviors** (dog, bear, squirrel, skunk, cat) — each represents a different territorial strategy: marking, warning, hoarding, deterrence, displacement
- **1 prison/office crossover** (skull + Cubicle Shank pen) — darkest sticker, Easter egg cross-reference
- **2 consequence-based** (coffin lineup + sandwich pictogram) — horizontal strips, larger format
- **Mix of die-cut shapes and standard formats** for visual variety on the sheet
- **Range from cute (squirrel) to dark (skull) to absurd (Viking funeral coffin)**
- **The two horizontal strips** (coffins + pictogram) anchor the sheet and provide visual weight. The die-cut animals fill around them.

## Decisions Log

| Decision | Resolution | Rationale |
|----------|-----------|-----------|
| Sticker count | 8 (not 12) | 8 strong stickers > 12 with filler. Box copy updates to match. |
| Design approach | Image-forward with short text | Space limitations on stickers eliminate copy-heavy formats. Every sticker needs a strong visual. |
| Animal theme | 5 of 8 stickers are animals | Each animal represents a different territorial strategy. Creates a visual theme (nature guide to office survival) without forcing it. |
| Text role | Contrast/understatement, not labels | Text adds a layer the image doesn't provide. "PET FRIENDLY." contrasts claw marks. "RESTRUCTURING." reframes destruction. "PREVIOUS OWNER." understates a skull. |
| Prison/office blend | Skull + pen shank sticker | Direct prison graffiti aesthetic with office-supply weapon. Cross-references Cubicle Shank. Darkest sticker on sheet, balanced by deadpan "PREVIOUS OWNER." text. |
| Consequence stickers | Coffins + pictogram | Two formats showing what happens to territory violators. Coffins offer options. Pictogram shows the process. |
| Copy-only stickers | Eliminated | Space constraints at sticker scale make text-heavy stickers unreadable and invisible. All 8 stickers are image-forward or combination. |
| Flowchart/threat meter | Cut | Strong concepts but require too much text/detail at sticker scale. Would work as bumper stickers but not at the sizes available on this sheet. |
| "NO." red circle | Cut | Functional but not funny enough. Didn't earn its slot against the other concepts. |
