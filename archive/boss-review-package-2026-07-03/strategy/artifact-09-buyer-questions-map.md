# 9. Office Survival Kit - Buyer Questions Map

**Product:** A novelty gag gift kit containing 8 individually packaged items in a mailer box, using sardonic office-is-prison humor, sold on Amazon under the Witty Yeti brand for the coworker/friend gift market.

**This Artifact:** Every question - spoken and unspoken - that the buyer needs answered before they'll purchase, prioritized so marketers know exactly which information gaps to fill first. These are information needs, not resistance (see Artifact 8 for objections).

## Must-Answer Questions

### "What's actually in this kit?"
* **Priority**
   * Must-Answer
* **Type**
   * Conscious
* **Why this question exists**
   * Gift kits on Amazon are notorious for vague contents - buyers have learned to demand specifics
   * "8 items" is a claim that needs visual proof
   * The buyer is mentally calculating value: do these 8 items justify $25-30?
* **What happens if it goes unanswered**
   * The buyer assumes the worst - that the kit is padded with filler or the items are tiny/cheap
   * They leave the listing without purchasing

### "Is the humor actually good, or is this 'novelty product' funny?"
* **Priority**
   * Must-Answer
* **Type**
   * Conscious
* **Why this question exists**
   * The buyer has been disappointed by products that promise humor and deliver mediocrity
   * "Funny" in a listing description has been devalued by overuse
   * The buyer needs to judge the comedy quality for themselves, not be told about it
* **What happens if it goes unanswered**
   * The buyer defaults to category skepticism - "probably not that funny"
   * They move on to a safer choice (gift card, single known-funny mug)

### "What kind of humor is it? Dark? Clean? Slapstick? Puns?"
* **Priority**
   * Must-Answer
* **Type**
   * Conscious
* **Why this question exists**
   * Humor is deeply personal - the buyer needs to know if this matches their (and the recipient's) sensibility
   * "Office humor" could mean dad jokes, crude humor, sarcasm, or gentle observations - these are very different audiences
   * The buyer is screening for compatibility, not just quality
* **What happens if it goes unanswered**
   * The buyer can't assess fit for the recipient
   * They may assume it's the generic "funny office mug" style of humor and pass

### "Is this appropriate for a work gift exchange?"
* **Priority**
   * Must-Answer
* **Type**
   * Conscious
* **Why this question exists**
   * Many purchases will be for workplace events where HR norms apply
   * "Office survival kit" with prison vocabulary could raise concern without context
   * The buyer needs assurance that this won't get them in trouble
* **What happens if it goes unanswered**
   * The buyer defaults to caution and chooses something safer
   * This is especially true for Scrambler-type buyers who evaluate quickly

### "How much does it cost relative to what I'm getting?"
* **Priority**
   * Must-Answer
* **Type**
   * Conscious
* **Why this question exists**
   * $25-30 is above the typical gag gift price range
   * The buyer needs to see that the item count and quality justify the premium
   * They're comparing against $12-18 single-item alternatives
* **What happens if it goes unanswered**
   * The buyer perceives it as overpriced and filters it out during comparison shopping

## Should-Answer Questions

### "What do the cards actually say? Can I see examples?"
* **Priority**
   * Should-Answer
* **Type**
   * Conscious
* **Why this question exists**
   * The three card decks are 30-45 cards of comedy content - the buyer wants to sample before committing
   * Reading 2-3 card examples is the fastest way to judge comedy quality
   * This is especially important because the cards are the highest-content items in the kit
* **What happens if it goes unanswered**
   * The buyer can't evaluate the comedy quality of 60% of the kit's content
   * They have to trust the listing claims rather than judge for themselves, which reduces conversion

### "Is this good quality or is it going to feel cheap when they open it?"
* **Priority**
   * Should-Answer
* **Type**
   * Conscious
* **Why this question exists**
   * The buyer will watch the recipient open this in front of other people
   * Cheap-feeling materials create immediate visible disappointment
   * The buyer has experienced the Amazon photo-to-reality gap before
* **What happens if it goes unanswered**
   * The buyer remains uncertain and may add to cart without purchasing, or choose a known-quantity alternative
   * Customer photos in reviews partially answer this, but the listing should address it directly

### "Does it come gift-ready, or do I need to wrap it?"
* **Priority**
   * Should-Answer
* **Type**
   * Conscious
* **Why this question exists**
   * Gift buyers want to know if the packaging IS the presentation or if they need to do additional work
   * A mailer box might look like shipping packaging, not gift packaging
   * This is a convenience question that affects perceived effort
* **What happens if it goes unanswered**
   * Minor friction - the buyer may assume they need a gift bag, which is fine but adds a step
   * If the mailer box IS intended to be the presentation, this needs to be stated

### "Will it arrive in time for my event?"
* **Priority**
   * Should-Answer
* **Type**
   * Conscious
* **Why this question exists**
   * Most purchases are occasion-driven with a hard deadline
   * Prime eligibility answers this for most buyers, but Q4 shipping delays create extra anxiety
* **What happens if it goes unanswered**
   * The buyer checks delivery estimates, which Amazon handles automatically
   * But in peak Q4 season, any doubt about timing kills impulse purchases

### "Is this for men, women, or anyone?"
* **Priority**
   * Should-Answer
* **Type**
   * Unconscious - most buyers won't articulate this but will evaluate it instinctively
* **Why this question exists**
   * Some gag gifts skew obviously male or female in their humor
   * The buyer needs to instinctively feel that this works for their specific recipient regardless of gender
   * Listing imagery and copy subtly signal gendered or gender-neutral appeal
* **What happens if it goes unanswered**
   * If the listing reads as skewing male (tactical pen foregrounded, dark/aggressive imagery), female buyers shopping for female recipients may self-select out
   * The product IS gender-neutral, but the listing needs to demonstrate that visually

### "How big is it? Will it fit in a gift bag?"
* **Priority**
   * Should-Answer
* **Type**
   * Conscious
* **Why this question exists**
   * The buyer needs to plan the physical logistics of gift-giving
   * A mailer box has different dimensions than a briefcase - some buyers may want to know the size for planning
* **What happens if it goes unanswered**
   * Minor friction - the buyer may order and figure it out, or check the product dimensions if listed

## Nice-to-Answer Questions

### "Can I see what other people thought when they received this?"
* **Priority**
   * Nice-to-Answer
* **Type**
   * Conscious
* **Why this question exists**
   * Social proof reduces purchase anxiety
   * The buyer wants to see real reactions, not marketing promises
* **What happens if it goes unanswered**
   * This is primarily answered through reviews - the listing itself has limited ability to provide this
   * Pre-launch, this is a gap that will fill naturally with initial reviews

### "Who makes this? Is this a real company?"
* **Priority**
   * Nice-to-Answer
* **Type**
   * Conscious
* **Why this question exists**
   * Minor credibility check - the buyer wants to know this isn't a fly-by-night Amazon seller
   * Witty Yeti's existing catalog provides some reassurance here
* **What happens if it goes unanswered**
   * Most buyers won't investigate beyond the listing itself
   * A professional-looking listing is its own answer

### "Is the pen actually usable as a daily pen?"
* **Priority**
   * Nice-to-Answer
* **Type**
   * Conscious
* **Why this question exists**
   * Some buyers specifically value the utility angle - "at least the pen works"
   * Knowing the pen is aluminum with a replaceable cartridge adds confidence
* **What happens if it goes unanswered**
   * Doesn't prevent purchase but reduces one selling point

### "Is the mug microwave/dishwasher safe?"
* **Priority**
   * Nice-to-Answer
* **Type**
   * Conscious
* **Why this question exists**
   * Standard mug question - buyers ask it reflexively
   * Enamel mugs are generally not microwave safe, which is worth noting to prevent negative reviews
* **What happens if it goes unanswered**
   * May generate a negative review post-purchase: "Didn't know I couldn't microwave it"
   * Low impact on purchase decision but worth addressing to protect star rating

## Questions by Purchase Stage

### Before they even consider this product
* "What should I get my coworker/friend who works in an office?"
* "Is there a funny gift that isn't just a mug or a candy bag?"
* "What's a good White Elephant gift under $30?"

### While evaluating options
* "What's actually in this kit?"
* "Is this humor my style? Is it their style?"
* "Is $27 reasonable for what I'm getting?"
* "What do the cards actually say?"
* "Is this appropriate for the office?"

### At the moment of purchase decision
* "Will it arrive in time?"
* "Am I confident this will make them laugh?"
* "Is this better than just doing a gift card?"

### Immediately after purchase
* "When will it arrive?"
* "Does it come gift-ready?"
* "Should I read through it myself before giving it?"
   * This last one is interesting - some buyers will want to pre-screen the content, and the answer should be "yes, and you'll enjoy it too"
