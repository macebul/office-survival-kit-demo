Two beats — "on file" = expected (bureau keeps records), "on your face" = the flip (the records are tattoos, on your face).

## SURFACE 2 — BACK FACE (5.0" x 3.5")

*Distinguished Honorees section. Professional headshots of suited people with visible prison-style tattoos, styled like employee-of-the-month. PHOTOS do the comedy.*

---

### Back Face Header `[LOCKED — LOCK-14]`

> **OUR MOST DECORATED**

"Decorated" does triple duty: (1) honored with commendations, (2) covered in tattoos, (3) corporate recognition. All three readings hit simultaneously when the reader sees the photos.

---

### Karen Whitfield `[LOCKED — LOCK-15, LOCK-16]`

**Photo:** Professional headshot — business attire, serious corporate portrait. Teardrop tattoo under eye, starburst tattoo near temple.

> **Karen Whitfield — Associate Account Manager (Formerly Assistant) — 3 Years**
>
> "I earned my first teardrop last week. I'd rather not say how. But I will say my cubicle neighbor's desk is available if anyone needs one. Anyway — hoping to earn a second one soon."

Character voice: Bright, eager, enthusiastic. "(Formerly Assistant)" implies promotion after cubicle neighbor's disappearance.

---

### Dennis Rourke `[LOCKED — LOCK-17, LOCK-18]`

**Photo:** Professional headshot — business attire, thoughtful expression, chin resting on hand showing "LIFER" tattooed across knuckles.

> **Dennis Rourke — Senior Operations Analyst — 14 Years (Consecutive)**
>
> "People think the tattoos are about expression. They're not. They're about efficiency. I tell every new hire the same thing: skip the icebreakers. Get the teardrop. It's an introduction, a boundary, and a conversation starter. Three birds. One tattoo."

Character voice: Strategic, measured, mentoring. "(Consecutive)" implies 14 straight years noted like a prison sentence served without parole.

---

### Brad Quote `[KEEP — BRAD-13]`

> "I was going to get a real tattoo once. Then I thought, what if I could have the experience without the experience? That's when I knew I was an entrepreneur."
>
> — Brad Sterling, Chief of Permanent Records

Avoidance reframed as entrepreneurship. Short, punchy, classic Brad Mode 2.
