**Design note:** "Because it keeps you more alive than O2." is visually subordinate to the division name — smaller type, parenthetical feel. It explains why the division is called O3. ### 3.2b Brand Positioning Line `[LOCKED]`

> **They call it premeditated. We call it prepared.**

**Design note:** Standalone typographic element on card front, near product name or brand block. Not a tagline — a brand thesis statement. Everything else on the card is evidence supporting this position. Typographic treatment: highlight the shared prefix "pre" in both "premeditated" and "prepared" with a color or weight change to visually connect the two words and reward the close reader.

*Comedy mechanics: Two-beat reframe. Same prefix, same act, completely different framing. "Call it" gives it institutional weight — implies someone (law enforcement, HR, a jury) raised the issue and the brand has a position. Legal/crime vocabulary delivered as product positioning. The prefix highlight is a design flourish, not the comedy engine — the engine is the attitude shift: you brought a weapon to work on purpose, and the brand's position is that this is just good planning. ## SECTION 8: DESIGN PRINCIPLES (established across sessions)

1. **Content-first, dimensions later.** Write the best material for each surface's intent. The box adapts to the content, not the other way around.
2. **Every piece of text earns a laugh.** If a word isn't funny or setting up something funny, it's wasted real estate.
3. **Reversals.** Lines should set up one expectation and deliver something else.
4. **Short beats long.** Especially in chart cells and small copy blocks. 5 words max per chart cell, ideally 3 or fewer.
5. **Specificity over concepts.** Concrete nouns, specific details, visual images.
6. **The professional packaging design IS the comedy technique.** The box looks real. The copy is where the humor lives.
7. **Stop at the punchline.** If the line was funnier three words earlier, cut those three words.
8. **Physical product facts are non-negotiable.** No cap = no cap jokes. Blister card = no side panels.
9. **Badges need layered comedy.** Single-mechanic labels don't clear the bar. The badge, its format, and its sub-text should create multiple simultaneous comedy layers.
10. **Second lines can diminish the first.** Sometimes the best revision is deleting the follow-up.

## SECTION 9: KEY LEARNINGS FROM ALL SESSIONS

### From boss feedback
- v01 of all items was universally rejected: "not funny enough, doesn't meet the standards set by existing Witty Yeti products."
- Rule of three: "Normal, normal, crazy. Three of the same category isn't funny."
- Verified purchaser reviews: "A user who is using the product normally, and then it turns [dark] is the right setup/structure."
- Boss wants it DARK, delivered with warmth. Not tame.

### From iterative development
- **Quantity lines need subversion.** Cross-category comparison works. Just listing a number with a dark implication isn't enough.
- **Feature bullets need playful energy.** Dark and ominous without warmth fails. Absurd material escalation works.
- **The voice is a warm, cheerful, conspiratorial friend** — not a dry military briefing, not a neutral corporate entity.
- **Weapon-spec double-meaning lines are a narrow comedy lane.** They represent one approach. The newer WY-voice lines are stronger.
- **Warmth comes from word choice, not word count.** Short AND warm beats long AND warm.

### From June 17 session (taglines, subtitles, badges refinement)
- **Label every option.** When presenting multiple candidates, label them (TAG-3A, SUB-2B, etc.) so the user can reference specific lines without quoting entire sentences.
- **"Clever" is not "funny."** Descriptive statements and observations without setup/reversal/subversion are clever but don't land. Every line needs a comedy mechanic — reversal, escalation, misdirect, or pattern-break.
- **Don't push rejected directions.** When a line or direction is explicitly rejected, stop generating variants of it. Move to a different angle entirely.
- **Floating lines are valid.** Not every good line needs a surface assignment right now. Some lines work best saved for placement once the full card layout is finalized.
- **Chain puns benefit from story order.** TAG-2's chain (pen → con → shank → stab) works because it follows narrative causality — you get the pen, become a con, do the shanking, deliver the stab. Random order loses the momentum.
- **Badges need visual specificity.** "Janitor" isn't just a word — it's a retro-illustrated character with a mop and a thumbs up. The visual details ARE part of the comedy design.
- **Fine print can carry its own joke.** "Guarantee void if used as directed" is a standalone inversion that adds a fourth comedy layer to BADGE-1 without cluttering the main seal.

### From June 18 session (chart cells, warnings, fine print, Assisted Shiving)
- **Observations are not jokes.** "Kids are more competent than adults" is an observation everyone's already had. It's not a reversal, not a surprise, not funny. A reversal would be: the age restriction exists because of child labor laws, not safety.
- **Don't repeat the same joke structure across iterations.** When every variant is "kids do better at X than adults," that's one idea in 10 costumes. Move to a different angle entirely.
- **Extensions ≠ escalation.** Adding more people who saw the video (HR, Legal, corporate) is extension. Adding what happened BECAUSE of the video ("So did the jury") is escalation. One makes it longer, the other makes it worse.
- **Avoid "actually" as a dry qualifier pattern.** "Aerodynamic, actually" works once. "Writes beautifully, actually" / "They'll be carried, actually" — at three uses across the chart, it's becoming a crutch. Watch for repeated qualifiers.
- **Short characters say short things.** Carl's entire personality lands in three words: "That'll buff out." When a character doesn't say much, their best line should be their shortest.
- **Combining saved concepts creates density.** The Assisted Shiving element works because it gives Shiv-ision, Evidence Lockers, and Edith a shared home instead of competing for separate real estate on the card.
- **Pharma ad language is universally recognizable.** "Talk to your doctor" beats "Ask your surgeon" because everyone recognizes the pharma ad format instantly. Recognizability > precision.
- **Ellipsis can replace a punchline.** FB-1 ending at "Through a light jacket..." is funnier than spelling out "We stopped testing after the jacket." The reader's imagination fills in something worse than any written ending.
- **QTY lines should look like QTY lines.** The old cross-category weapon lists were funny concepts but didn't read like real packaging quantity lines. The Doug footnote version starts from authentic packaging language and subverts it.
