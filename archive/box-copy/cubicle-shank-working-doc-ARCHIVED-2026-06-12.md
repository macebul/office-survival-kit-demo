### The 7 mechanical rules (derived from approved product copy)

**Rule 1: Joke lands on the LAST word or LAST phrase.**
Nothing after the punchline. No button. No reaction. No "if you know what we mean."
- "Includes everything but your pride." — "pride" is last
- "Small enough to panic quietly." — "quietly" is last
- "Perfect for baby showers, birthdays, or revenge." — "revenge" is last

**Rule 2: Setup is a REAL product copy format.**
Feature bullet, spec, disclaimer, care instruction, FAQ, contents list, shipping policy. The reader's brain recognizes the format BEFORE the joke hits.

**Rule 3: Punchline word must be CONCRETE and UNCOMFORTABLE.**
Not "concerns," "intimidation," "responsibility." Instead: specific nouns (your pride, your lawyer, Kevin, witnesses, blood), specific actions (panic, pretend, leave), specific situations (cult rituals, revenge, crime scenes).

**Rule 4: Talk TO the buyer.**
"Your ___." "You'll ___." Make the reader complicit. They're not observing this product — they're involved in what it implies. ("Your moral compass probably isn't either." "60 days to pretend none of this happened.")

**Rule 5: Be CHEERFUL about something you shouldn't be cheerful about.**
The copy is HAPPY, WARM, ENTHUSIASTIC about something alarming. Not dry. Not clinical. Not neutral. Cheerful. The brand is your fun, slightly irresponsible friend.
- "Full commitment." — proud
- "We're not here to judge." — warm, permissive
- "We believe in you." — encouraging
- "Enjoy a front-row seat." — excited on your behalf

**Rule 6: Never instruct the reader to be threatening.**
Don't say "maintain eye contact" or "aim at coworker." Describe the product in a way that makes the threat OBVIOUS without directing it. "Small enough to panic quietly" doesn't tell you to panic — it just tells you the product is sized for it.

**Rule 7: Rule of three when using lists.**
Two normal items, third item breaks the pattern. The third item is a REAL THING, not a concept.
- "baby showers, birthdays, or revenge"
- "desks, stocking stuffers, or cult rituals"
- "Gluten-free, cruelty-free, hydration-free"

### Additional voice characteristics (identified June 10 session)

**Word count:** Strongest lines are 5-13 words.

**Ratio:** ~60-70% normal product copy, 30-40% absurd. The joke is concentrated in a small number of words.

**The second sentence makes the first sentence worse.** "Scientifically dehydrated. Which is to say: we didn't put any in." The follow-up darkens or deflates, never just comments.

**The brand has a PERSONALITY.** It's not a neutral corporate entity. It's a warm, casual, slightly conspiratorial friend who is genuinely excited to sell you something ridiculous. Key tone markers: "Look," "Seriously," "Trust us," "We're not going to ask," "We believe in you," "Which is to say."

## SECTION 5: WHY THE REJECTED SHANK COPY FAILED (specific diagnosis)

**Failure 1: Punchline words too tame.** "Concerns," "responsibility," "intimidation" — abstract corporate words vs. the approved products' concrete gut-punch words.

**Failure 2: Copy instructs the reader to be threatening.** "MAINTAIN EYE CONTACT," "REMOVE WHILE MAINTAINING EYE CONTACT WITH THE NEAREST COWORKER" — the WY voice describes, never directs.

**Failure 3: Format-joke without funny content.** The tactical/military packaging FORMAT was the joke, but the words inside it weren't individually funny. Approved WY products are funny at the sentence level even stripped of formatting.

**Failure 4: No direct address to buyer.** Approved lines say "YOUR pride," "YOUR lawyer." Rejected Shank copy talks about the product in third person.

**Failure 5: Not cheerful enough.** Rejected copy is serious, dry, clinical, trying to be cool. Approved WY copy is enthusiastic, warm, casual — genuinely excited about something it shouldn't be excited about.

**Failure 6: One-joke repetition.** Hundreds of lines all executing the same joke (pen = weapon) with the same mechanism (double-meaning spec language). Different copy types (warranty, FAQ, care, contents) give you different angles on the same premise, preventing staleness.

## SECTION 8: KEY LEARNINGS & NOTES

1. **The boss wants volume to lock the voice.** He said to just write content, not worry about placement. But writing for specific copy types gives natural variety and prevents the one-joke-repetition problem.

2. **The existing WY products are the standard.** Everything must be measured against the Shart Kit, Dehydrated Water, Child Chucker, and original Shart Kit.

3. **The WY voice is a cheerful, warm, slightly conspiratorial friend** — not a dry military briefing, not a neutral corporate entity, not a one-liner comedian. The brand is genuinely excited to sell you something ridiculous.

4. **Copy must serve a real product purpose.** Lines should feel like they belong on actual packaging, not like disconnected jokes. Each line should have a job (telling you what's inside, warning you, guaranteeing quality, answering a question).

5. **Kevin should be used sparingly on this item** — the leak-proof line is likely the best Kevin usage for the Shank. Other Kevin refs should be distributed across other kit items.

6. **The maybes from earlier sessions (6-30) are almost all weapon-spec double-meaning lines.** These may or may not survive boss feedback. They represent one narrow comedy lane. The newer lines (31-37) attempt the warmer WY voice but are still being calibrated.

7. **The session prompt file and email version file are OUT OF DATE.** They contain lines that were subsequently rejected (duct tape, grip spec, effective range, comparison table, etc.) listed as "locked." This working document supersedes both.

8. **Warmth comes from word choice, not word count.** The boss's feedback about needing more personality/levity/peppiness does NOT mean longer copy. It means warmer words in the same short format. The tight v2 bullets ("Guaranteed for life. Ideally a long one.") are already warm AND short. The chatty variants (lines 51-54) were useful for finding the energy but are too long for actual box surfaces. These boxes are small — warmth must fit in 6-15 words, not paragraphs. When the boss gave paragraph-length examples, he was showing the ENERGY he wanted, not the word count.

9. **The professional packaging design IS the comedy technique.** The box looking like a real corporate product is intentional and correct. The other WY products (Shart Kit, Child Chucker) look like gag gifts immediately. This kit delays the reveal — it looks real, then you read it and it's dark. That gap between format and content is the entire joke. Do NOT add lifestyle photos, funny illustrations, or gag-gift visual cues to make it "look more like WY." The straight-faced packaging is the format. The copy is the content. "Format stays buttoned-up. Content gets unleashed."

## SECTION 9: WHAT NOT TO DO (comprehensive)

- Do NOT generate lines where the copy is aware of its own joke
- Do NOT add buttons, tags, or reactions after the punchline
- Do NOT use Kevin as a punchline — Kevin appears unremarked in lists (and sparingly on this item)
- Do NOT write office observational humor ("meetings suck," "survived another Monday")
- Do NOT leave the office/prison world (no military, government, international law)
- Do NOT repeat structural tricks endlessly
- Do NOT explain the joke in any way
- Do NOT lead with violence and pivot to product — lead with product
- Do NOT instruct the reader to be threatening ("maintain eye contact," "aim at coworker")
- Do NOT use abstract/corporate punchline words ("concerns," "responsibility," "intimidation")
- Do NOT write dry, clinical, neutral copy — the voice is WARM and CHEERFUL
- Do NOT write disconnected one-liners — every line should serve a product copy purpose
- Do NOT reuse rejected v01 mockup copy or concepts

## SECTION 12: KEY VOICE DISCOVERY (June 10, late session)

**The brand KNOWS it's selling a shank.** It's not confused, coy, or playing dumb. Just like:
- Shart Kit KNOWS it's about pooping — "Your colon has committed mutiny"
- Dehydrated Water KNOWS it's empty — "We didn't put any in"
- Child Chucker KNOWS it's about launching children — "Chuck Your Child From Anywhere"

The Cubicle Shank brand is DELIGHTED to be selling you a shank disguised as a pen. It found a loophole (HR said no to weapons, so they made it a pen) and is cheerfully proud of it. This is the core voice insight that cracked the description line.

**Do NOT write the brand as confused about its own product.** No "we don't know why your coworkers are afraid of it." The brand knows exactly why. It just doesn't think that's a problem.
