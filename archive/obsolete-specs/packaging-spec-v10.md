## 1. Concept: Brad Sterling's Office Survival Kit

**Brad Sterling** (B.S., Chief Vision Officer & Founder of **Elevate Co.**) is the sole voice of the entire product. He discovered the concept of office survival, mass-produced it, and now sells it on Amazon as a retail product. He has repackaged every item into individual retail-style boxes, slapped corporate branding on everything, and wrapped it all in a polished Amazon box. Brad is the voice of every soulless product launch, every "synergy" meeting, every forced fun initiative. His aesthetic is clean, corporate, and deeply punchable.

The humor comes from Brad's complete lack of self-awareness — he genuinely believes he is changing lives. The packaging is played straight as his corporate product. The comedy is embedded in the content itself, not in a second voice commenting on it.

### Chalk Outline & Die-Cut Recess Style

Every item box in every tray sits within a die-cut recess (Pelican case style) — a molded cutout shaped precisely to each item's RETAIL BOX (not the raw item). This is not an open tray with loose boxes; each box has a fitted pocket that prevents shifting and provides a premium unboxing feel.

Each die-cut recess has a chalk-outline silhouette printed on the tray surface surrounding the cutout, plus the item name and a short tagline printed adjacent to the recess. The chalk outlines serve three purposes: they tell the recipient where each item goes (and that one is "missing" if removed), they reinforce the crime-scene / institutional world, and they provide a clean, premium presentation that reads as intentional rather than cluttered.

**Exception: Confiscated Stapler Recess.** One recess in Tray 2 is intentionally EMPTY — a small stapler silhouette with a **prison orange chalk outline** (not white/cream like the other chalk outlines — it stands out). The label reads: "CONFISCATED — WEAPONIZED OFFICE EQUIPMENT. INCIDENT #147. DO NOT REISSUE." The absence of the item is the punchline. See Section 7 for details.

Outlines are printed in white or cream on the darker tray floor. Line weight should feel hand-drawn — not perfectly uniform, not digitally precise. Slightly rough edges. Think actual chalk on a dark surface, not vector art. Recess shapes match the ITEM BOXES, not the raw items inside.

## 5. Interior Structure: Two-Zone Layout

### Zone Overview

The interior is divided into two zones by the compartment cover's vertical surface (integral to the J-shaped compartment cover — not a separate divider wall):

**Front Zone (facing the person):** Trays 1 and 2 stacked vertically with Document Set 1 on top. Trays are full interior width (no ring clearance needed) and centered in the front zone. Each tray contains individually boxed items in Pelican-style die-cut recesses sized for the boxes. Both trays use a single identical mold with landscape-oriented recesses (wide at back ~320mm, pen-sized at front ~168mm).

**Back Zone (along back wall):** A hidden padded compartment containing the Mug Shot mug (on its side, handle to the side) nestled in shredded "CLASSIFIED" paper padding. Completely hidden while trays are in place. Revealed when Tray 2 is lifted and the J-shaped compartment cover is removed. Doc Set 2 rests on top of the compartment cover — visible when Tray 2 is lifted, before the cover itself is opened.

*For all zone dimensions and clearance values, see Physical Dimensions & Sourcing Spec. Key constraint: back zone is the tightest fit (~0.44" clearance).*

## 7. Tray Specifications

### Tray Design Principles (Both Trays)

- **Single identical tray mold:** Both trays use the same mold. Landscape-oriented recesses — wide at back (~320mm), pen-sized at front (~168mm). This simplifies tooling and reduces cost.
- **Die-cut recesses (Pelican case style) SIZED FOR BOXES:** Each item's retail box sits in a precisely shaped molded cutout — not an open tray with loose items. Recesses are shaped to fit the PRODUCT BOXES, not the raw items inside. Each recess has a chalk-outline silhouette printed on the tray surface surrounding the cutout, plus the item name and a short tagline printed adjacent to the recess. This provides a premium unboxing feel and prevents all shifting during shipping.
- Copy on tray surfaces includes item names and short taglines next to each die-cut recess — the extra inch of case length (14" vs. 13") provides space for this copy alongside the cutouts.
- Story lives in the document sets; trays are the payoff, not the explanation. Tray copy is labels and taglines, not paragraphs.
- Each tray lifts out independently via hybrid lift tabs (see Section 9).
- Tray material: rigid paperboard or molded pulp. Dark carbon fiber texture finish — very dark charcoal/near-black with subtle sheen. Prison orange accent trim on top edge border and inside finger notches.
- Trays are full interior width of the front zone — no ring clearance needed.
- **Tray undersides are easter egg surfaces** — visible when each tray is lifted out (see Section 13).

*For all tray dimensions (exterior, interior, wall heights, floor thickness), see Physical Dimensions & Sourcing Spec.*

**Design principle:** The boxes ARE Brad Sterling's product. They look like actual retail products — the kind of over-packaged, buzzword-laden junk a corporate exec would produce. The humor is embedded in the copy itself.

**Design principle:** Hidden surfaces are bonus moments. Not everyone will flip a tray over or peer into tray walls, but those who do should be rewarded. One good line per surface. Keep it short. ### Assembly Sequence (Manual Steps)

1. Place shredded "CLASSIFIED" paper in mug compartment (thin foam sheet at bottom if needed)
2. Place mug on its side in compartment, handle to the side
3. Place J-shaped compartment cover — top surface over back zone, vertical surface acts as zone separator, floor flap extends forward into front zone
4. Place Doc Set 2 on top of compartment cover
5. Place Tray 2 in front zone on top of cover floor flap (item boxes already placed in die-cut recesses)
6. Place Tray 1 in front zone (item boxes already placed in die-cut recesses)
7. Place Document Set 1 on top of Tray 1
8. Insert check-in card and map into lid pockets
9. Close lid (magnets engage)
10. Slide belly band around briefcase (if belly band is retained — TBD)
11. Place briefcase in outer Amazon box
12. Close and seal outer box

**Note:** Individual box assembly is a new manual step — placing each of the 6 item boxes into its die-cut tray recess. This may be done as a sub-assembly (pre-load trays) before final case assembly.

### Shipping Considerations

- Mug lying on its side in shredded paper padding — must prevent all movement during transit. Confirm adequate protection with drop test; thin foam sheet at bottom as backup if needed.
- Outer Amazon box provides an additional layer of shipping protection around the briefcase.
- Compartment cover vertical surface provides zone separation — integral to the J-shaped cover, not a separate piece.
- J-shaped compartment cover must not shift during shipping (floor flap is held down by tray weight above; top surface rests on side walls and back wall).
- Belly band (if retained) provides additional closure security during transit.
- Total assembled weight TBD after all items are sourced.
- Briefcase footprint (14" x 11.5") inside outer box (~15.5" x 13" x 6.5") — factor into shipping cost.
- Back zone clearance is ~0.44" — tight but workable; confirm with prototype.
- Individual item boxes in die-cut recesses should not rattle — recesses must be snug fits.
