# ELEVATE Magazine — Full Structure

**Version:** v1
**Last Updated:** May 22, 2026
**Status:** LOCKED — Approved structure. Individual spreads to be developed.
**Format:** 8.5" x 11" portrait, full color, glossy, saddle-stitched magazine
**Supersedes:** `product/doc-sets/doc-set-1-copy-v1.md` (12-page booklet format)

> **What this document is:** The complete page-by-page structure of the ELEVATE magazine — the Doc Set 1 insert included in the Office Survival Kit. This document defines what goes on every page, what format each page uses, and what voice register each section operates in. Individual spread copy and image prompts are developed in separate documents and reference this structure.

> **What this document is NOT:** This is not the copy document. Actual written copy for each page lives in the Copy Document (see `copy-doc-v7.md`) or in dedicated spread development files. This is the architectural blueprint.

---

## The Format Concept

**Title:** *ELEVATE — The Official Magazine of Elevate Co. Workplace Solutions Division*

**Subtitle:** *Issue No. 1 — The Only Issue — A Brad Sterling Publication*

This is a magazine that Brad Sterling published. There is one issue. There will never be a second. Brad doesn't know that yet. He thinks this is the launch of a media empire.

The magazine format gives us variety of voices — feature articles, product ads, advice columns, testimonials, infographics — all under one editorial roof. Different sections have different energy, but they all live in the same upbeat, professional, enthusiastic-about-insane-things register.

---

## Voice Architecture

The product copy voice is **enthusiastic, upbeat, playful, persuasive** — a willing, polished participant in the insanity. Think of a magazine editor who is THRILLED about every piece of content. Brad's personal brand copy stays the same — hustle-culture, zero self-awareness, aspirational.

The comedy engine: **everyone is aggressively enthusiastic about the horror.** The format is SELLING you the insanity.

### Voice Styles by Section

| Voice Style | Used For | Energy |
|---|---|---|
| QVC / Infomercial | Product ads (Shank, Tags) | "YOU NEED THIS. Here's why." |
| Wellness / Self-Care | Lifer Ink ad, affirmation cards | "This is your healing journey." |
| Fitness / Transformation | Before/after features, stickers | "RESULTS. REAL PEOPLE." |
| Luxury Lifestyle | Hero product shots, the mug reveal | "For those who understand." |
| Pharma | Card deck ads, side effects | "Ask your manager if this is right for you." |
| Editorial / Feature | Narrative chapters | "Our investigative team reports..." |
| Advice Column | Dear Brad letters | "Brad answers YOUR questions." |
| Classified Ads | Small margin fillers | One-line gags in tiny type |

---

## Design Principles

- Every page earns its spot with either a laugh, a striking image, or both
- Copy stays light — 15-50 words per page max for most pages
- Images do 70% of the work
- Narrative chapters and product ads alternate to create rhythm
- The reader never goes more than 2 pages without a format change
- The format of each page should be immediately recognizable as a real magazine element before the content registers

## Page-by-Page Structure

### FRONT MATTER

#### Page 1 — Cover

Magazine cover format. Brad's face, huge smile, purple satin shirt, aviators. Headline teasers around the margins like a real magazine:
- "EXCLUSIVE: The Pen That Changed Everything (p. XX)"
- "Are You a Lifer? Take the Quiz (p. XX)"
- "7 Signs Your Cubicle Isn't Up to Code"
- "MEET THE FOUNDER — He's Exactly What You'd Expect"

Masthead: *ELEVATE — Issue No. 1 — $0.00 (Included Whether You Wanted It or Not)*

**Format:** Magazine cover
**Voice:** Brad Mode 2

---

#### Page 2 — Masthead / Table of Contents

Real magazine masthead format. Brad is listed as Editor-in-Chief, Creative Director, Lead Photographer, Head of Sales, Sole Subscriber, and Intern Liaison. Staff listing is just Brad under different titles.

Table of contents lists every feature and ad spread with page numbers.

Small text at bottom: *"ELEVATE Magazine is a publication of Elevate Co. Workplace Solutions Division. Circulation: 1. Readership: Aspirational."*

**Format:** Magazine front matter
**Voice:** Brad Mode 2

---

#### Page 3 — Letter from the Editor

Brad Mode 2. Full hustle-culture editorial letter. Brad welcomes the reader to "the inaugural issue of what will undoubtedly become the defining voice of workplace culture." He's launching a media property. He has plans for a podcast. He's considering a conference. This letter is 100% personal brand Brad — sincere, delusional, aspirational.

Keep it to 60-80 words. It should read like a real editor's letter from a magazine nobody asked for.

**Format:** Editorial letter
**Voice:** Brad Mode 2

---

### ACT 1: ASSESSMENT — "Your World, As We See It"

The narrative chapters. The reader's office life observed, reported on, celebrated with insane enthusiasm. These are the magazine's "feature articles."

---

#### Pages 4-5 — "WELCOME TO THE WORKFORCE" (Spread)

*Feature article format. Subhead: "A Field Guide to Your First Day."*

Reworked from the existing "Arrival" chapter. Same content — Day 1 intake, the photographing, the cataloguing — but now framed as an enthusiastic feature article. The magazine is CELEBRATING onboarding. "Congratulations! You've been processed!" The photography is bright, colorful, aspirational — like a university welcome brochure, but the content is prison intake.

Copy direction:
> Welcome to the team! On Day One, they'll photograph you, catalogue you, and assign you a number and a seat. Think of it as onboarding — but with less paperwork than actual prison. (Just kidding. More paperwork.)

Brad sidebar pull quote: *"I do the same thing. I just call it market research."*

**Format:** Feature article
**Voice:** Upbeat editorial

---

#### Pages 6-7 — "YOUR PERSONAL WORKSPACE" (Spread)

*Real estate listing format. Subhead: "Tour Your New Cell — Sorry, Cubicle."*

The cubicle presented as an aspirational real estate listing. Photography styled like Zillow — warm lighting, staged plants, the works. But the listing copy reveals the truth. "Open concept: walls stop at 5 feet. Natural light adjacent: 30 feet from the nearest window."

Dimensions callout: *"6x8 ft — A cubicle is 6x8. Death row is 6x9. We like to think of it as an upgrade."*

**Format:** Real estate listing
**Voice:** Wellness / aspirational

---

#### Pages 8-9 — "THE BLOCK: A NEIGHBORHOOD GUIDE" (Spread)

*Magazine neighborhood guide format. Think local lifestyle magazine covering a hip district.*

The office floor plan presented as a neighborhood guide. "The Mess Hall (break room): Known for its eclectic selection of expired condiments and passive-aggressive Post-it notes about fridge hygiene." "The Yard (common area): Great natural light. Terrible natural conversations."

The carpet union joke from the existing copy can be a sidebar: *"The carpet's been here since 1987. It's union."*

**Format:** Neighborhood lifestyle guide
**Voice:** Playful editorial

---

### PRODUCT AD BREAK #1

#### Pages 10-11 — CUBICLE SHANK AD (Spread)

*Full magazine product advertisement. Mixed voice: QVC enthusiasm + luxury product photography.*

Hero product spread for the Executive Tactical Stylus. This is the spread developed via the self-contained prompt (see `prompts/cubicle-shank-spread-prompt.md`).

**Format:** Product advertisement
**Voice:** QVC + luxury

---

### ACT 2: DIAGNOSIS — "We've Identified the Problem (It's You)"

The daily grind. Brad has studied the reader's behavior and has findings. The magazine reports on them with the enthusiasm of a wellness publication covering a breakthrough.

---

#### Pages 12-13 — "THE MORNING LOADOUT" (Spread)

*Tactical gear review format. Subhead: "What You're Already Carrying — And What It Actually Is."*

Reworked from existing "Loadout" chapter. Same content — coffee as NERVE AGENT, headphones as PERIMETER DEFENSE, laptop as SHIELD, pen as CUBICLE SHANK — but now presented as an enthusiastic gear review, like an outdoor adventure magazine reviewing hiking equipment. "This season's must-have office loadout includes..."

The tactical labels stay. The voice around them becomes enthusiastic gear-reviewer energy.

**Format:** Gear review
**Voice:** Adventure magazine

---

#### Pages 14-15 — "THE CODE: YOUR COMPLETE TRANSLATION GUIDE" (Spread)

*Infographic / decoder format. Subhead: "What They Said vs. What They Meant."*

Reworked from existing "Code" chapter. Same translations — "Sounds good" means no, "Let's circle back" means never — but presented as a colorful, well-designed infographic. Magazine-quality design, the kind of thing you'd tear out and pin to your wall.

Brad's line stays: *"You've been speaking in code for years. I just put it in writing."*

**Format:** Infographic / decoder
**Voice:** Colorful editorial

---

### PRODUCT AD BREAK #2

#### Page 16 — LIFER INK AD (Full page)

*Wellness / self-care brand advertisement. "Your healing journey starts with temporary tattoos."*

Wellness voice. "Some milestones deserve more than a LinkedIn post. They deserve ink." Bright, aspirational photography of someone proudly displaying a tally-mark tattoo on their forearm. The ad treats temporary tattoos as a genuine self-care ritual.

Tagline: *"Not given. Earned."*

**Format:** Wellness brand ad
**Voice:** Self-care

---

#### Page 17 — TURF TAGS AD (Full page)

*Real estate / territory / fitness transformation voice. Before/After format.*

"BEFORE: Shared desk. Stolen lunch. No boundaries. AFTER: Claimed. Marked. Yours."

Bright photography of a workspace covered in territory stickers. The ad sells stickers the way a home security company sells alarm systems.

**Format:** Territory / transformation ad
**Voice:** Transformation

---

### ACT 3: INTERVENTION — "Brad Has the Answer"

Brad arrives with the solution. The narrative turns from "your world" to "what Brad built for you."

---

#### Pages 18-19 — "THE EXPERIMENT" (Spread)

*Investigative journalism format. Subhead: "What Happened When We Left a Pen in Conference Room B."*

Reworked from existing "Conference Room B" chapter. Same story — Brad plants a pen, the office weaponizes it — but now framed as investigative reporting. The magazine sent a journalist. There are witness statements. There's a timeline. It's presented like a New Yorker longform piece condensed to a spread, but the subject is a pen.

The locked copy stays but the framing around it is enthusiastic: *"The results surprised even our research team."*

**Format:** Investigative journalism
**Voice:** Longform editorial

---

#### Pages 20-21 — "THE IDENTITY CRISIS" (Spread)

*Fashion/lifestyle editorial format. Subhead: "You've Got the Suit. Where's the Proof?"*

Reworked from existing "Ink" chapter. The contrast between surface-level status signals and actual earned marks. Framed as a fashion editorial that pivots: the magazine celebrates the suit, the haircut, the watch — then asks where the REAL proof of time served is.

*"Where's the mark for the lunch that didn't make it? For being a flight risk? You earned your ink. You just haven't worn it yet."*

**Format:** Fashion editorial
**Voice:** Lifestyle

---

### PRODUCT AD BREAK #3

#### Pages 22-23 — CARD DECK TRIPLE AD (Spread)

*Three mini-ads on one spread, each in a different voice:*

**Cell Talk** (left third) — Pharma voice:
*"Side effects may include: unsolicited self-esteem, brief moments of optimism, and the quiet realization that someone left this on your desk on purpose."*

**Rap Sheets** (center third) — Dating app voice:
*"Valentine energy on the front. The truth on the back. Swipe to flip."*

**Sweet Nothings** (right third) — Espionage/intelligence voice:
*"Deploy silently. Leave no evidence. Watch results from a safe distance."*

Each mini-ad has a hero product photo and 15-20 words. The three different voices on one page demonstrate the magazine's range and give the reader three quick laughs.

**Format:** Triple mini-ad
**Voice:** Pharma / dating / espionage

---

### THE REVEAL

#### Page 24 — "DEAR BRAD" (Advice Column)

*Magazine advice column format. 2-3 fake reader letters, Brad's answers.*

Letters from "readers" asking Brad for workplace advice. Brad's answers are enthusiastically wrong but delivered with total confidence. This is Mode 2 Brad — the advice is hustle-culture nonsense that accidentally sounds motivational.

Example:
> *"Dear Brad, My coworker keeps stealing my lunch. What do I do?" — Hungry in HR*
>
> *"Great question. Have you considered that you're not protecting your lunch — you're failing to establish territorial sovereignty? See page 17."*

**Format:** Advice column
**Voice:** Brad Mode 2

---

#### Pages 25-26 — "MEET THE FOUNDER" (Spread)

*Magazine profile / interview format. The Brad portrait spread.*

Brad's full portrait — purple satin, gold chain, aviators, holding the open briefcase. Framed as a magazine profile. Pull quotes from Brad about himself. His credentials. His vision. His mission statement. This is 100% Mode 2 Brad. The reader has been laughing WITH the magazine for 24 pages. Now they're laughing AT Brad. The gap is the joke.

*"Everybody's trying to survive. That's the problem. Nobody's trying to win."*

**Format:** Magazine profile
**Voice:** Brad Mode 2

---

#### Page 27 — "THE CONFISCATED" (Full page)

*Evidence report / incident file format.*

The Confiscated Stapler. A full-page incident report about the empty recess. Police-report styling but with magazine production values — glossy, well-photographed chalk outline, vibrant colors. An enthusiastic write-up of a workplace tragedy.

*"CONFISCATED — WEAPONIZED OFFICE EQUIPMENT. INCIDENT #147. DO NOT REISSUE."*

This page works as both comedy AND as a teaser for the actual kit — the reader will find this empty recess in Tray 2.

**Format:** Evidence report / incident file
**Voice:** Institutional with magazine polish

---

#### Pages 28-29 — MUG SHOT REVEAL (Spread)

*Luxury lifestyle product reveal. The finale.*

The mug presented as a luxury lifestyle product. Moody, beautiful photography. The mug in shredded classified documents. This is the one spread that earns the quieter, luxury voice — because it's the emotional peak of the kit. The mug is discovered last, remembered most.

*"You've done your time. This is your mug shot."*

**Format:** Luxury product reveal
**Voice:** Quiet luxury

---

### BACK MATTER

#### Page 30 — Fine Print / Back Cover

The existing legal comedy, adapted for magazine format. Subscription offer: *"Subscribe to ELEVATE Magazine: $0/year. You're already subscribed. You can't unsubscribe. This is your life now."*

Classifieds section at the bottom: 2-3 tiny classified ads for absurd office services.

**Format:** Legal + classifieds
**Voice:** Brad Mode 1 + Mode 2

## Narrative Arc (Preserved from Booklet)

The three-act structure carries over from the original Doc Set 1 booklet, now reframed as magazine editorial sections:

| Act | Pages | Theme | Magazine Framing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Assessment | 4-9 | Your world, as we see it | Feature articles about your office life |
| Diagnosis | 12-15 | The daily grind, Brad identifies the problem (you) | Gear reviews and infographics |
| Intervention | 18-21 | Brad has studied the problem and has a solution | Investigative features and fashion editorials |

Product ads fill the breaks between acts. The reveal section (pages 24-29) shifts from the magazine's editorial voice to Brad's personal brand — the reader has been laughing WITH the magazine, now they're laughing AT Brad.
