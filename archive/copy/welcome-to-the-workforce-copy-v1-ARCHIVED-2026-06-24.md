## Pages 4-5 — The Intake (Feature Article Opener Spread)

**Headline (Article Headline — Freight Display Pro Bold, 48/48pt):**
Welcome to the Workforce

**Deck (Article Deck — Freight Text Pro Medium, 14/19pt):**
A Field Guide to Your First Day — From Processing to Placement

**Byline:**
BY BRAD STERLING

### Body Copy — First Paragraph (with 3-line drop cap)

Your first day starts the way all great careers do: standing in a line, holding a clipboard, waiting for someone to tell you where to sit. Don't worry — they've streamlined the process. You'll be photographed, catalogued, assigned a number, and issued a lanyard before you've finished your first cup of coffee. Think of it as onboarding. Corporate calls it "new hire orientation." The rest of us call it intake.

### Body Copy — Continuation

They'll walk you through the facility. They'll introduce you to people whose names you'll forget within the hour. Someone from Internal Affairs — sorry, Human Resources — will hand you a stack of forms and ask you to initial seventeen things you won't read. You'll sign a code of conduct. You'll sign a confidentiality agreement. You'll sign something that may or may not waive your right to a window seat.

By lunch, you'll have a badge with a photo that looks nothing like you, a temporary password you'll never successfully change, and the growing suspicion that "unlimited PTO" means something different here than it did in the listing.

Congratulations. You've been processed.

>
> — BRAD STERLING, Founder & Sole Employee, Elevate Co.

## Copy Notes

### Voice Calibration

- **Pages 4-5:** The voice is warm, upbeat, and welcoming — but the content reveals that "welcoming" is relative. The humor comes from the gap between orientation-brochure enthusiasm and the reality being described. Nobody breaks character. The magazine is genuinely excited about intake.

- **Page 6:** The schedule is the comedy engine here. Each time slot is its own micro-joke. The format (daily planner) is the straight man. The entries escalate from plausible (arrive early) to absurd (the clock stares back). The DID YOU KNOW callout is the kicker — presented as data, delivered as a punchline.

- **Page 7:** The voice shifts to real estate listing copy — aspirational, breezy, using property-listing vocabulary ("open concept," "natural light adjacent," "previously occupied"). The specs table is the comedy payload. The "Related Properties" footer should be visually styled exactly like Zillow/Realtor.com thumbnails. Each listing is 2-3 sentences max.
