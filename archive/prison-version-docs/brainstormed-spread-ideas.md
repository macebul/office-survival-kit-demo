## Brainstormed Categories & Ideas (Need Development)

### 1. "Official Forms & Documents"
> **STATUS: ON HOLD** — Risk of being too text-heavy / just copywriting. People may flip past dense form pages without a strong visual hook. Revisit later — may work better as a single-page accent rather than a full spread. Needs a visual concept that makes the form itself visually interesting, not just funny to read.

Fake institutional paperwork that looks real at first glance, then reveals itself.
- **Intake Processing Form** — "Employee Onboarding Questionnaire" asking things like allergies, emergency contact, preferred method of restraint (open floor plan / cubicle / solitary office)
- **Commissary Order Sheet** — office supplies listed like prison commissary items with inmate pricing and trade values
- **Incident Report (Blank)** — corporate HR incident form with checkboxes like "Unauthorized fraternization at the Keurig," "Contraband snacks in desk drawer," "Attempted escape (left before 5pm)"
- **Visitor Log** — sign-in sheet for "the outside" visitors, columns for relationship to inmate, items surrendered at reception, duration of approved visit
- Comedy mechanic: these look 100% like real forms. The joke is in the details.

### 2. "Institutional Signage"
Spreads that look like official posted notices, safety posters, or motivational signage.
- **Motivational Posters** — stock-photo-style inspirational posters where the text is prison-honest. Sunset over mountains: "PERSEVERANCE: Only 11,680 meetings until retirement."
- **Safety Warnings** — OSHA-style safety placards. "This floor has gone [3] days without a passive-aggressive email"
- **Posted Rules** — "Break Room Code of Conduct" that reads like yard rules
- **Evacuation Plan** — "In case of all-hands meeting, remain calm. There is no exit."
- Comedy mechanic: institutional signage format is inherently authoritative. Prison-office content does the joke.

### 3. "Maps & Diagrams"
> **STATUS: ON HOLD** — Strong visual concept but holding until art direction is finalized. The illustration style will heavily determine whether this works as a schematic/blueprint look, a comic illustration, or something else. Revisit once the new art direction is locked.

Architectural or schematic drawings reframing the office as a correctional facility.
- **Facility Map** — office floor plan labeled as correctional institution. Conference rooms = "interrogation suites," break room = "the yard," corner offices = "warden's quarters," open plan = "general population"
- **Organizational Chart** — prison hierarchy (warden > guards > trustees > general pop) that's actually a corporate org chart
- **Escape Route Analysis** — tactical diagram of paths to exit before 5pm without being seen by your manager
- Comedy mechanic: visual precision + absurd labeling. Rewards close reading.

### 4. "Survival Guides & How-Tos"
Instructional content with dead-serious institutional formatting.
- **How to Survive Your First Day** — intake processing tips that are actually onboarding advice
- **Contraband Concealment Guide** — hiding personal items (phone, snacks, headphones) from management walkthroughs
- **Alliance Formation Protocol** — identifying reliable workplace allies, presented as prison yard survival
- **The Art of Looking Busy** — tactical guide with diagrams
- **Commissary Economics** — barter economy of office supplies, who controls the good stapler, K-cup exchange rates
- Comedy mechanic: genuine useful-ish office advice through prison survival framing.

### 5. "Profiles & Dossiers"
Character sheets, mugshots, or institutional records for office archetypes.
- **Inmate Profiles** — mugshot-style portraits of archetypes (The Reply-All Offender, The Micromanager, The Kitchen Thief, The Loud Talker). Rap sheets with charges, sentence, known associates
- **Staff Profiles** — "guards" (HR, IT, facilities) with specialties and threat levels
- **Snitch Registry** — who tells the boss things, as a formal intelligence document
- Comedy mechanic: everyone recognizes these people. Prison-record format lets you say things about coworkers that'd be HR violations in plain language.

### 6. "Classifications & Taxonomies"
Scientific/institutional classification systems applied to office phenomena.
- **Threat Level Assessment** — color-coded meeting system (green: optional, could skip / red: CEO present, no survivors)
- **Email Decoding Guide** — translation chart. "Per my last email" = evidence submission. "Let's circle back" = solitary confinement. "As discussed" = you're on record
- **Noise Classification System** — decibel chart of office sounds ranked by psychic damage
- **Food Chain Diagram** — ecosystem chart of who eats whose lunch (literally and metaphorically)
- Comedy mechanic: authority of classification systems applied to petty office grievances.

### 7. "Contraband & Evidence"
Mundane office items presented as seized goods or evidence exhibits.
- **Evidence Locker Inventory** — photographs of confiscated items (desk fan, branded mug, headphones) with evidence tags and case numbers
- **Shiv Identification Guide** — office supplies assessed for tactical potential (extends the Cubicle Shank product into a full taxonomy)
- **Approved vs. Prohibited Items** — workstation rules formatted like prison commissary regulations
- Comedy mechanic: treating mundane objects with forensic seriousness.

### 8. "Communications & Correspondence"
Fake letters, memos, and messages.
- **Intercepted Communications** — emails between coworkers with "redacted" sections about mundane things (who stole the good chair, who microwaved fish)
- **Letters Home** — "Dear family, it has been 47 days since I entered the open floor plan..."
- **Official Memoranda** — corporate memos that read like warden's notices. "Effective immediately, all inmates—employees—will surrender personal space heaters."
- **Denied Requests** — stack of PTO/WFH requests with escalating denial stamps
- Comedy mechanic: epistolary comedy. Voice of institution vs. voice of inmate.

### 9. "Statistics & Data"
Infographic-style spreads with charts and data visualizations.
- **Recidivism Rates** — percentage who say "I'm never going back to the office" then go back
- **Time Served Calculator** — formula based on age, retirement target, and will to live
- **Satisfaction Survey Results** — bar charts with devastating data. "72% of respondents described morale as 'present'"
- **Daily Schedule Breakdown** — pie chart of 8-hour day (actual work: 3.2 hours, looking busy: 2.1 hours, existential dread: 45 min)
- Comedy mechanic: the authority of data. Charts don't lie. (These ones do.)

### 10. "Wellness & Rehabilitation"
Parodies of corporate wellness programs through corrections lens.
- **Rehabilitation Program Options** — "Career Development" named like prison rehab (Anger Management -> Slack Etiquette, Substance Abuse -> Coffee Dependency Reduction)
- **Mental Health Resources** — wellness poster that's accidentally honest. "Remember: your feelings are valid. They are also not billable."
- **Exercise Yard Schedule** — walk to/from parking lot reframed as recreation time
- **Chaplain's Hours** — the one coworker everyone vents to
- Comedy mechanic: corporate wellness culture is already so close to parody you barely have to push it.

## Success Metrics Each Spread Should Hit
- **Shareability** — works as standalone photo/post
- **Recognition humor** — "I know that person / I've been in that meeting"
- **Rereadability** — dense detail that rewards close inspection
- **Gift-ability** — recipient knows immediately what it is
- **Edginess** — prison framing lets you say true things that'd sound bitter without the comedy wrapper
