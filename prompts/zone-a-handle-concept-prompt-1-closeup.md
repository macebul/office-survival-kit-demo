# Image Prompt: Zone A Handle Concept — Closeup View

> **Purpose:** Design team reference image showing ONLY the Zone A portion of the blister card backing — the "BONUS SHIELD" handle strip concept.
> **Date:** July 6, 2026
> **Context:** This is for the left column of the L-shaped blister card backing (4.75" wide x 7.00" tall). The concept shows a die-cut handle on the left side with printed instructional content on the right side.

---

## Prompt

A close-up of a single vertical cardboard strip, approximately 4.75 inches wide by 7 inches tall, printed on white/cream heavy cardstock (18pt paperboard). This is one portion of a larger product backing card that has been cropped to show only this section.

**Left side of the strip (~1 inch wide):** A narrow vertical rounded-rectangle die-cut hole punched cleanly through the cardboard. This is a physical cutout — you can see through it. It runs most of the height of the strip. This is a handle opening sized for 2-3 fingers to grip through from the left edge of the card.

**Right side of the strip (~3.5 inches wide):** Printed instructional content, arranged top to bottom:

1. **"BONUS" header** at the top — bold, colorful, eye-catching. This should POP against the rest of the card. Think bright color block background (yellow, orange, or red starburst/banner), large bold stencil or block lettering. It should immediately read as "free extra value" the way a cereal box advertises a prize inside. This is the most visually energetic element on the strip.

2. **"SHIELD"** directly below in large, clean, institutional block text. Black or dark. Straightforward.

3. **Three simple stick-figure instructional diagrams** stacked vertically, each showing a step of how to use this cardboard strip as a defensive shield. IKEA assembly instruction style — clean black line art on white, minimal detail, deadpan.
   - Step 1: Figure gripping the handle cutout FROM THE LEFT SIDE of the strip (fingers through the die-cut opening on the left edge), holding the cardboard vertically in front of their body like a riot shield.
   - Step 2: Figure in defensive stance, cardboard strip held up by the left-side handle, deflecting something (paper airplane, crumpled memo, passive-aggressive email printout).
   - Step 3: Figure standing victorious behind the cardboard shield, still gripping from the left-side handle.

   **CRITICAL: The figure holds the card from the LEFT SIDE where the handle cutout is — NOT from the center of the card.**

   Each step has a very short label (2-4 words max) like "GRIP" / "DEFLECT" / "SURVIVE" — images do the heavy lifting, not copy.

4. **NO fine print, NO disclaimer, NO safety note at the bottom.** The space is too tight. The diagrams and BONUS header fill the strip.

**Overall style:** The strip should look like a real printed product insert or packaging panel. Clean, institutional, professional print quality — but the BONUS header breaks that institutional tone with color and energy. The diagrams are deadpan instructional while the content is absurd. The format is the straight man.

**Camera angle:** Flat lay or very slight perspective, shot from directly above or at a shallow angle. The cardboard strip is lying on a neutral surface (light gray, white desk, or craft paper). Natural lighting. The die-cut hole is visible — you can see the surface underneath through it.

**Do NOT include:** The rest of the blister card, any product, any blister plastic, any retail context. This is JUST the Zone A strip isolated.

## Reference Notes for Design Team

- This strip is the left column of the L-shaped blister card backing
- The die-cut handle replaces the traditional hanging hole — it serves as both extraction grip and retail hang point
- Each of the 7 cards in the kit will have a DIFFERENT "bonus" alternate use (Shield, Sword, Boomerang, Shark Fin, Flyswatter, Privacy Screen, Shovel)
- This image shows the Shield version as the reference example
- The BONUS header is universal across all 7 cards — the use name and diagrams change per card
- Key visual principle: BONUS header = colorful, energetic, eye-catching. Everything else = institutional, deadpan, clean
