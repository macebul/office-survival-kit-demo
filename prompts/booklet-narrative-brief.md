# OFFICE SURVIVAL KIT — BOOKLET NARRATIVE BRIEF

## PRODUCT IN ONE SENTENCE
A premium gag gift: a matte black briefcase containing 7 survival-themed items (+ 1 empty recess) and 2 document sets, framing office life as a literal prison sentence.

**Tagline:** "Standard issue for your corporate sentence."
**Price:** ~$25–30 | **Target:** Office workers, coworker gift-givers

---

## THE WORLD RULE

**"The office is a prison. This is not a metaphor."**

Nobody thinks it's clever to compare office to prison — there's nothing to compare. The vocabulary of incarceration IS the vocabulary of corporate life. The tone is **sardonic, not cynical** — people haven't given up; they've adapted.

**Core vocabulary (the water everyone swims in):**
- Doing time / serving your sentence = working at a company
- Your cell = cubicle or desk
- The yard = break room
- Contraband = anything making the day bearable
- Lifers = long-tenured employees
- Release day = last day at a job
- The warden = upper management / HR
- Intake / processing = onboarding
- Shank = your pen, your reliable tool
- Ink = tattoos marking time served (earned, not given)
- Turf = your desk, your claimed spaces
- Rap sheet = performance review history

## BRAD STERLING — THE CHARACTER

**Who:** Chief Vision Officer & Founder, Elevate Co. Initials: B.S.

**Look:** Late 30s, purple satin shirt (unbuttoned to mid-chest), oversized gold rope chain (80s rapper scale), aviators indoors, pinky ring, permanent cheesy teeth-too-white smile.

**Key fact:** Brad has never worked in an office. He's an entrepreneur — always on the outside. He speaks the office-as-prison vocabulary fluently because it's his market, but he's never lived it. Like a guy opening a military surplus store without having served.

**Two modes (same person, different subjects):**

1. **Product Copy Brad** (COMPETENT) — Sardonic, institutional, straight-faced humor. Actually funny. Format stays buttoned-up; content escalates into absurdity. This Brad knows how to sell a gag gift.

2. **Personal Brand Brad** (DELUSIONAL) — Aspirational, self-mythologizing, motivational-speaker cadence. Uses "premium" constantly. Self-generated awards. Zero self-awareness. Never winks — completely sincere.

**Comedy rule:** "Format stays buttoned-up. Content gets unleashed." The institutional format (labels, FDA layouts, corporate letterhead) is the straight man. The actual words are the comedian.

## DOCUMENT SET 1: "FROM THE DESK OF BRAD STERLING"

**Format:** Saddle-stitched booklet, ~5.5" × 7" portrait, full color, glossy. Currently spec'd at 16 pages (cover + back cover + 14 interior).

**Position:** First thing visible when briefcase opens (on top of Tray 1).

**Voice:** 100% Brad Sterling / Elevate Co.

**Visual style:** Generated photorealistic/styled imagery. Brad appears on every page in full visual DNA. Each page is 2–3 image vignettes with 10–25 words of copy woven between. Images carry half the comedy — copy and visuals are one integrated scene.

### Current 3-Act Narrative Arc

**PHASE 1: ASSESSMENT (Brad observes your world)**
- Page 2 "Arrival" — Day one parallels: your intake vs. Brad's "market research"
- Page 3 "Your Desk" — Cubicle is 6×8; death row is 6×9. Enjoy the upgrade.
- Page 4 "The Block" — The office hallway, the lifers, the carpet that's outlasted everyone
- Page 5 "The Instincts" — Reversal: it's not the people that matter, it's the survival behaviors

**PHASE 2: DIAGNOSIS (Brad identifies the problem — it's you)**
- Pages 6–7 "Conference Room B" (spread) — Brad leaves a pen; by Thursday it has a name and a reputation. Origin of the Cubicle Shank.
- Pages 8–9 "The Loadout" (spread) — Tactical equipment diagram format applied to mundane office items (coffee = NERVE AGENT, headphones = PERIMETER DEFENSE)
- Pages 10–11 "The Ink" (spread) — New suit doesn't prove anything. Where's the mark for the lunch that didn't make it?
- Pages 12–13 "The Code" (spread) — "Sounds good" means no. "Per my last email" means war.

**PHASE 3: INTERVENTION (Brad arrives with the solution)**
- Pages 14–15 "The Reveal" (spread) — Brad holding the open briefcase. "Everybody's trying to survive. That's the problem. Nobody's trying to win."
- Page 16 "Back Cover" — Dense legal comedy fine print (contradictions, confessions Brad doesn't realize he's making)

**Design philosophy:** Format (tactical loadout, institutional documents, legal fine print) is always the straight man. Content escalates into absurdity. The reader who scans laughs; the close reader laughs harder. Density is a feature.

---

## WHAT THE BACKSTORY NEEDS TO ENABLE

The booklet's narrative arc needs a coherent backstory so that:
1. Brad's observations in Phase 1 feel grounded in specific (fictional) events, not generic office jokes
2. Phase 2's escalation has callbacks and continuity (the pen from page 6 connects to the loadout on page 8)
3. Phase 3's "reveal" feels earned — Brad's pivot from observer to profiteer lands because the reader has been on a journey
4. Every page can reference shared lore (incidents, characters, locations, running gags) that reward close reading
5. Card deck content, box copy, and fine print can all draw from the same universe without contradicting each other
