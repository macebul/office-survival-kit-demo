## Comedy Principles — READ THIS SECTION CAREFULLY

**This is the single most important section in this prompt.** Every previous session has failed for the same reason: writing lines that describe things or make observations without any actual comedy technique. Lines that are "clever" or "dark" or "institutional" but have no surprise, no flip, no moment where the reader's brain pivots.

### What MUST Be In Every Line

**1. Reversals / Subversion** — Reader expects one thing, gets another. Brain does a flip.
- "IF SWALLOWED, CONTACT YOUR SUPERVISOR. NOT BECAUSE IT'S DANGEROUS. BECAUSE WE HAVE QUESTIONS." — You expect "dangerous." You get "questions." Surprise.
- "AGES 16+. Child labor laws. You can't shank someone if you can't legally be in the building." — Age limit isn't about SAFETY, it's about workplace ACCESS. Logic inverts.
- "Study discontinued. Not because of the goose. We wish it had been the goose." — Misdirect, then reversal reveals it was WORSE.

**2. Puns / Wordplay / Multiple Simultaneous Meanings** — Words that work 2+ ways at once. Reader discovers layers on re-reading.
- "The pen that ends sentences. May also extend yours." — Triple meaning: grammar / prison / killing. Mirror + contrast.
- "We put the pen in penitentiary. The con in conference room. The shank in Shawshank. And the stab in stability." — Each word IS inside the larger word. Story order.
- "For doing hard time in someone else's cell." — Prison cell + spreadsheet cell. Both readings active simultaneously.

**3. Escalation** — Each beat makes it worse. FORMAT stays steady.
- "Through the sentence. Through the paragraph. Through a light jacket..." — Material escalation, ellipsis lets imagination work.
- "Patent pending. Trial also pending. Jury still out. Literally." — Each sentence recontextualizes the previous one.
- "Nominations are closed. They were never open. Participation was not optional." — Three beats, each worse.

**4. Specificity** — Concrete nouns, specific details, visual images.
- "Kevin's says LIFE instead of LIFER" — you SEE the misspelled tattoo
- "She's been there 11 years" — specific number, specific person
- "Conference Room B. Specifically." — not just "banned," banned from a SPECIFIC room

**5. Flow / Rhythm** — Must sound good read aloud. Short beats. Parallel structure.
- "Fear, tears, then beers" — four words, rhyming, three-stage narrative
- "Smooth ink. Smooth barrel. Smooth criminal." — parallel structure, pattern break on third
- "Bold. Brave. Washable." — three beats, third deflates

**6. Playful / Warm / Conspiratorial Tone** — A friend showing you something ridiculous. Excited, not dark.
- The darkness is in the PRODUCT/SITUATION, not the TONE
- "We're genuinely flattered" energy — cheerful about absurd things
- "You will touch it. We've never seen anyone not touch it." — warm, knowing, shared experience
- Think: someone who found the most absurd product and can't wait to tell you about it

### What Does NOT Work — Failure Modes From 11+ Sessions

These are the specific patterns that have been rejected REPEATEDLY. Do not do these:

- **Observations without jokes** — "Karen has been here three years" describes a situation. It's not a joke. There's no flip, no surprise, no moment where the reader's expectation gets inverted.
- **Institutional straight-man copy** — Playing it so straight there's no comedy layer at all. "RECIPIENTS OF THE ELEVATE CO. COMMENDATION FOR DISTINGUISHED SERVICE" — this is a description, not comedy.
- **Dark consequence humor** — Threatening, intimidating, cynical tone. "Applied the barbed wire before standup. My manager hasn't made eye contact since." The TONE should be warm even when content is dark.
- **Walls of text** — Paragraphs of copy on packaging. The Cubicle Shank uses charts, quotes, bullets, discrete blocks. Keep everything SHORT.
- **Explaining the joke** — If you have to explain why it's funny, it isn't.
- **Classified ad format** — Tried and rejected for product description. Wrong world. This is a commendation program, not a want ad.
- **Generic insults** — Every insult needs absurd specificity.
- **Random one-liners** — Disconnected jokes that don't build toward anything usable on a specific surface.
- **Describing what the product does** — "These are temporary tattoos that commemorate office achievements" is a product description, not comedy.
- **Recycling rejected concepts** — Read the Cut tables. Don't bring back killed lines in different clothes.

### The Kill List (never do these)

- No exclamation points in product copy
- No winking at the reader ("get it?")
- No actual workplace advice
- No making Brad self-aware — he never realizes the contradiction
- No acknowledging the office/prison comparison as a metaphor — the office IS a prison
- No puns in general copy (puns ARE allowed in taglines and wordplay-specific slots per Section 4.5)

### Humor Elements Checklist

Every piece of copy needs at least 2 of these 6 elements. "Recognizable" should almost always be one:

1. **Clever** — wordplay, structure, hidden layers
2. **Bizarre** — unexpected, surreal, "wait what?"
3. **Cruel** — pointed, specific, earned meanness (not generic)
4. **Naughty** — edgy, pushing boundaries, "should they be saying this?"
5. **Recognizable** — "oh my god that's MY office" — the audience sees themselves
6. **Cute** — endearing, warm, makes you like the brand

## Voice Standard

**The Witty Yeti (WY) voice** is a warm, cheerful, conspiratorial friend who is excited to show you something ridiculous.

The best locked example on Lifer Ink is the application instructions (APP-3v2):

> 1. CUT around design. *(Custom modification? We support it.)*
> 2. REMOVE clear film. *(Two layers. You'll remove the wrong one first.)*
> 3. PLACE face-down on skin. *(Counterintuitive. Like most good decisions.)*
> 4. APPLY wet cloth, 30 seconds. *(The longest 30 seconds that doesn't involve a microwave.)*
> 5. PEEL backing slowly. *(Panic is normal. Peeling too fast is why Kevin's says "LIFE" instead of "LIFER.")*
> 6. ALLOW to dry, 60 seconds. *(You will touch it. We've never seen anyone not touch it.)*
> 7. DISPLAY. *(Optional. But you didn't go through all that for nothing.)*

**Why it works:** Talks TO the buyer. Cheerful about mishaps. Specific concrete images (Kevin's misspelled tattoo, the microwave, touching it). Warm and conspiratorial — sharing insider knowledge. Jokes land LAST in each line. Format is real (numbered application steps) while content goes sideways.

The best locked examples on Cubicle Shank:
- Seal: "GOOD SHANKING SEAL OF APPROVAL" / "That'll buff out." — Carl, Custodial
- Subtitle: "The pen that ends sentences. May also extend yours."
- Warning: "CAUTION: Not a letter opener. We know. We saw the video. So did the jury."
- Fine print: "Patent pending. Trial also pending. Jury still out. Literally."
- Assisted Shiving: "Fits walkers, canes, and hip replacements. Talk to your doctor." / "Finally." — Edith, Room 4

## Quality Bar — Self-Check Before Presenting ANY Copy

1. Is there an actual comedy MECHANIC here (reversal, pun, escalation, misdirect, pattern-break)? Can I NAME the technique? If not, the line doesn't have one.
2. Would this make an office worker laugh and show it to a coworker? Not nod — LAUGH.
3. Does this have layers — does a second reading reveal something the first missed?
4. Is the tone warm and playful, or cold and institutional?
5. Does it flow well read aloud? Does it have rhythm?
6. Is it SHORT enough? Could it be shorter and still land?
7. Is there surprise? Does the reader's expectation get inverted at some point?
8. Have I checked the Cut tables to make sure this approach hasn't already been tried and rejected?
