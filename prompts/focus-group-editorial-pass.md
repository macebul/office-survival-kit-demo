# Focus Group Editorial Pass — Card Deck Content

## Context

You are helping me edit the card deck content for the **Office Survival Kit**, a prison-themed office humor gift product. The product has a single fictional character — **Brad Sterling**, Chief Vision Officer & Founder of **Elevate Co.** — whose voice appears across all surfaces.

I ran a 5-persona AI focus group (Target Buyer, Amazon Seller, Comedy Writer, Gift Recipient, CPG Packaging Designer) across two rounds. Round 1 evaluated the full product. Round 2 evaluated the actual card deck content. The results were strongly positive — all 5 panelists unanimously agreed to keep all 3 decks — but identified specific editorial issues that need to be resolved before production.

This prompt contains everything you need to execute the editorial pass.

## The Three Decks — Format Summary

### Deck 1: Cell Talk — Self-Actualization Protocol Cards (15 cards)
- **Front:** A sincere, recognizable affirmation (the kind you see on Instagram, office posters, therapy worksheets)
- **Back:** Brad Sterling's narcissistic reinterpretation — he agrees with the affirmation but accidentally confesses to being a sociopath
- **Use case:** Solo reading, desk display, Slack sharing, personal humor
- **Comedy engine:** Character delusion — the gap between what Brad thinks he's saying and what he's actually revealing

### Deck 2: Rap Sheets — Interpersonal Feedback Delivery System (15 cards)
- **Front:** A universal office misdemeanor/charge (something everyone has witnessed)
- **Back:** Brad's prosecutorial response — he escalates the minor offense into existential indictment, sometimes hijacking it to brag about himself
- **Use case:** Hand to the offending coworker, leave on their desk, read aloud in groups
- **Comedy engine:** Observational escalation + Brad as judge/jury/motivational speaker
- **Strongest deck per panel consensus — varied mechanisms, highest re-read value**

### Deck 3: Sweet Nothings — Ambient Communication Cards (15 cards)
- **Front:** A genuinely nice office compliment (3-7 words)
- **Back:** A hyper-specific, absurd insult built around an everyday image so bizarre nobody can take it personally
- **Use case:** Leave on desks, hand to coworkers, tuck in birthday cards — tactical deployment
- **Comedy engine:** Extended simile/metaphor — the CRAFT of the insult is funnier than the insult itself
- **NOT in Brad's voice** — this deck is the world's anonymous ambient cruelty

## Editorial Issues to Resolve

There are 7 categories of issues identified by the focus group. Work through them in this order.

---

### ISSUE 1: Over-Explanation (Highest Priority)

**The problem:** At least 8 cards would be funnier if they lost their last sentence. The joke lands, then one more sentence explains it. The OG manual never does this — it trusts the reader. The cards need to learn the difference between a **reframe** (adds a new angle) and an **explanation** (restates what the reader already found).

**Specific cards flagged for trimming:**

| Card | Current Ending | Recommended Cut | Rationale |
|------|---------------|-----------------|-----------|
| Cell Talk #5 | "...The universe provides. You just have to be willing to take what it provides before the other person gets back." | Cut everything after "The universe provides." or even after "unattended." | The desk-theft joke already landed. The rest narrates the joke. |
| Cell Talk #13 | "...That's not a contradiction. That's self-sufficiency." | Cut this final sentence | Reader already made the leap from "I gave myself all of them" |
| Cell Talk #15 | "...During business hours. They'll come around." | Cut "They'll come around." | Stronger ending on "During business hours." — the staccato should end on the knife twist, not a softener |
| Cell Talk #3 | "...That's peace. That's what peace is." | Cut "That's what peace is." | Redundant — "That's peace" is the complete joke. The doubling is Brad insisting, which COULD work, but 3 of 5 panelists flagged it as padding. Your call. |
| Sweet Nothings #15 | "...With spots. And at some point everybody just agrees it's time for banana bread." | Consider cutting after "With spots." | "Banana bread" is affectionate — almost circles back to being sweet, which undercuts the insult. Counter-argument: 2 panelists loved the banana bread line. This is a judgment call. |
| Sweet Nothings #8 | "...that goes sideways while the other three are trying to move forward." | Consider cutting after "goes sideways" | Reader already sees it. "While the other three try to move forward" over-explains. Counter-argument: the explicit contrast may help less analytical readers. |

**Guiding principle for all trims:** Read the card. Find the moment where you laugh. Everything after that moment is suspect. If the next sentence makes you laugh AGAIN (a new joke, not the same joke restated), keep it. If it makes you nod (understanding, not laughing), cut it.

**Production reason this matters:** Card backs are constrained to ~30 words max at 3.5" x 2" card size to maintain 8pt+ readable type. Trimming long backs is both a comedy improvement and a production requirement. Any back currently exceeding 30 words needs to be edited down.

### ISSUE 2: Cell Talk Repetition Problem

**The problem:** Cards 5, 8, 9, and 14 all run the same joke — "Brad reframes taking from others as self-improvement." Four out of fifteen cards on the same engine is too many. By card 3 or 4 running this bit, the reader has learned the trick.

**What to keep:** Cards 5 and 8 are the best versions of this joke.
- Card 5 ("Abundance sits on someone else's desk unattended") — best in class
- Card 8 ("Gratitude is just taking credit in a softer font. Especially the parts other people started.") — sharp, concise

**What to rewrite:** Cards 9 and 14 need different Brad engines. Brad has documented dimensions beyond "takes credit" that are underrepresented in Cell Talk:

- **Credential obsession** — self-generated awards, keynote speaker at conferences he organizes, trademark everything
- **Metric fixation** — Brad quantifies unquantifiable things, treats feelings as KPIs
- **Relationship wreckage trail** — business partners who "resigned," advisors who quit, a staff that keeps shrinking
- **Brand-building compulsion** — everything is a product, every moment is content, every failure is a pivot
- **Self-mythology** — Brad narrates his own legend, treats mundane events as origin stories
- **Premium-stacking** — Brad makes everything "premium," "curated," "executive-grade" regardless of reality

**Card 9 rewrite brief:**
- Affirmation front: "I honor my own journey." (keep)
- Current back runs "someone else's step" (same as Card 5's desk theft)
- Rewrite using a DIFFERENT Brad dimension — e.g., his self-mythology, his metric fixation, or his brand-building compulsion
- Must be under 30 words on the back

**Card 14 rewrite brief:**
- Affirmation front: "My potential is limitless." (keep)
- Current back runs "someone already did the hard part" (same take-credit engine)
- Rewrite using a DIFFERENT Brad dimension
- The current closer — "Because someone already did the hard part" — is the most explicit version of the take-credit joke across all cards. It should be replaced entirely, not trimmed.
- Must be under 30 words on the back

### ISSUE 3: Cards That Need Full Replacement

**Cell Talk #10 — "I welcome change."**
- Current back: "Change is just improvement that hasn't been branded yet. Once you add a trademark symbol, it's no longer change. It's a product. I love products."
- **Unanimously flagged as the weakest card in all three decks.** "I love products" is a flat closer. The card meanders without landing.
- **Replace entirely.** Keep the affirmation front. Write a new Brad back that uses the "change" concept but with a sharper comedic engine. Consider: Brad welcomes change because he IS the change — he's the disruption nobody asked for. Or: Brad redefines change as something he controls, so it's not really change.
- Must be under 30 words.

**Cell Talk #4 — "I am exactly where I need to be."**
- Current back references Brad finding the survival kit on an intern's desk — breaks the fourth wall by referencing the product's origin story within the product.
- **2 of 5 panelists flagged this as a format mismatch.** Works only if the recipient has already read Brad's origin story on the outer box. Falls flat if this is the first Brad content they encounter.
- **Decision needed:** Keep (it does build Brad's narrative) or replace (it's format-dependent). If keeping, consider moving it to the last card in the deck so it reads as a callback, not an introduction.

**Rap Sheets #13 — "Working from home in a location that is clearly not home."**
- Current back: Brad tells an anecdote about taking a board call from a pool. "Water is persuasive."
- **Flagged by 3 panelists.** Breaks the deck's prosecutorial pattern — Brad is bragging, not roasting. The closer ("Water is persuasive") is trying to be absurd but reads as random.
- **Replace entirely.** The charge (front) is fine. Write a new Brad back that PROSECUTES the offense rather than endorsing it. Brad should be indicting the WFH-at-beach person, not admitting he does the same thing.
- Must be under 30 words.

**Sweet Nothings #6 — "You bring so much energy to the team."**
- Current back: "You have the vibe of a guy who claps when the plane lands and then tries to start a conversation about it."
- **Flagged by 3 panelists as the only card that feels "borrowed."** Plane clapping is a well-worn internet joke. Every other card in the product feels original. This one feels sourced.
- **Replace entirely.** Keep the front. Write a new insult back that uses the same hyper-specific everyday image structure but with a FRESH comparison nobody has heard before. Match the specificity level of the check engine light (Card 2), the Roomba (Card 7), or the IKEA furniture (Card 9).
- Must be under 30 words.

### ISSUE 4: Cross-Deck Redundancy

**The problem:** The "Brad takes credit / claims other people's work" joke appears across both Brad decks:
- Cell Talk #5 (abundance/desk theft) — KEEP, best version
- Cell Talk #8 (gratitude/softer font) — KEEP, different enough angle
- Cell Talk #9 (journey/someone else's step) — REWRITE (see Issue 2)
- Cell Talk #14 (potential/someone already did hard part) — REWRITE (see Issue 2)
- Rap Sheets #7 (taking credit for group project) — EVALUATE
- Rap Sheets #10 (self-promotion in email signature) — EVALUATE

**Rap Sheets #7 additional problem:** In 14 of 15 Rap Sheets cards, Brad roasts or escalates the offense. In Card #7, Brad DEFENDS the offense ("That's not theft. That's initiative."). This makes it read like a Cell Talk card in the wrong deck. The front (the charge) is strong. The back needs rewriting so Brad is commenting on/prosecuting the behavior, not endorsing it.

**Rap Sheets #10:** "That's not fraud. That's a soft launch." — The "soft launch" closer is genuinely good. But the self-appointed-title gag also appears in Brad's founder bio and Cell Talk #13. Evaluate whether this card adds enough unique value or whether the "soft launch" line could be repurposed elsewhere.

**Target:** Maximum 2 cards across all 45 running the "takes credit" engine — one per Brad deck.

### ISSUE 5: Sweet Nothings Pattern Monotony

**The problem:** Almost every Sweet Nothings card runs on the same mechanism — "[compliment] / You are like [household object/animal/appliance] that [extended description of dysfunction]." By card 8-9, the reader has fully absorbed the pattern. The similes are individually strong but collectively monotonous.

**The fix:** Rewrite 3-4 cards to use a DIFFERENT mechanism. Options:
- **Single devastating sentence** instead of an extended metaphor. Example structure: "Your perspective really matters." / "Unfortunately."
- **List format.** Example: three short descriptors instead of one long simile.
- **Redefinition.** Example: "Valued: see also: replaceable."
- **Subverted compliment** where the back is SHORTER than the front. The brevity itself becomes the joke.

**Which cards to convert:** The weakest simile cards are the best candidates for format-breaking:
- Card #5 (car alarm) — generic annoyance, less specific than A-tiers
- Card #3 (toddler) — less inventive comparison
- Card #10 (expired coupon) — decent but less vivid than top cards
- Card #13 (vending machine) — straightforward metaphor without the extra dimension

Pick 3-4 of these and rewrite them using a non-simile format. This will re-engage the reader at the fatigue point (cards 8-12) and make the remaining simile cards feel fresher by contrast.

### ISSUE 6: Deck Ordering

**Rap Sheets Card #1 ("Replying 'sounds good'")** is one of the weaker entries and it's the first card in the deck. First impressions set expectations.

**Recommendation:** Reorder so a stronger card leads. Best candidates for the #1 slot:
- Card #3 (microwave seconds) — universally relatable, immediately funny
- Card #6 (headphones/breaking and entering) — clean metaphor, fast payoff
- Card #12 (guitar at office party) — strongest card, but may be better as a mid-deck discovery

Also consider: **reorder all three decks** so the A-tier cards are distributed evenly rather than clustered. You want a strong opener, strong middle, and strong closer in each deck. The current ordering appears to be the order they were written, not the order they should be experienced.

**Rap Sheets backup card swap:** The backup card ("That's a great question" / "One was someone asking where the bathroom is. You called it great. It was directions.") was rated stronger than Cards #7, #10, and #13 by multiple panelists. Swap it in for whichever of those three is cut or rewritten.

### ISSUE 7: Production Constraints to Write Against

These are physical constraints that should govern the editorial pass:

1. **30-word maximum on card backs.** At 3.5" x 2" card size, anything over 30 words drops below 8pt type. This is not optional. Every card back must be 30 words or fewer after editing. (Count the words on every revised card.)

2. **Cards currently over 30 words that need trimming even if the comedy is fine:**
   - Cell Talk #5 (currently ~45 words) — trim per Issue 1
   - Rap Sheets #5 "per my last email" (~45 words) — needs trimming for fit even though the comedy is strong. Tighten without losing "That's not aggression. That's infrastructure."
   - Sweet Nothings #2 "check engine light" (~40 words) — tighten. The three-clause structure is the comedy; find ways to compress each clause.
   - Any other back exceeding 30 words after the rewrite pass

3. **Flip cue.** The design will include a small visual indicator on the front of each card telling the recipient to flip it over. You don't need to write this — just be aware that the front/back reveal mechanic is the core UX and every card must reward the flip.

## Output Format

For each issue, provide:
1. **The revised card text** (front and back, clearly labeled)
2. **Word count on the back** (must be 30 or fewer)
3. **Brief rationale** for the change (1-2 sentences)

For trims, show what was cut and why.
For rewrites, show the new version and explain which Brad dimension or comedy mechanism it uses.
For Sweet Nothings pattern-breakers, explain how the new format differs from the standard extended-simile.

After all individual card edits, provide:
- **Recommended deck order** for all three decks (card numbers in suggested sequence)
- **Final word count audit** — confirm every back is 30 words or fewer
- **A summary of all changes made** in a simple table

## Quality Bar

Every card must pass these tests:
1. **The flip test:** Does the back reward flipping the card over? Is there a comedic payoff?
2. **The read-aloud test:** Would someone read this to a coworker? Would the coworker want to see the card?
3. **The re-read test:** Is this funnier the second time, or is it spent after one read?
4. **The photo test:** Would someone photograph this card and send it to a group chat?
5. **The HR test:** Could this be displayed in an office without triggering a complaint?
6. **The 30-word test:** Is the back 30 words or fewer?
7. **The trust-the-reader test:** Does the card leave space for the reader to complete the thought, or does it narrate the punchline?
