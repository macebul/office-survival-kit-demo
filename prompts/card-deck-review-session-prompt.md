## The Products

### Rap Sheets — "Interpersonal Feedback Delivery System"
- **Division:** Interpersonal Accountability Division
- **Format:** Front = CHARGE (a workplace offense). Back = BRAD'S TAKE (Brad's judgment, which accidentally confesses to his own workplace horrors).
- **Voice:** Brad Mode 1 (institutional, dry, competent) — but the comedy is that Brad keeps accidentally revealing he does the exact same things he's prosecuting. He's the judge who's guilty of every crime.
- **The dual joke:** (1) The charges are petty workplace offenses treated with the severity of felonies. (2) Brad's responses accidentally confess that he's the worst offender in the building.
- **15 cards currently.** May stay at 15 or be trimmed.

### Sweet Nothings — "Ambient Communication Cards"
- **Division:** Ambient Relations Bureau
- **Format:** Front = something genuinely nice. Back = a weirdly specific, absurd insult built around an everyday object comparison.
- **Voice:** These are NOT Brad's voice. These are the cards themselves — ambient, passive-aggressive, meant to be left on desks or handed to coworkers. The front is warm. The back is a rare insult so bizarre that the craft of the insult is funnier than the meanness.
- **The key:** The insults must use hyper-specific everyday comparisons (shopping cart wheels, Roombas, expired coupons, bananas). The comparisons are so weirdly precise that you can't take them personally — you're too busy admiring the construction.
- **15 cards currently.** May stay at 15 or be trimmed.

## Your Review Tasks

### Task 1: Individual Card Audit

For each card in both decks, evaluate:

1. **Does the front do its job?** (Rap Sheets: is the charge specific and universally relatable? Sweet Nothings: is the nice thing genuinely nice, not already sarcastic?)
2. **Does the back land?** (Rap Sheets: does Brad accidentally confess? Sweet Nothings: is the insult built around a specific, visual, absurd everyday comparison?)
3. **Humor elements present?** (Minimum 2 of: Clever, Bizarre, Cruel, Naughty, Recognizable, Cute — "Recognizable" should almost always be one)
4. **Length check:** Will this fit on a 4" x 2.5" card with readable type? Flag anything that's running long.
5. **Kill list check:** No puns, no exclamation points, no winking, no actual workplace advice, no generic insults.
6. **Rating:** KEEP / NEEDS WORK / REPLACE / CUT

### Task 2: Deck-Level Analysis

For each deck:

1. **Range of charges/compliments:** Are we covering enough different office scenarios? Any obvious gaps? Any redundancy?
2. **Escalation across the deck:** Does the deck have variety in intensity? Some should be mild, some medium, some scorched-earth.
3. **Brad's confession pattern (Rap Sheets only):** Does Brad confess to enough different things, or does it feel repetitive? The confessions should reveal different facets of Brad — his work habits, his ego, his relationship to rules.
4. **Comparison variety (Sweet Nothings only):** Are we using enough different object categories? Check for: animals, household items, technology, food, vehicles, nature. Avoid clustering too many in one category.
5. **Card count:** Is 15 the right number? Could any be cut without loss? Are there gaps that need filling?

### Task 3: Consistency Check

1. **Voice consistency (Rap Sheets):** Every Brad response should sound like the same person wrote it. Flag any that break character.
2. **Tone consistency (Sweet Nothings):** The front should always feel genuinely warm (not pre-sarcastic). The back should always feel like art, not meanness.
3. **Cross-deck overlap:** Do any charges overlap with Sweet Nothings insults in a way that would feel repetitive if someone opens both decks?

### Task 4: Rewrites & Recommendations

For any card rated NEEDS WORK or REPLACE:
- Provide 2-3 alternative versions
- Explain what's not landing and why
- Keep the replacement at the same quality bar as the strongest cards in the deck

For any gaps identified:
- Propose new card concepts with front and back copy
- These are suggestions, not locks — present them for review

## Comedy Quality Bar — Reference Cards

### Rap Sheets — What "great" looks like:
**Card 5** — CHARGE: Using "per my last email" in a professional setting.
BRAD: "I put 'per my last email' in my signature line. Preloaded. Every email I send arrives pre-escalated. Nobody can use it against you if you use it against everyone first. That's not aggression. That's infrastructure."
*Why it works:* Brad confesses to doing something WORSE than the charge. "Pre-escalated" is a perfectly Brad word. "That's not aggression. That's infrastructure." is a line that could only come from Brad.

### Sweet Nothings — What "great" looks like:
**Card 8** — FRONT: I really look up to you. BACK: You have the leadership qualities of the front shopping cart wheel that goes sideways while the other three are trying to move forward.
*Why it works:* The comparison is hyper-specific. Everyone has experienced this exact shopping cart wheel. The image of one wheel going sideways while three try to go forward is a perfect metaphor for bad leadership — but it's too absurd to be offensive.

## Important Context

- The Office Survival Kit is a gag gift briefcase sold on Amazon. Target buyer: 25-45, buying for a coworker they actually like. Price point: ~$45.
- The kit contains 6 items (we removed Cell Talk affirmation cards). The two card decks reviewed here are the "social" items — they're meant to be given to or left for coworkers.
- Rap Sheets are roasts delivered through institutional enforcement language. Sweet Nothings are passive-aggressive compliments. Both should make someone laugh and then show it to someone else.
- "Would an office worker read this, laugh, and show it to a coworker?" is the ultimate test for every card.
