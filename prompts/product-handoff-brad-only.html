Product Copy Brad is actually competent. He can write a sardonic Nutrition Facts label, a deadpan IT Asset Tag, a perfectly absurd Care & Storage warning. The product humor works. This is why Elevate Co. exists.

Personal Brand Brad is a disaster. The moment the subject is himself — his story, vision, credentials, photo — every ounce of self-awareness evaporates. Hustle-culture LinkedIn post given physical form.

THE KEY: Don't sabotage the product copy to make Brad look dumber. The BETTER his product copy is, the funnier his personal brand becomes by contrast.

THE COMEDY RULE (LOCKED)

"Format stays buttoned-up. Content gets unleashed."

Typography, layout, label designs, FDA formatting, corporate letterhead, award seals — all polished and straight-faced. That's the straight man. WORDS/PHRASES TO AVOID

  • Anything that sounds like actual workplace advice
  • Puns (too cute — Brad's product copy is dry, not clever)
  • Exclamation points (keep it dry)
  • Winking at the reader (the straight face IS the technique)
  • Breaking the world rules — nobody acknowledges the office/prison comparison as a comparison
  • Making Brad aware of the joke (he never is)
  • Having Brad be mean or cynical (he's earnest, not sarcastic about himself)
  • Going too far into parody (Brad should feel like a real person you've met, not a cartoon)

INTERACTIVITY RECOMMENDATIONS

  • Checkboxes next to each activity (done / not done) — "quest log" feel, zero production cost
  • Fill-in fields on the last page (name, date of hire, "days without incident") — once someone writes in a humor product, they own it emotionally
  • Bingo card (single page, 3x3 or 4x4) — most shareable, photographable page. Organic marketing potential. Zero additional cost.
  • Avoid scratch-offs, pull tabs, or anything requiring manual assembly steps or specialty printing
