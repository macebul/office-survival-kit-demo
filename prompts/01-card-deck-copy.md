# Creative Session: Card Deck Copy

## Context

You are writing copy for three decks of humorous cards that ship inside the **Office Survival Kit** — a premium gag gift presented as a matte black paperboard briefcase. The world rules: **the office IS a prison** (not a metaphor — just reality). Two characters inhabit this product:

- **Brad Sterling (B.S.)** — Founder & Chief Vision Officer, Elevate Co. He discovered an underground office survival kit, mass-produced it, and genuinely believes he improved it. His product copy is straight-faced, competent office humor in buttoned-up formats. He is never self-aware.
- **The OG (Owen Granger)** — The original creator. Dry, deadpan, institutional. His voice shows up in handwritten sticky notes hidden throughout the kit.

The comedy rule: **"Format stays buttoned-up. Content gets unleashed."**

All three card decks are **business card format (~3.5" × 2")**, shipped in individual tuck-end retail boxes in **Tray 2** of the briefcase. Each deck has **10–20 cards** (final count TBD based on what works creatively and physically).

---

## Deck 1: Cell Talk

**Brad's product name:** Self-Actualization Protocol™ Cards
**Purpose:** Positive messages for coworkers — twisted through the office/prison lens
**Tone:** Encouraging on the surface, subtly unhinged underneath
**Usage:** Give to coworkers, leave on desks, tape to monitors, read aloud in meetings

### What I need:
- **15–20 affirmation card texts** (front side only — single statement per card)
- Each should read like a genuine affirmation at first glance, then land the office/prison twist
- Mix of: motivational ("You survived another Monday"), territorial ("Your desk is your kingdom"), existential ("The meeting will end. Probably."), and absurdist
- They should be funny enough to read aloud to coworkers but not mean-spirited
- Keep each card to 1–3 sentences max
- Avoid anything that would make HR actually intervene

### Examples of the vibe (not final — just direction):
- "You are exactly where you're supposed to be. Unfortunately."
- "Today's challenges are tomorrow's stories you'll tell at a different job."
- "You have survived 100% of your worst meetings. That's a perfect record."

---

## Deck 2: Rap Sheets

**Brad's product name:** Interpersonal Feedback Delivery System™
**Purpose:** Dual-sided cards — Valentine-style front makes the recipient smile, flip reveals the complaint
**Tone:** Sweet/wholesome front, dry/specific complaint on back
**Usage:** Hand to coworkers, leave on desks, slip into folders

### What I need:
- **15–20 card pairs** (front text + back text for each card)
- **Front:** Genuinely warm, Valentine-card energy (hearts, sincerity, kindness)
- **Back:** Specific, relatable office complaint that the front was actually about
- The contrast between front and back IS the joke — bigger gap = funnier
- Complaints should be universal office experiences, not personal attacks
- Keep fronts to 1 sentence, backs to 1–2 sentences

### Examples of the vibe:
- Front: "You make every room brighter." / Back: "Please stop replying-all."
- Front: "I think about you every day." / Back: "Because you took my stapler and haven't returned it."
- Front: "You always know just what to say." / Back: "Specifically, 'Let's take this offline,' which has never once led to anything being taken offline."

---

## Deck 3: Sweet Nothings

**Brad's product name:** Ambient Communication Cards™
**Purpose:** Cards to deploy silently — leave on desks, slip under keyboards, place on communal surfaces
**Tone:** Polite surface, devastating subtext. Corporate formatting, devastating content.
**Usage:** Anonymous deployment. No fingerprints. No evidence. Just results.

### What I need:
- **15–20 passive-aggressive card texts** (single-sided)
- Each should read like it could be a legitimate office communication
- The passive-aggression should be the kind everyone recognizes but nobody says out loud
- Mix of: food-related ("Per my previous note about the shared fridge..."), meeting-related, email-related, noise-related, space-related
- Some could be formatted as memos, notices, or gentle reminders
- Keep each to 1–3 sentences

### Examples of the vibe:
- "As per my previous email. And the one before that. And the one before that."
- "NOTICE: The microwave is for warming food, not for whatever that was."
- "Gentle reminder that 'quick question' and 'can I pick your brain' are not the same as a 30-minute meeting."

---

## Deliverables

For each deck, please provide:
1. The full set of 15–20 card texts
2. A brief note on which 3–5 you think are the strongest (for if I need to cut the deck down)
3. Any cards that work across decks or could be swapped between them

## Constraints
- No profanity (product needs to be giftable in a workplace setting)
- No targeting of protected characteristics
- Universal office humor — should land whether you work at a startup, a bank, or a government agency
- The humor should make people want to share them, not hide them
