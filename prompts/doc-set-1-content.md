## What Doc Set 1 IS

Doc Set 1 is Brad Sterling's corporate onboarding materials for the kit. It's Brad in **Mode 2 (Personal Brand Brad)** — polished, earnest, completely sincere. He genuinely believes these materials elevate the unboxing experience. He thinks this is what premium brands do.

### Narrative Role
- **First printed piece the recipient reads inside the briefcase** (they've already read Brad's Letter from the Founder on the outer box inside lid — OB-20)
- Sets the tone: "This is a Brad Sterling product. He takes this VERY seriously."
- Should make the recipient laugh before they even touch the items
- Creates anticipation for what's underneath
- Establishes that Brad has over-produced every element of this kit

### What It Should Feel Like
Think: the unnecessary welcome materials in a subscription box that nobody asked for but someone clearly spent way too much time on. The warranty card for a $5 item. The "Getting Started" guide that's longer than the product requires. The founder's note that's way too personal. Brad has created an entire corporate onboarding packet for a gag gift, and he sees nothing wrong with that.

---

## What Doc Set 1 Is NOT

- **Not a repeat of OB-20 (Letter from the Founder).** That letter is Brad's origin story and welcome. Doc Set 1 is the NEXT beat — operational, instructional, corporate. Different format, different function.
- **Not a product manual.** The items don't need instructions that live here. Item boxes have their own copy.
- **Not Doc Set 2.** Doc Set 2 is a 16-page saddle-stitched booklet that lives deeper in the kit. Doc Sets 1 and 2 must look and feel different from each other — different format, different texture, different function.
- **Not the OG Manual.** That's the quality benchmark for CRAFT, not for voice. Doc Set 1 is 100% Brad's voice — earnest, corporate, zero self-awareness.

---

## The Comedy Engine

Brad's Personal Brand copy is funny because:
1. **He is completely sincere.** He never winks. He thinks this is what visionary founders do.
2. **The gap between effort and product.** He has created corporate onboarding materials for a novelty gift. The mismatch is the joke.
3. **His credentials escalate.** They start plausible and slide into obviously self-generated.
4. **Trademark symbols on everything.** Brad's nervous tic.
5. **He refers to himself in the third person** in marketing copy.
6. **Premium stacking.** "Premium" appears 3+ times. He thinks repetition = truth.

The reader should think: "Someone actually made this. Someone thought this was necessary. Someone is PROUD of this."

## Comedy Rules

1. **Brad is never in on the joke.** Every line is written by a man who thinks he's creating a premium consumer experience.
2. **Format is the straight man. Content is the comedian.** If it looks like a real warranty card, the content inside should escalate into absurdity while the format stays perfectly straight.
3. **Specificity creates absurdity.** Don't say Brad is excessive — show it through specific details (exact award names, exact trademark counts, exact statistics).
4. **The credentials always escalate.** Start plausible, end obviously self-generated.
5. **No winking.** The moment Brad acknowledges the joke, the comedy dies.
6. **Every line should reward a close reader.** The person who skims should smile. The person who reads closely should laugh harder.
7. **Use Brad's rebranded names**, not the real item names.
8. **World-rules vocabulary** (office = prison) should be deployed naturally — it's the water Brad swims in as a marketer, not a forced joke.

---

## Quality Bar

The outer box copy (especially OB-8, OB-9, OB-13, OB-20) is the voice standard. The OG Manual is the craft standard. Every line in Doc Set 1 should match the density and compression of the best lines on the outer box.

**Test for every element:** Would someone photograph this and send it to a group chat? If not, it's not done yet.
