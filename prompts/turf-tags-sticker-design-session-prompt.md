# Turf Tags — Sticker Design Session

**Purpose:** Decide the final sticker designs for the Turf Tags sticker sheet. This session determines what's actually ON the stickers — the individual designs, copy, sizes, and count. These decisions feed directly into the box copy (QTY line, tray card content, image prompts).

## Context

Turf Tags is a vinyl sticker sheet inside the Office Survival Kit. The stickers are "Workspace Personalization Labels™" — territory-marking stickers for office equipment. The concept: you're claiming your desk, your mug, your fridge shelf, your stapler. The office IS a prison, and these are property markers.

The box is nearly finalized. But the actual sticker designs have never been locked. Right now, the existing documentation only has loose concepts:

**Existing design concepts (from feature/benefit analysis):**
- Dog silhouette
- Flag
- "MINE"
- Lock icon
- Crown
- Example copy: "Marked," "The throne is occupied," "Cross at your own risk"

**Existing references on the box packaging:**
- Box front says "CONTAINS: 12 ADHESIVE PROPERTY MARKERS" (count not confirmed)
- Tuck flap says "START WITH THE FRIDGE." (implies at least one sticker is fridge-appropriate)
- IKEA icons show: PEEL → STICK → HOLD COURT → ACCEPT PARADE → EXIT
- Short Side A: "SHARING IS ~~CARING~~ OVER."

**Physical specs:**
- Sheet size: ~6.5" x 4.5"
- Material: Vinyl preferred
- Format: Die-cut individual shapes (not square-cut from grid)
- Size mix: small (~0.75") and medium (~1.5"-2")

## What You Need to Decide

### 1. How many stickers?

The box currently says 12. The packaging spec says 6-10. You need to pick a number. Consider:
- Too few (6) feels light for a product called "12 Adhesive Property Markers"
- Too many (15+) means each sticker is too small on a 6.5" x 4.5" sheet
- 8-12 is the sweet spot for variety + legibility

### 2. What does each sticker say and/or show?

Each sticker needs:
- **A visual element** (icon, illustration, or typography-only)
- **Copy** (the text on the sticker — could be one word, a phrase, or a short line)
- **Size category** (small ~0.75" or medium ~1.5"-2")
- **Shape** (die-cut to what shape?)
- **Where it gets deployed** (desk, monitor, mug, fridge, chair, stapler, etc.)

### 3. Comedy standards for sticker copy

Every sticker is a micro-joke. Apply these rules:
- At least 2 of the 6 humor elements (Clever, Bizarre, Cruel, Naughty, Recognizable, Cute)
- "Recognizable" should be present on most stickers — office workers need to see their reality
- The format is the straight man: the sticker LOOKS like an official property marker or compliance label. The CONTENT is where the comedy lives.
- No puns. No exclamation points. Institutional voice.
- Specificity over generality. "MINE" is generic. "CONTENTS UNDER SURVEILLANCE" is specific.

### 4. Variety principles

The sticker sheet needs a MIX of:
- **Tone:** Some territorial ("this is mine"), some threatening ("don't touch"), some passive-aggressive ("noted"), some absurd
- **Target surfaces:** Some for desks/monitors, some for mugs/bottles, some for the fridge, some for chairs, some universal
- **Visual approach:** Some icon-based, some text-only, some combined
- **Comedy temperature:** Some mild (usable on Day 1), some escalated (deployed after you've committed to the bit)

## Sticker Design Categories to Consider

These are starting categories, not prescriptive. Mix, combine, or replace as needed.

### Category A: Property Claims
Stickers that declare ownership. The most direct territory markers.
- Think: deed language, property law, asset tags, "CLAIMED," serial numbers

### Category B: Warnings / Threats
Stickers that warn others to stay away. Deterrence-based.
- Think: hazard symbols, "DO NOT TOUCH," restricted access, biohazard

### Category C: Passive-Aggressive
Stickers that communicate displeasure about past boundary violations. The office cold war.
- Think: "This was labeled," "See previous email," "Per my last sticker"

### Category D: Surveillance / Monitoring
Stickers that imply the owner is watching. Paranoia-based.
- Think: camera icons, "UNDER SURVEILLANCE," monitoring notices

### Category E: Brad Mode 2 (Personal Brand)
Stickers that are clearly Brad's additions — self-promotional, trademarked, delusional.
- Think: "PREMIUM WORKSPACE™," Brad's signature, fake certification seals

### Category F: Absurd / Escalation
Stickers that go too far. The ones you deploy when you've fully committed.
- Think: crime scene tape motifs, evidence markers, territorial dispute documentation

## Process

1. Start by brainstorming sticker concepts freely — quantity over quality first
2. Group them by category
3. Select the strongest from each category to build a balanced sheet
4. Pressure-test each finalist against the comedy checklist
5. Decide the final count
6. Lock the designs
