# 3. Office Survival Kit - Feature/Benefit Analysis

**Product:** A novelty gag gift kit containing 8 individually packaged items in a mailer box, using sardonic office-is-prison humor, sold on Amazon under the Witty Yeti brand for the coworker/friend gift market.

**This Artifact:** Every meaningful feature mapped to what the buyer actually gets from it, ruthlessly tiered by importance to the purchase decision.

## Features That May Hurt More Than Help

* **Blister packaging that must be destroyed to access items**
   * The comedy is printed on the card backing, but ripping open the blister damages or discards the surface the jokes live on
   * This creates a tension between the unboxing experience (satisfying to open) and the re-reading experience (comedy surfaces get thrown away)
   * This is a real product design problem that needs solving before production

* **Character/lore complexity if Brad Sterling and Elevate Co. remain**
   * A gift buyer scanning an Amazon listing doesn't want to learn about a fictional CEO to understand why the product is funny
   * If the packaging requires understanding who Brad is to get the jokes, it adds friction that the comedy needs to overcome
   * The voice that works (warm, conspiratorial, institutional formats) doesn't actually require Brad to exist

* **Potential comedy density overload**
   * 50+ comedy surfaces is a feature for the engaged reader but could overwhelm at first glance
   * If the initial impression is "there's too much to read," some buyers may feel exhaustion rather than excitement
   * The listing needs to convey "lots to discover" without making it feel like homework
